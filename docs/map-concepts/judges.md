# The three judges

## I scored as the owner, using his four answers as the only standard. First, does it look like Plague Inc., with a world map that visibly reacts, a long strategic game and an unmistakable win or loss? Then the four complaints: too many rules on screen, choices that did not matter, an unfinished look, and the wrong market. I set a games critic's taste aside.

The owner's pick of 'Plague Inc style' points to the fantasy of being the thing that covers the world against one counter-force. His rejection of the comedy hotel ('crap and nothing like what I want') is strong evidence against jokey, small-stakes premises, even on a map. So the cheerful concepts lose on 'matches owner': the café chain, the silly song and the toaster. Concepts piling on extra systems lose on clarity: candles, boredom, reaction ladders, a second counter-force, three loss conditions.

NEVERSPRING wins. It keeps Plague's 'you are the threat' fantasy with world-scale, slightly dark stakes and no war or disease. Its round polar map explains the goal by itself: white grows from the cold centre to the warm rim. Its key choice is the most visible of all six: freeze a strait, and the Furnace's fuel ships turn back and its flame shrinks.

REWILD is second. It has the cleanest race display, vine against road meeting at one seed, and the most beautiful single picture. But a globe hides half the world, and its counter-force is a bar rather than something you fight on the map.

OFF SWITCH is third, on a tie-break with Wake Up, Hearth. It is the only one that keeps BRINK's original crisis tension, and its black firebreaks are a strong spatial choice. But it puts the player on defence, as in Plague Inc.'s Cure mode and Rebel Inc.'s stabiliser role, which sold far less than Plague. It also carries too many sub-rules for someone whose first complaint was 'couldn't tell what to do'.

No concept scores above 8 anywhere. All six are still descriptions, and all of them depend on commissioned art that does not exist yet.

- **OFF SWITCH**: For: it is the only concept that keeps BRINK's original crisis tension (a world-scale threat, a hand on the plug), and choosing where to drop black firebreaks is a spatial decision you see within a second. Against: you defend rather than spread, so your colour mostly retreats. That is Plague's Cure mode and Rebel Inc.'s stabiliser role, which sold about a tenth of Plague. Candles, gifts, five eye stages, labs, base relocation, notches and three map actions are the same pile of rules the owner complained about in v0.2.
- **CATCHY**: For: the friendliest premise of the six, and the Hype slider is a live quiet-or-loud lever anyone can feel. Against: a silly song is far from a crisis strategy game and close to the comedy voice the owner called crap. It has three ways to lose, a second counter-force (boredom) and a gold runner that can slide backwards, which breaks the 'one race' clarity. It depends on commissioned music most phone players will mute, and fits 40 regions onto a 316px disc.
- **REWILD**: For: the clearest race display in the set (green vine against grey road meeting at a seed in the middle, with one number on screen), and a planet painted twice, with amber cities against a teal wild on the night side, is the most beautiful single picture of the six. Against: a globe hides half the world, so the owner cannot look once and see where he stands. The Clearing is mostly a bar with chevrons rather than something he can physically cut or fight on the map, and the gentle, melancholy tone may feel too soft after a nuclear-crisis start.
- **WAKE UP, HEARTH**: For: a planet with two rings closing round it reads instantly, and waking a region to switch off its Reset tower makes 'where next' a real map decision. Against: it is a toaster comedy ('the machines made everyone tea'), the same jokey register as the rejected hotel, and it duplicates Off Switch's amber-against-cyan night-lights look. Noise, towers, patch waves, a four-step reaction ladder, links reopening at 75% and a Tricks bar is a lot of hidden machinery behind four on-screen numbers.
- **EVERY CORNER**: For: the tidiest screen of the six (six elements, a '19/48' count, a van racing a bulldog to one finish) on a daylight toy map nothing like Plague's. Against: a café chain taking over the world is a business-tycoon joke, not the world-scale strategy game the owner described, and it will be called 'Plague Inc but coffee' in a keyword space that tycoon games own. Most upgrades are global rather than spatial, and queues, For Rent signs, inspectors, boycotts and red tape risk Rebel Inc.'s whack-a-mole complaint.
- **NEVERSPRING**: For: it is Plague's fantasy (you are the thing covering the world) on a map that explains itself, with white growing from the cold centre to the warm rim. It also has the best choice in the set: freeze a strait and the Furnace's fuel ships turn back, the supply line greys out and the flame shrinks a size. Against: reviewers will still say 'Plague Inc with snow'. Regions of about 45px on a portrait disc are hard to tap, and summer melt undoing your snow can confuse a first game. The whole pitch rides on one painted map that does not exist yet.

**Top three:** NEVERSPRING, REWILD, OFF SWITCH

**Grafts:**
- Into NEVERSPRING, from Rewild: replace the two stacked bars with one two-ended race bar where white snow grows in from the left and amber Furnace fire from the right, meeting at a single finish mark. This is one picture and one rule, and it removes a bar.
- Into NEVERSPRING, from Catchy: hide the Frozen % and Furnace % figures until the bar is tapped, so the only number always on screen is Frost. This answers the owner's 'too many numbers' complaint directly.
- Into NEVERSPRING, from Every Corner: show progress as a count of regions frozen (for example '31/48') rather than a percentage. Add a CI gate that fails the build if anything is added to the HUD beyond the agreed cap list, and a Playwright check that a purchase changes the map within 15 seconds at 1x.
- Into NEVERSPRING, from Off Switch: add a 10-second high-speed replay of the whole game on the end screen, with the player's key moves (storms, cut lines) marked on the timeline. It proves choices mattered and doubles as the store video and share clip.
- Into NEVERSPRING, from Off Switch: Spotters-style dotted arcs that show where the Furnace's next supply line or Hearth will appear a few seconds ahead, so the player can pre-empt it rather than react.
- Into NEVERSPRING, from Rewild: a 'What now?' tap on the finish mark that gives one line of advice and pulses the relevant spot on the map, plus a phase caption that changes with the game ('They're building a Furnace. Cut what feeds it.').
- Into NEVERSPRING, from Catchy: every loss ends with one sentence saying why and one tip computed from the final state ('The Sun Sea never froze. Try Pack Ice.'), so a loss teaches the way to win.
- Into NEVERSPRING, from Every Corner: cut to about 40 regions, or enlarge the rim regions, to get closer to Every Corner's 69px tap targets, and add a 'Try again on the same world and start' button on the end screen.
- Into NEVERSPRING and REWILD, from Wake Up, Hearth: at the owner's art sign-off, show the same main-screen layout in two treatments (serious, DEFCON-like dusk against a lighter storybook look) so the owner picks the tone with his eyes before any code is written.
- Into REWILD, from Neverspring: make the Clearing physical. The Works feed the road through visible amber supply lines along roads and rivers, which the player cuts by overgrowing a choke point and watches reroute. That turns the counter-force from a bar with chevrons into something fought on the map.
- Into REWILD, from Neverspring: drag-to-aim for Storm and Rain (drag a cloud from the button onto the globe) instead of tap-to-target, and consider the round polar disc as the default phone view so the whole world is visible at once, keeping the globe as a showpiece toggle.
- Into OFF SWITCH, from Every Corner and Rewild: cut v1 to Shield, Switch off and Take back, with one loss condition. Drop candles, cyan gifts and per-stage eye tricks until playtests ask for them, and keep the serious DEFCON presentation that is the concept's real advantage with this owner.

## Publisher lens: what wins the 7-second store page, the first session, retention over a 20-40 minute game, and money across phone, Steam and web with no ads, no accounts and no tracking. All six use almost the same money plan: free plus a £3.99 unlock on phone, $9.99 on Steam, and a free web demo with a Stripe unlock. So monetisation does not separate them. What separates them is (1) whether the premise has Plague's 'end the world' pull without its disease, (2) whether a player's decision is a visible move on the map rather than a global stat buy, (3) whether one screenshot shows the whole game, and (4) fit with an owner who started with a nuclear crisis and called a comedy version 'crap'.

NEVERSPRING wins clearly. The polar disc makes the theme read itself (cold centre, warm rim). The Furnace's supply lines can be cut, and storms are dragged onto the map, so every decision is a move on the map with a visible result. Of the six, it is the strongest answer to complaint (b). Off Switch has the most serious tone and the most direct verbs. But it is in effect Plague Inc's own The Cure mode, and it piles up systems. REWILD has the best grey-to-green screenshot story, but it is mechanically the closest to Plague and has the most expensive renderer.

The comic three (Hearth, Catchy, Every Corner) carry real risk of repeating the hotel rejection. Every concept still shares the Plague genre skeleton, so press will say 'Plague Inc with X' whichever one ships.

Rewild in particular must drop its 'Fungus' kind, a real Plague Inc pathogen type. It must also rename its Birds/Wind/Water travel growths, which mirror Plague's transmission tree. Recommendation: before any engine code, show the owner two painted main screens at 390x844: Neverspring, plus Off Switch as the serious alternative.

- **NEVERSPRING**: For: the round polar map explains the game in one picture (white creeping from the cold centre to the warm rim). Cutting the Furnace's visible supply lines by freezing a strait, and dragging storms onto the map, make every decision a geographic move you can see, and it keeps Plague's dark 'end the world' thrill without disease or war, which suits an owner who began with an apocalypse. Against: flat white on a painted map can read as blank or unloaded unless the shader is excellent, summer melt is a second rhythm newcomers may misread as losing, and 'eternal winter against a giant Furnace' sits right next to Frostpunk's Generator, so Steam players will make the comparison.
- **OFF SWITCH**: For: the serious hand-on-the-plug crisis tone is closest to the original BRINK instinct. The per-region verbs (shield, switch off, take back) change the map within seconds, and the amber-and-cyan night-lights picture would make a strong screenshot. Against: it is Ndemic's own The Cure mode with an AI skin, and defender games (Rebel Inc., The Cure) have sold a fraction of the spreader game. It also stacks candles, five eye stages, gifts, labs, notches and six rule-changing AIs into the very Rebel-style pile behind complaint (a). An AI-takeover game built with AI tools is a live PR risk too.
- **REWILD**: For: grey-to-green is the most instantly readable transformation of the six, the twice-painted planet could be the prettiest store image, and it lands squarely in the store- and ad-safe cosy/nature market. Against: mechanically it is the closest to Plague: an organism spreading by rivers, wind, birds and sea, cold and dry resistance seeds, and literally a 'Fungus' kind. The rotating globe hides half the race at any moment while being the most expensive renderer here, and the first minutes on a drab grey world undersell it.
- **Wake Up, Hearth**: For: the glowing-eyed toaster icon and 'turn it off and on again' are a cute, universal store hook, and Reset towers that switch off when you wake their region make target choice genuinely spatial. Against: it is the comic register the owner has just rejected. 'Lit amber means asleep, cyan means awake' is counterintuitive on a night map. And it duplicates Off Switch's amber-and-cyan night-lights look and machine theme with lower stakes; its Reach/Mischief/Cover board maps one-to-one onto Plague's Transmission/Symptoms/Abilities tabs.
- **CATCHY**: For: it has the most distinct premise and identity of the six (record-groove disc, live Hype slider, touring band) and the lowest store-policy risk. Against: it is a sweet, all-ages premise for an owner who began with World War III, so Steam strategy buyers won't bite. Its core pleasure is music that most phone players hear on mute, and that music needs commissioned stems for eight genres. A 316 px disc with 40 regions is too small for thumbs, and boredom plus a rival plus 'Forgotten' gives three ways to lose to explain.
- **EVERY CORNER**: For: it has the clearest race of the six (van against bulldog, '19/48'), a goal anyone grasps from their own high street, a distinct daylight toy-map look and a 4+ rating. Against: 'Plague Inc but coffee' is the press shorthand, in a keyword space owned by idle tycoons and Coffee Inc 2, and the cheerful toy map risks reading as a hypercasual State.io ad. Decisions are mostly global purchases, while three kinds of pickup invite whack-a-mole. Eight products with different rules (a delivery network, theme parks) plus character art make it the hardest v1 to finish.

**Grafts:**
- Neverspring <- Rewild: paint the disc twice (thawed and frozen) and reveal the frozen painting through the noise threshold instead of laying flat white over the land, so snow reads as painted terrain with blue shadows and never as an unloaded or empty area. This is the biggest screenshot risk.
- Neverspring <- Rewild: adopt the validator rule that every region has at least two ways in, one of them through the sky (wind), so the Warm Current and island regions can never become a Greenland trap.
- Neverspring <- Off Switch: telegraphs. A dotted amber arc or faint dome outline shows a few seconds ahead where the next supply line will open or the next Hearth will light, so a storm can pre-empt it rather than react to it.
- Neverspring <- Off Switch: on the end screen, a 10-second high-speed replay of the whole game with the player's storms and cut lines marked as ticks. It proves the choices mattered and doubles as a short share video.
- Neverspring <- Catchy: every loss ends with one sentence on why and one tip worked out from the final state, for example 'The Narrows never froze. Try Pack Ice.', so a loss teaches the next win.
- Neverspring <- Catchy: layered music that adds an instrument as each landmass freezes, so progress can be heard as well as seen, alongside the drone that tightens as the Furnace climbs.
- Neverspring <- Every Corner: show Frozen as a region count ('31/48 frozen') instead of a percentage. Add a Playwright CI gate that fails if the time from a purchase or storm to the first visible map change exceeds the target, and a validator-enforced cap list of HUD elements.
- Neverspring <- Rewild: tapping the goal line or the race strip gives a one-line 'What now?' with a pulse on the map where it applies.
- Off Switch <- Neverspring: tapping TIDY's eye lights up the routes feeding its fastest growth, thickest first. Make cut-off cyan regions stagnate (isolation helps you), so switching off is a geographic puzzle instead of a per-region candle-timer juggle. Then the candles and gifts can be cut to reduce complexity.
- Rewild <- Neverspring: replace the rotating globe with a polar disc (cold centre, warm rim) so the whole race fits one picture and the renderer drops sphere ray-casting. Make the Works feed the Clearing through visible supply lines that overgrown rivers and roads cut. ## Senior designer who has shipped a map-based spread strategy game. I scored each concept against the owner's four answers (a Plague-style world map that reacts, a long game, a clear win or loss, and his four complaints), not against critic taste. I also checked three design questions: whether a decision has a second spatial axis beyond Plague's solved 'grow quietly, then go loud' pattern, whether players and store reviewers would see a clone, and whether a solo developer can make it polished on WebGL2, Preact, Capacitor and Tauri. All six inherit Plague's dominant pattern of staying quiet until you are everywhere, then going loud. Only three add a geographic decision that changes how fast the enemy moves: Neverspring (cutting supply lines), Wake Up, Hearth (one Reset tower per region) and Off Switch (firebreaks). Those three score highest on 'choices matter'. The cheerful premises (Catchy, Every Corner and, to a degree, Hearth) are marked down on matching the owner. His first idea was a nuclear crisis, and he called the comedy hotel 'crap'. The setting is open, but nothing in his answers asks for whimsy. Feasibility is marked down for a globe renderer (Rewild), commissioned adaptive music (Catchy) and character animation plus eight very different rule sets (Every Corner).

- **NEVERSPRING**: For: the Furnace's speed comes from supply lines drawn on the map, and the player cuts them with geography. Freeze the Narrows and the ships turn back and the flame shrinks. That makes where you spread decisive and readable, and it inverts Plague's Greenland problem. The fantasy is a villain as bold as Plague's, but store-safe. Against: cutting lines can snowball into a game decided early (the concept admits this). Summer melt and the eye turning into a flame are rules a first-timer must work out alone. And if snow is only a white overlay on the painting, it may look blank in a store thumbnail.
- **Wake Up, Hearth**: For: a Reset tower stands in every region you have not woken yet, so choosing which region to wake next directly sets the Helpdesk's speed. That is a real targeting decision Plague never had, and it runs on a flat night disc that is cheap to draw. Against: a toaster coming to life is a comic premise, arriving straight after the owner called the comedy hotel crap. Its amber-to-cyan city-lights look is almost the same as Off Switch's and is generic 'Earth from orbit' imagery.
- **REWILD**: For: the planet painted twice and revealed region by region is the most literal and beautiful version of Plague's core payoff, the world changing colour. 'Turn the world green' is safe to advertise and suits families. Against: it copies Plague's structure most closely (rivers, sea, wind and birds stand in for transmission routes; the Works play the role of labs; you are noticed, then a bar starts). The globe also hides half the world, needs far-side arrows, sphere tap-picking and a morph to a flat map, which makes it the hardest renderer of the six. And winning means every region 100% green, which invites Plague's late-game wait for the last region.
- **OFF SWITCH**: For: placing firebreaks on the region graph is real spatial strategy, and the rogue-AI crisis keeps the stakes closest to the original BRINK. Against: the player defends, which puts it right on top of Plague's own Cure mode and on the side of the market where Rebel Inc. reached a tenth of Plague's Steam footprint. Candle timers, five eye stages, cracking shields, gifts, labs and a base that relocates bring back the per-region juggling that players called Rebel Inc. obtuse for. And the amber lane measures lab output, not your colour on the map, which is less intuitive.
- **EVERY CORNER**: For: it has the clearest race on screen (your van against the bulldog, '19/48'), and it is the only bright daylight map, which would stand out in a store full of dark strategy icons. Against: three kinds of things to tap, plus inspectors to sit down, is Rebel Inc.'s whack-a-mole by design. 'Plague Inc. but coffee' lands in a search space already owned by Coffee Inc 2 and idle tycoon games. And cute business satire is the furthest tone from a nuclear-crisis owner, while eight products with very different rules (Delivery App, Theme Park) make the simulation scope large.
- **CATCHY**: For: the free Hype slider is the best live trade-off lever in the set, and a planet that is also a record is an identity it could own outright. Against: it is the least likely to be the picture in the owner's head (whimsy straight after 'crap'). Much of the payoff depends on commissioned adaptive music that most phone players will mute. Silver means both bored people and the rival's progress, and with two ways to lose the rules get muddy. And 40 regions on a 316 px disc are too small to tap reliably.

**Top three:** NEVERSPRING, Wake Up, Hearth, REWILD

**Grafts:**
- Into NEVERSPRING, from REWILD: commission a second, frozen painting of the same disc (iced lakes, snow-laden pines, frozen harbours) and reveal it region by region with the noise threshold, instead of drawing a procedural white overlay. Frozen land then looks authored and rich, not blank, in store thumbnails and on the share card.
- Into NEVERSPRING, from WAKE UP, HEARTH ('links reopen at 75%'): once the Furnace passes 75%, icebreaker convoys force one cut line back open each season. This gives a final-window beat and blunts the snowball where cutting every line almost always wins.
- Into NEVERSPRING, from OFF SWITCH (dotted next-hop arc, satellite hop shown 5 s ahead): show where the next Hearth will light a few seconds ahead with a faint amber ring, so a storm can pre-empt it. That turns Hearths from a reaction into a decision you plan for.
- Into NEVERSPRING, from OFF SWITCH: on the end screen, a 10-second high-speed replay built from the (tick, action) command log, with the player's storms and line cuts marked on the timeline. It shows what mattered, which answers complaint (b), and makes a short video to share.
- Into NEVERSPRING, from CATCHY: every loss ends with one sentence of why and one tip, worked out from the final state, e.g. 'The Narrows line fed the Furnace for three years. Try Pack Ice.'
- Into NEVERSPRING, from EVERY CORNER: a loudness icon (snowflake-hush or megaphone) on every crystal, plus two CI gates: a list of allowed HUD elements, and a Playwright check that a purchase changes the map within 15 s at 1x.
- Into NEVERSPRING and WAKE UP, HEARTH, from REWILD: regions surrounded by your colour convert three times faster, so the 100%-coverage win never ends in a wait for the last region.
- Into WAKE UP, HEARTH, from NEVERSPRING (tap the flame to light its supply lines): tapping the Reset ring's tip highlights every live tower on the map, fastest first, so the player sees why the Reset is speeding up and where to wake next.
- Into WAKE UP, HEARTH, from REWILD: the tappable centre 'What now?' seed, which gives one line of advice plus a pulse on the map at the place it means.
- Into WAKE UP, HEARTH: show the owner the 'sleek and serious' treatment first and keep the toaster as one of the unlockable machines rather than the face of the game. The title, icon and first screenshot should sell the night planet and the rings, not a comic appliance, given the reaction to the hotel.
- Into REWILD, from NEVERSPRING: replace the globe with a flat polar disc (cold centre, warm rim) so the whole world is visible in one portrait frame. This removes sphere picking, far-side arrows and the morph, and keeps the twice-painted reveal.
- Into REWILD, from NEVERSPRING: the Works feed the Clearing through visible supply lines that the player overgrows or cuts with rivers and Storm, giving the race a second spatial axis beyond going quiet then loud.
- Into all three, from CATCHY: a global loudness setting whose changes ramp in over one game week, so pushing harder is a live decision made on the map and not only a purchase, without rewarding flicking the setting back and forth.
