# REWILD

> You are the last living seed on a grey, paved planet. Turn the whole world green again before its cities finish clearing you away.

Working-title note: REWILD (working title; store subtitle “Grow back the world”; one constant, BRAND.name in /home/user/brink/src/config.ts)

## Pitch

You play nature itself, starting as one seed on a fictional planet covered in concrete, roads and dull fields. You choose where it takes root, then watch green spread across a painted map, along rivers and carried by the wind and by geese, until whole cities disappear under forest with their lights still on. The cities don't want this. They send mowing crews out from their factories, and the more factories are running, the faster they clear you. Plant giant trees, grow over the factories to stop them, and surround cities until they give in. Turn every region green before the cities finish the Clearing and you win. No one is hurt: when you win, the people are still there, living in the forest.

## Goal and win

Goal line, on screen every second: “Turn the world green. First to the middle wins.”

WIN: all 42 regions are green. That means every region's city has turned: a great tree rises from its centre and its lights soften from amber to teal. The green vine on the race strip reaches the seed in the middle. The card reads “The world is green again.” with “The cities are still here. They just stopped fighting.”

LOSS (the only one): the grey road on the race strip reaches the seed first. Grey paving rolls out from every running factory in 4 seconds, and the green shrinks back to the regions you had already turned. The card reads “The Clearing is finished.” with “Somewhere, a seed waits.” It adds one line on why and one tip (“Two Works in the Old Heart ran all game. Plant a Great Tree beside one.”). The earlier early-loss rule (losing if all your green is cleared) is removed.

Rules that keep it readable:
- A region you have turned stays green for good; the Clearing only fights over places you have not turned yet.
- A region with green on every land side turns three times faster (‘the wild closes in’), so there is no waiting for the last region.
- ‘Skip to the end’ appears once the result is certain.
- Every region has at least two ways in, one of them through the sky (wind or geese), so no island can lock you out.

Length: 22 to 30 minutes at 1× on Normal, about 12 at 4×.

## What you do

EVERY 10 SECONDS: watch green creep across the painted map. Every few seconds a gold-white flower (a bloom) opens on your green land with a soft chime. Tap it and its petals fly to the Seeds counter while a green ripple pushes growth outward from that spot. A missed bloom drops half its Seeds after 6 seconds, and the Bees growth collects them in full. Glance at the race strip: the yellow chevrons on the road are the factories still running, one each.

EVERY MINUTE OR SO, one real decision, made on the map:
1. Buy one growth on the Tree. Each one makes every other growth cost a little more, so you commit to a direction.
2. Plant a Great Tree: tap Plant, then tap your green land. A huge tree rises, pushes green out in a wide circle, can never be mown and makes Seeds. But it is loud: nearby city lights flare amber and the Clearing wakes or speeds up. You get one at first and earn more.
3. Pick a target: grow over a Works (factory) to stop its crews and remove a chevron, surround a city so it turns fast, or open a new continent by river, sea, wind or geese.
4. Quiet or loud: Roots keep the Clearing asleep but grow slowly; Branches and Great Trees are fast and spectacular but wake it.
5. Answer what the map shows you: a faint dashed white line flickers a few seconds ahead of where the next wall will rise. Get there first, or send Underground Threads beneath it.

## The map

A fictional, Earth-like planet drawn as one FLAT, tall painted map, about 3:4 in portrait. A phone held upright shows the whole world at once, with no rotating and no hidden far side. Climate runs in horizontal bands a non-gamer reads at a glance: frozen north, mild rich middle, a dry belt, wet tropics, farmland, a cold southern tip, and island chains along the edges.

6 continents and 42 regions, each with exactly one named city (42 cities to turn). The average region is about 55 px across at default zoom.
- THE NORTH REACH (top): cold, ice and grey pine, few people, 1 Works.
- THE OLD HEART (centre left): rich grey sprawl, 2 Works.
- THE LONG COAST (running down the right side): crowded harbour cities, 2 Works.
- THE DRY BELT (middle band): pale sand, poor, big regions.
- THE GLASS ISLES (lower left): remote islands.
- THE SOUTHERN HOOK (bottom): farmland and a cold tip, 1 Works.
That makes 6 Works in all, each drawn as a small grey chimney with smoke.

Natural routes, drawn when you buy them: about 18 rivers, 4 sea currents, 3 wind belts and 4 goose flyways between continents. There are no airports or shipping lanes.

Each region shows three things as icons and words, never numbers: climate (cold, mild, dry or wet), ground (plains, farmland, desert, mountains, ice, wetland or island) and wealth (rich or poor). City and region names are invented and must pass the check against real places (Vell, Sefi, Brannoch, Tarrowin, Ellisk). ‘Merrow’ and ‘Orrin’ were dropped because both are real places in the UK.

The planet is PAINTED TWICE:
- the Grey World: concrete sprawl, dull ochre patchwork fields, slate seas;
- the Green World: moss, deep forest, wetland and blossom over exactly the same coastlines, with city shapes still visible under the canopy.
Your green is the second painting showing through the first. A soft day-night band drifts slowly across the flat map, like an old world-clock map. In the night part, cities glow amber and your green glows teal. An ‘always day’ switch is in settings.

Why fictional: the no-real-countries rule, Apple's rule 1.1.2, no border disputes, and full control of the geography for balance.

## The counter force

THE CLEARING: the world's cities working together to clear the wild. They are frightened people who want their roads and fields back, not villains. It is one side of the race strip: a grey road growing in from the right.
1. ASLEEP: until you are noticed, the road is a faint dotted outline with a closed eye. It wakes when green first touches a city, or sooner if you grow loudly (Great Trees, Branches). Ticker: “Strange forests reported near Vell.”
2. THE WORKS DRIVE IT: the road's speed comes from the factories still running, shown as small yellow chevrons sliding along it, one per running Works and up to six. Each running Works sends grey clearing crews out along the roads. You see them as a grey mowing fringe eating the edges of your green near cities.
3. OVERGROW A WORKS TO STOP IT: when its region turns, the chimney rusts and stops smoking, vines climb it, its crews stop, and one chevron vanishes from the road. Choosing which factory to go after next is the main decision about places.
4. MILESTONES, each announced in one ticker line and shown as a change on the map:
- a quarter of the way: Mowing. The grey fringe pushes green back near cities.
- halfway: Walls. White walls rise around worried cities. Growth over land slows to a crawl, but rivers, wind and geese still cross.
- three-quarters: Paving. Grey paving rolls out along roads and fast turns green that hasn't turned yet back to concrete.
- near the end: every city fights back and the drone peaks.
There is no fire anywhere.
5. ADVANCE WARNING: about 5 seconds ahead, a faint dashed white line flickers where the next wall will go up.
Nothing stops the Clearing outright, so the race stays a race. But in a world where every Works has rusted, it moves very slowly.

## Upgrades

THE TREE (its button is labelled Grow): one hand-drawn tree in a half-height sheet, with the live map still visible above it. About 36 growths at launch. Roots grow downward, Branches upward, and Reach outward to the sides.

Each node shows:
- an icon;
- a name of up to 2 words;
- one line of up to 8 words, with no numbers;
- a cost in Seeds;
- a loudness mark (a quiet leaf or a loud leaf).
Every growth makes every other one cost 1 Seed more, and ‘Let go’ refunds half. Selecting a node previews it on the live map: rivers outlined, flyways dotted, worried cities pulsing amber.

ROOTS, quiet (where and how toughly you grow): Pine Roots (“Grow in the cold.”), Cactus Roots (dry land), Lichen (mountains and ice), Deep Roots (mowing pushes you back less), Cracks (green breaks through paving into cities), Underground Threads (pass under walls), Hush (cities notice you less), Rust (vines jam the Works, so their crews slow).

BRANCHES, loud (speed, Seeds and Great Trees): Fast Growth, More Blooms, Bees (blooms collect themselves), Another Great Tree (one more to plant), Canopy (Great Trees reach further), Road Vines (green runs along roads into cities), Thorns (walls and mowing work less well), and the top growth SPRING (once per game, a season of growth everywhere; very loud).

REACH, to the sides (crossing gaps; renamed so nothing echoes Plague's transmission tree): Riverbanks (green follows rivers), Driftwood (sea currents carry seeds to far coasts), Dandelion Clocks (wind belts carry seeds over land and sea), Migrating Geese (flocks carry seeds between continents), Long Migration (geese reach the far isles), and SEED RAIN (a power: once per season, tap any region to drop seeds there, even behind a wall).

How the map changes, within 2 seconds of buying: rivers glow green, geese lift off in a V, Great Trees rise with a deep wooden creak, green veins run into grey street grids, and worried city lights dim to a steady glow. You never get a number back.

## First 60 seconds

Five taps. Every hint is a floating line of 6 words or fewer next to what it points at; there are no pop-up windows.
- 0 s: a grey painted planet in soft daylight, with a gentle night band drifting across one side and amber city lights in it. The REWILD wordmark fades in with “The last seed on a grey world.” One button: Plant.
- 3 s (TAP 1, Plant): land brightens as the finger passes over it. Hint: “Tap anywhere on land.”
- 6 s (TAP 2, a region): a seed falls in a little arc and lands. A green ring blooms with root-like veins, and the region's name appears (“Tarrowin”). Hint: “Your seed is growing.”
- 10 s: the first gold flower opens with a chime. Hint: “Tap the flower.”
- 12 s (TAP 3): petals fly to the Seeds counter, which appears with a bounce (0 to 3), and a green ripple spreads.
- 16 s: the Grow button breathes. Hint: “Tap Grow.”
- 18 s (TAP 4): the Tree sheet rises and the map shrinks above it. One node glows, Riverbanks (“Your green follows rivers.”), and Tarrowin's river lights up on the map as a preview.
- 22 s (TAP 5, buy): a leaf appears on the drawn tree and the sheet slides away. Within 2 seconds the river glows green; within 5, green flows into the next region. Hint: “It's spreading.”
- 30 s: the race strip slides in, with a sliver of vine on the left and a dotted grey road with a closed eye on the right. The goal line types itself.
- 35–55 s: two more blooms. The first ticker line: “Tarrowin gardeners report ‘very confident’ weeds.” The speed control pulses once: “Faster? Tap here.”
- 55–60 s: green reaches the neighbouring region and a shower of Seeds plays.
Later hints, one line each and only when relevant: at about 2 minutes the Plant button appears (“Plant a Great Tree. It's loud.”); when the Clearing wakes, “They've noticed. Grow over the Works to slow them.”

## Art direction

‘One planet, painted twice.’ A commissioned illustrator paints the same tall world twice in one soft, painterly style:
- the Grey World: concrete sprawl, muted ochre patchwork, pale sand, slate seas, white poles;
- the Green World: moss, deep forest, wetland and blossom over the same coastlines, with city shapes still visible under the canopy.
Plus three small support maps: city lights (amber), the wild's glow (teal), and a leafy noise texture.

The Green World is revealed region by region by comparing the noise texture with how green each region is. Green therefore arrives as living patches, never flat fills. A slow, curved day-night band crosses the flat map. At night, cities glow amber and the wild glows teal, which is the signature store image. Small hand-drawn sprites (blooms, Great Trees, geese, chimneys, walls) sit on top.

The interface is cream paper and dark ink, the only light UI of the three, so the whole product reads as warm daylight.

References in words:
- the painted forests of Japanese animated nature films;
- photographs of overgrown ghost towns, without the ruin;
- old world-clock maps with a moving night shadow;
- Mini Metro and Alto's Odyssey for restraint.
Not Terra Nil: no isometric tiles.

Motion: everything eases into place; only blooms bounce.

Accessibility: green and grey differ in brightness and texture (leafy against gridded), and a high-contrast palette is offered.

Sound: soft wood, wind and bell tones, the drone following the Clearing, and a commissioned ambient score.

At sign-off the artist shows the main screen in two treatments, calm naturalist and brighter storybook, and the owner picks by eye.

## Session and replay

ONE GAME: 22 to 30 minutes at 1× on Normal, about 12 minutes at 4×. Pause, 2× and 4× are free from the start. The game autosaves every 10 seconds and when you leave, and resumes mid-game on any device.

REPLAY:
- 4 difficulties: Easy, Normal, Hard, Relentless.
- 6 kinds of wild, each unlocked by winning with the one before, each with its own twist:
  - Forest: balanced.
  - Moss: nearly silent but slow; cannot cross deserts.
  - Bramble: loud and thorny, shrugs off mowing. It replaces the earlier Fungus, which is a Plague Inc. disease type.
  - Vine: fast in cities; win by turning every city.
  - Mangrove: spreads from coasts; win by greening every coast.
  - Wildflower: huge numbers of blooms, but fragile to mowing.
- 12 scenarios on the same map, including Ice Age, Long Drought, The Walled Garden, One Big City, High Seas, Last Tree and The Quiet Year.
- Every game varies the start, which Works are strongest, and one world modifier on Hard and above.

PHONE HABIT: the free Daily Continent. One continent of about 7 regions, with the same seed and two modifiers for everyone. About 8 minutes, replays allowed, local best and streak only, no names.

META: a record book stored on the device, about 40 achievements matched one to one with Steam, and ‘Same world, same start’ plus a 10-second replay on every end screen.

STEAM DEPTH: 6 kinds on 4 difficulties plus 12 scenarios is about 20 to 30 hours. After launch: free scenario drops, then a new painted world every 4 to 6 months as paid DLC (an ocean world with a Coral kind, a desert world).

## How it answers the four complaints

(a) COULDN'T TELL WHAT TO DO
- One goal sentence is always on screen.
- One race bar: vine against road, meeting at a seed.
- One big number (Seeds) and one count (27 of 42 green).
- The Clearing's speed is shown as chevrons and a city's mood as the colour of its lights, never as a meter.
- Five guided taps, with hints of 6 words or fewer.
- A region card says in words why the green is stuck (“Too cold here. Pine Roots would help.”), and tapping the seed answers ‘What now?’.
- The content checker limits growth descriptions to 8 words with no digits and ticker lines to 70 characters. A fixed list of on-screen elements fails the build if anything is added.

(b) CHOICES FELT POINTLESS
- There is no score anywhere. Every growth adds a behaviour you see within 2 seconds, and you preview it before buying.
- The big choices are about places: where to plant each Great Tree, which factory to overgrow next (a chevron visibly disappears) and which city to surround.
- An automated test fails the build if a purchase does not change the map within 15 seconds at 1×.
- The balance simulator must show that:
  - a player who does nothing always loses;
  - a random buyer wins under 20% of games on Normal;
  - quiet and loud builds can each win (30 to 60%);
  - no growth appears in more than 35% of winning builds.

(c) LOOKED UNFINISHED
- One planet painted twice and revealed region by region, with a drifting night band.
- The owner signs off a painted mockup and a 15-second clip before any game code.
- No placeholder art on any channel.
- The mid-game map is the first store screenshot, and the finished green world is the share card.

(d) WRONG FOR THE MARKET
- ‘Turn the world green’ is understood instantly by anyone.
- It sits in the rebuilding and watch-the-world-grow space the stores reward right now.
- It passes every app store and ad policy and rates about 4+ to 9+ / PEGI 3 to 7, which opens up families and the cosy, nature-loving audience.
- It is built for phones held upright, with the whole world visible.
- Prices match what already sells, and players will want to post the share card.

## Distance from plague inc

Shared, honestly: the loop (spread across a map, a points currency, a race against one counter-force) is the Plague formula. Of the three concepts this one is mechanically the closest, so reviewers may say ‘Plague Inc. with trees’.

Different on every visible surface:
- Premise: nature takes back a world and nobody dies; people live on inside the forest.
- Look: a flat, tall painted world in daylight with a drifting night band and cream paper UI, not a dark real-Earth map with red dots.
- Routes: rivers, sea currents, wind and geese, renamed (Riverbanks, Driftwood, Dandelion Clocks, Migrating Geese) so nothing echoes Plague's transmission tree. The Fungus kind is gone.
- Player verbs Plague lacks: planting Great Trees as permanent landmarks, surrounding cities, overgrowing factories.
- Counter-force: physical crews, walls and paving that come from factories you can rust, not an abstract cure bar.
- Pickups: blooms that open, are safe to miss, and push growth out where you tap.
- Upgrades: one drawn tree in a half-sheet with the live map above it, not three tabs of hexagons on a separate screen.
- Layout: a race strip filled from both ends at the top, the ticker and buttons at the bottom.
- Words: the content checker bans infect, cure, DNA, evolve, trait, symptom, lethality, pathogen and plague. No ‘Inc.’ in the name.

It also stays clear of Terra Nil: no isometric tiles, no ‘Terra’ in the name.

## Risks

- Owner taste: a hopeful, gentle premise after a nuclear-crisis start may feel too soft. Mitigation: the painted mockup in two treatments and a 15-second clip before any code, and a playable web slice at about week 6.
- Clone perception: of the three, this is mechanically the closest to Plague Inc. The defence is the look, the planting and targeting verbs and the gentler premise. Bio Inc. shows that a re-skinned formula gets called derivative.
- A softer hook: ‘heal the world’ gets talked about less than ‘end the world’, and there are no outbreak-style news spikes to ride. The tension has to come from the race and from Relentless difficulty.
- The art carries everything: two full paintings of the same world must line up exactly. Mediocre art brings complaint (c) straight back.
- Snowball: a turned region never reverts and a rusted Works stays rusted, so a strong opening could decide the game early. Paving at three-quarters and Relentless difficulty are the counterweights, and the bots must show fewer than 15% of games decided by minute 10.
- Performance: a two-painting reveal, the night band and sprites running for 30 minutes on mid-range phones (heat, battery), and Steam Deck's weaker graphics engine.
- Sensitivity: rewilding is a live topic among UK farmers, and ‘the Clearing’ may echo the Highland Clearances (fallback names: ‘the Paving’, ‘the Grey Plan’). Keep it fictional and non-political, with no fire imagery.
- Scope: about 14–20 developer-weeks plus art and music, three purchase systems, desktop and phone app builds that have never been built, and iOS needs a Mac.

## Judge scores

Owner-lens judge, 48/70 (second): ‘The clearest race display in the set and the most beautiful single picture.’ Against: the globe hid half the world, the Clearing was only a bar, and the tone may be too soft.

Publisher judge, 41/70 (third): ‘Grey-to-green is the most instantly readable transformation.’ Against: mechanically the closest to Plague (including a Fungus kind), and the most expensive renderer.

Designer judge, 42/70 (third): ‘The most literal and beautiful version of the world changing colour.’ Against: it copies Plague's structure, has the hardest renderer, and invites a long wait for the last region.

Combined 131 (second). Matches the owner's answers 20 of 30.

Changes made in response:
- the globe is replaced by a flat, tall map (not the round polar disc, so it stays visibly different from NEVERSPRING);
- the Clearing is made physical through the Works and their crews;
- Great Trees add a decision about where to place things;
- surrounded regions turn three times faster;
- the Fungus kind and the Plague-like route names are gone;
- fire is removed;
- the pop-up choice moments are cut.
