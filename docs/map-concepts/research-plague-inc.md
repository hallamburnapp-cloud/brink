# What made Plague Inc. work

Gathered by web research in October 2026. Every claim carries its source and a confidence level.

## Findings

- **Plague Inc. was made by one person, James Vaughan, in his spare time, for under $5,000. Freelancers built the engine, art and sound. It launched on iOS on 26 May 2012 at $0.99, with no marketing, and earned back its costs on the first day.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: PocketGamer.biz (12 Mar 2013): costs under $5,000; algorithms designed in Excel; a freelance team built the engine, art and sound; Vaughan just emailed friends about the launch. Shacknews (10 Jul 2012): priced at $0.99 on iTunes; costs recouped within one day; about 750,000 iOS devices 'infected' in the first three weeks. Wikipedia: iOS 26 May 2012, Android 4 Oct 2012.
  - Source: https://www.pocketgamer.biz/feature/49231/plague-vs-pandemic-how-james-vaughans-ios-hit-plague-inc-took-the-market-from-its-inspiration/ ; https://shacknews.com/article/74742/community-spotlight-plague-inc-by-ndemic-creations ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_Inc.
- **It reached #1 paid on the App Store through word of mouth alone and stayed in the top paid charts for years: #1 paid in the UK within 3 days, #1 in the US within 6 days, and held #1 for two weeks without an Apple feature. Wikipedia gives its annual US paid-iPhone rank as 15th (2012), 5th (2013), 3rd (2014), 7th (2015) and 4th (2016). Ndemic says 2020 was its 8th consecutive year among the world's top-selling games.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: PocketGamer.biz 2013 for the launch charts. Wikipedia for the annual ranks. Ndemic's 2016 report card gives 8th most popular paid app in the US for 2016, which conflicts with Wikipedia's 4th; the two may measure different lists (all apps vs games). The Ndemic 2020-21 report card covers 2020.
  - Source: https://www.pocketgamer.biz/feature/49231/plague-vs-pandemic-how-james-vaughans-ios-hit-plague-inc-took-the-market-from-its-inspiration/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_Inc. ; https://cdn.ndemiccreations.com/en/news/122-plague-inc-s-2016-report-card-85-million-infected ; https://ndemiccreations.com/en/news/185-ndemic-creations-2020-2021-report-card-building-the-cure
- **Player numbers over time (all developer-reported, all platforms, 'players' or 'infected'): 45M (end of 2014), 85M (end of 2016), 100M+ (Sept 2017), 130M+ (June 2020), 160M+ (2020-21; Wikipedia says downloads as of May 2021), 170M+ (current Ndemic press page). Ndemic's site also claims 'over 700 million games played'.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: Ndemic 2014 report card: 45 million. Ndemic 2016 report card: 85 million. iPhonesoft and PocketGamer.biz (Sept 2017): over 100 million. PocketGamer.biz interview (18 Jun 2020): over 130 million. Ndemic 2020-21 report card: over 160 million. Ndemic press page: over 170 million. A June 2026 retailer blog (Eneba) claims 190M; that source is weak and should not be relied on.
  - Source: https://cdn.ndemiccreations.com/en/news/85-plague-inc-s-2014-report-card-best-year-yet-with-45-million-infected ; https://cdn.ndemiccreations.com/en/news/122-plague-inc-s-2016-report-card-85-million-infected ; https://www.pocketgamer.biz/plague-inc-infects-more-than-100-million-players ; https://www.pocketgamer.biz/interview/73676/live-and-kicking-plague-inc-ndemic-creations/ ; https://cdn.ndemiccreations.com/en/20-media ; https://www.ndemiccreations.com/en/22-plague-inc
- **Real-world outbreak news repeatedly drove huge spikes in players: the 2014-16 Ebola outbreak and COVID-19 in early 2020. In Jan-Feb 2020 it became the top-selling app in China and then the top paid iOS app in the US. Steam concurrent players peaked at roughly 15,000 to 18,000, against a usual peak under 4,000.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: Wikipedia covers the surges and the China and US chart tops. PCGamesN (27 Jan 2020): 30-day peak just under 18k, usually under 4k. GamingOnLinux (Jan 2020): an all-time high of over 15,000.
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_Inc. ; https://www.pcgamesn.com/plague-inc-evolved/player-count ; https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2020/01/plague-inc-evolved-hits-new-all-time-high-on-player-count-due-to-the-coronavirus-outbreak-in-china/page=1/
- **Tying a game to a real disease theme also carries platform risk. On 27 Feb 2020 China's Cyberspace Administration had Plague Inc. removed from the China App Store, citing 'illegal content', and before that China was its second-largest market. Separately, Apple banned COVID-19-themed games in March 2020, and a 'Corona Control' game was accepted only after its coronavirus theme was removed. Asmodee delisted the digital Pandemic board game in January 2022 without a confirmed reason; some press speculated about COVID optics.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: Wikipedia: China removal; the cause was believed to be an unapproved 'Fake News' update. PocketGamer.biz 2020: over half of revenue came from non-English-speaking countries, and China was the second-largest market. Cult of Mac and 9to5Mac on Apple's ban and the Corona Control case. TechRaptor and DelistedGames on the Pandemic delisting.
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_Inc. ; https://www.pocketgamer.biz/interview/73676/live-and-kicking-plague-inc-ndemic-creations/ ; https://www.cultofmac.com/692491/apple-bans-covid-19-themed-games/ ; https://9to5mac.com/2021/01/22/apple-antitrust-covid-game/ ; https://techraptor.net/tabletop/news/digital-versions-of-pandemic-are-being-delisted
- **App Store guidelines directly bear on a world-map conflict game. Guideline 1.1.2 says '"Enemies" within the context of a game cannot solely target a specific race, culture, real government, corporation, or any other real entity'. Guideline 1.1.7 bars 'Harmful concepts which capitalize or seek to profit on recent or current events, such as violent conflicts, terrorist attacks, and epidemics.'** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: Quoted from Apple's App Store Review Guidelines page, fetched October 2026.
  - Source: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/
- **How Plague Inc. makes money on iOS: a $0.99 premium price (unchanged since 2012) plus paid content. The in-app purchases are expansion packs (Necroa, Neurax, Simian Flu and Shadow Plague at $1.99 each; Xenolith and Cure at $3.99), unlocks (Virus $0.99, 7 standard plagues $2.99, scenario pack $2.99, all genes $7.99, cheats $9.99) and a $12.99 'Plague Pack' bundle. It currently sits at #1 in Strategy (Top Paid), rated 4.8 from 168K ratings.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: US App Store listing fetched October 2026. Shacknews 2012 for the $0.99 launch price.
  - Source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id525818839 ; https://shacknews.com/article/74742/community-spotlight-plague-inc-by-ndemic-creations
- **On Android, Plague Inc. is free with ads and in-app purchases. A roughly $0.99 'premium' unlock removes the ads and also unlocks Mega-Brutal difficulty and fast-forward. Miniclip published the Android version from 2012 to 2022, and it had over 50 million Android installs by February 2019.** (medium confidence)
  - Evidence: plagueinc.wiki.gg: 'The premium version costs $0.99, which disables ads' and unlocks Mega-Brutal and fast-forward; a German review gives 89 cents. Wikipedia covers the Miniclip publishing years. Android Police (Feb 2019) gives the 50M+ installs. A third-party tracker (Feb 2026) lists the game as free with ads and IAP, rated 4.7 from 4.0M reviews.
  - Source: https://plagueinc.wiki.gg/wiki/Plague_Inc. ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_Inc. ; https://www.androidpolice.com/2019/02/11/rebel-inc-is-the-spiritual-successor-to-plague-inc-and-its-officially-available-on-android/ ; https://www.apppricinglab.com/app/google_play/com.miniclip.plagueinc
- **The PC version is a separate, larger product: 'Plague Inc: Evolved' at $14.99 on Steam. It entered Early Access on 20 Feb 2014 and fully released 18 Feb 2016, and the price has stayed $14.99 throughout. DLC: Aliens & Anti-Vaxxers $5.99, The Cure $5.99, soundtrack $4.99; Complete Bundle $29.40. Steam rating: 93% positive from 27,218 English reviews. It added multiplayer, co-op, a level editor and Workshop support with over 10,000 community scenarios.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: Steam store page fetched October 2026. Ndemic's Early Access pricing post and Gamewatcher (2014): $14.99.
  - Source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/246620/Plague_Inc_Evolved/ ; https://www.ndemiccreations.com/en/news/65-more-details-about-plague-inc-evolved-early-access-and-pricing ; https://www.gamewatcher.com/2014-19-02-plague-inc-evolved-on-steam-early-access-tomorrow-core-game-playable
- **Steam unit sales: over 100,000 units in the first Early Access month (2014), and over 1 million copies with 800,000 Early Access players by end of 2016 (developer figures). Later third-party estimates disagree widely, from about 1.4M (Raijin, 2026) to about 4.5M (Vaporlens, from review counts). PS4 and Xbox One together had only 'over 100,000' players by 2016.** (medium confidence)
  - Evidence: Ndemic 2014 report card: 'Selling over 100,000 units in its first month alone'. Ndemic 2016 report card: 'Plague Inc: Evolved sells over 1 million copies on Steam', 'over 800,000 players' in Early Access, 'over 100,000 people on PS4 and XB1'. Raijin and Vaporlens give the estimates.
  - Source: https://cdn.ndemiccreations.com/en/news/85-plague-inc-s-2014-report-card-best-year-yet-with-45-million-infected ; https://cdn.ndemiccreations.com/en/news/122-plague-inc-s-2016-report-card-85-million-infected ; https://raijin.gg/app/246620/Plague_Inc_Evolved ; https://vaporlens.app/app/246620/plague_inc_evolved/stats/details
- **iOS, Android and Steam each bring in broadly similar revenue for Plague Inc. Vaughan positions the studio as premium strategy outside free-to-play ('All the big players are focused in the free-to-play ocean next door'). The studio had 8 staff and no dedicated live-ops team in 2020.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: PocketGamer.biz 'Live and Kicking' interview with James Vaughan, 18 June 2020.
  - Source: https://www.pocketgamer.biz/interview/73676/live-and-kicking-plague-inc-ndemic-creations/
- **Steam price-cutting is a real lever: a 75% franchise sale plus a content update in May-June 2025 pushed Plague Inc: Evolved back above 5,000 concurrent players, roughly a quarter to a third of the 2020 COVID peak. In player reviews, the most common buying advice is to wait for a sale.** (medium confidence)
  - Evidence: AUTOMATON (2 Jun 2025): a 75% off sale from 30 May at the lowest price ever, plus the '2025 World Update' on 22 May; peak over 5,000. Vaporlens review summary: 43 reviewers say buy on sale. Vaporlens is automated, so treat it with caution.
  - Source: https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/plague-inc-evolved-20250602-342241/ ; https://vaporlens.app/app/246620/plague_inc_evolved
- **The core loop has three time scales. Every few seconds: watch the map and tap bubbles that pop up over countries to collect the points currency ('DNA'). Every minute or so: spend DNA on traits in three trees (Transmission, Symptoms, Abilities), where each trait you evolve raises the cost of every other trait by 1. Over a whole game: spread silently first, then raise lethality once the world is infected, all in a race against a global cure meter.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: plagueinc.wiki.gg DNA Points: 'DNA Points are earned actively when popping bubbles' and passively by infecting or killing; each evolved trait raises other costs by 1. Common Sense Media: points come from 'popping' bubbles 'that appear on screen as the virus spreads', and the DNA system keeps attention on the screen in an otherwise 'fairly passive' game. TheSixthAxis (PS4, June 2016): spend DNA to speed spread; too lethal kills hosts before spreading, too severe alerts countries to start a cure.
  - Source: https://plagueinc.wiki.gg/wiki/DNA_Point ; https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/plague-inc ; https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=269488
- **The counter-force is a single global 'cure' percentage that everyone can read. It starts once the disease is noticed, which happens mostly because symptoms raise its severity. Research speed comes from each country's labs, wealth and priority, and government actions can speed it up. The player slows it with specific traits and by killing research capacity. Warnings fire at 25/50/75/95/100%. You lose when the cure is finished and every infected person is cured; you win by wiping out humanity. A plague can also burn out if it kills its hosts too fast.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: plagueinc.wiki.gg Cure page: 'Plagues are more likely to be spotted through severity rises'; 2 to 10 research facilities per country (340 in the base game); lethality 'can slow down a country's cure research speed by destroying its research facilities'; 'Once all of the infected have been cured, the game ends in a defeat by cure'.
  - Source: https://plagueinc.wiki.gg/wiki/Cure
- **The world reacts in ways you can see: countries close borders, ports and airports, declare martial law and hand out masks. A one-line news ticker reports these reactions alongside random flavour and joke headlines, and the flavour headlines fade out as the game gets serious. On the map, red dots mark infected people and black dots the dead, and each country shows whether its routes are open or closed.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: plagueinc.wiki.gg News page, with headline examples such as '<country> shuts land borders', '<country> declares martial law' and 'Major oil field discovered in Arctic'. The SF Encyclopedia describes the map: groups of people as dots, 'red for infected, black for dead', and whether travel routes are open or closed.
  - Source: https://plagueinc.wiki.gg/wiki/News ; https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/plague_inc
- **Watching the world change colour is the payoff, at least as critics describe it. The SF Encyclopedia calls Plague Inc. 'heartbreaking and cathartic to play' and calls turning the world black one of the most satisfying acts in recent gaming. Vaughan deliberately chose a 'clean, dispassionate DEFCON feel' and avoided gore, because 'graphic violence would mis-sell it'.** (medium confidence)
  - Evidence: SF Encyclopedia entry. Shacknews interview (Jul 2012), quoting Vaughan directly.
  - Source: https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/plague_inc ; https://shacknews.com/article/74742/community-spotlight-plague-inc-by-ndemic-creations
- **There are four difficulty levels: Casual, Normal, Brutal and Mega-Brutal. Winning unlocks the next pathogen type and genes. Special plagues change the win condition, for example the Neurax Worm can win by enslaving humanity and the Necroa Virus turns people into zombies. The Steam version lists 10 disease types and 23 official scenarios, and modes include Speed Run, Cure Mode (play as humanity), and from Dec 2025 a free daily 'Outbreak' mode with modifiers, permanent upgrades and global rankings.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: Wikipedia: the four modes, unlocks, and special-plague goals. Steam page: 10 disease types and 23 scenarios. Ndemic news (15 Dec 2025): 'A new plague is unleashed on the world every 24 hours', 'Free for all Steam players!', plus global rankings per outbreak.

- **There is no reliable official figure for how long one game lasts. Speedruns on the easiest settings take about 1.5 minutes, and Mega-Brutal speedruns take about 11 minutes. HowLongToBeat puts the 'main story' at about 3.5 hours, 'main + extras' at about 14.5 hours and completionist at about 55 hours. One anecdotal casual game ran about 1.5 hours. Vaughan says the game is designed so people do not have to play constantly and can come back later.** (low confidence)
  - Evidence: speedrun.com leaderboards. HowLongToBeat figures as shown on Deku Deals and gg.deals. A student write-up for the 1.5-hour casual game. PocketGamer.biz 2020 interview on retention.

- **The first minute in the tutorial is very short. You name your disease, are told to tap a set starting country, pop a red bubble to collect DNA, pop an orange bubble, evolve one transmission trait, and return to the map. Bacteria is the only plague available at first.** (medium confidence)
  - Evidence: plagueinc.wiki.gg Tutorial page: 'you are first asked to name your disease'; select China as the starting country; pop the red bubble and collect DNA; pop an orange bubble; evolve Livestock 1.

- **Why non-gamers got it straight away: the pitch is one line, 'Can you infect the world?', and the goal is one sentence: infect and kill everyone before humanity finishes a cure. It flips the usual 'save the world' plot, borrows a mental model players already have from films like Contagion and from the news, and uses the world map as the whole interface. Vaughan credits polish, 'easy to learn but hard to master', a premise that 'translates perfectly across all cultures', and filling an empty niche.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: Ndemic official page tagline. Wikipedia on the inspirations (Contagion, Pandemic 2). Shacknews (2012): 'highly polished, easy to learn but hard to master'. PocketGamer.biz (2013): 'it sits firmly in an underdeveloped niche'. Scott Mitchell's Convergence paper (2018) argues the game is an incomplete text that relies on players' existing familiarity with disease media, organised around 'politics of global scale', 'viral realism' and a 'visual culture of contagion'.

- **Realism and authority are part of the brand. The CDC invited Vaughan to speak in March 2013 and praised the game's 'non-traditional route to raise public awareness'. The Cure mode (Nov 2020) was built with input from CEPI, the WHO and GOARN. The store page cites 'real-world data'. Vaughan nonetheless admits the model has 'a significant and unscientific bias against humanity', and he merged some borders (Alaska, Ireland, Portugal, Holland) to make the game play better and the map easier to read.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: Wikipedia on the CDC. Ndemic 2020-21 report card on The Cure. Shacknews 2012 quotes on the bias and the border merges.
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_Inc. ; https://ndemiccreations.com/en/news/185-ndemic-creations-2020-2021-report-card-building-the-cure ; https://shacknews.com/article/74742/community-spotlight-plague-inc-by-ndemic-creations
- **Critics were positive but not glowing: iOS Metacritic 80/100, IGN 7/10 ('Killing billions has never been so fun'), TouchArcade 4/5. It won Pocket Gamer's 2013 Overall Game of the Year. On PS4, TheSixthAxis gave it 7/10, criticising 'repetitive game flow', long waits (the reviewer needed something else playing in the background), 'isn't much of a looker', 'Game UI could be better', and frustrating losses.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: Wikipedia reception section. TheSixthAxis review, 20 June 2016.

- **The most common player complaints are: a single island (Greenland) closing its port before you can infect it, which makes the game nearly unwinnable (the #1 complaint); repetitive runs and limited variety after a few games; reliance on luck; grindy achievements locked behind DLC; and late-game waiting. The older Flash game Pandemic 2 had the same problem with Madagascar, which became the 'Shut Down Everything' meme.** (medium confidence)
  - Evidence: Vaporlens automated summary of Steam reviews: Greenland port closure (52 mentions), limited replayability (41), repetitive loop (29), luck (33), grindy achievements (36). GamesRadar also notes Greenland closing early. Know Your Meme and Giant Bomb cover the Madagascar meme.

- **Plague Inc. followed an earlier Flash game, Pandemic 2 (Dark Realm Studios, 2008), with the same premise. Dark Realm first called Plague Inc. a cash-in, but by 2013 said it did not consider it a clone. Vaughan said he deliberately added depth: 'There is no honour in mindlessly copying someone else's game'. The lesson is that a borrowed premise survives when the depth, polish and platform fit are clearly better.** (high confidence)
  - Evidence: Wikipedia on the dispute. PocketGamer.biz 2013 for the Vaughan quote and the list of additions (more story, more strategy, graphs, realism).

- **Rebel Inc. (iOS 6 Dec 2018, Android 11 Feb 2019) kept the map, the branching upgrade trees and the four difficulties, but made you the stabiliser rather than the destroyer. You balance money, inflation, corruption, local support and reputation across map sectors, chase insurgents with troops, and finish with peace negotiations. You lose if reputation hits 0, and failing to stabilise a zone within 160 turns starts draining reputation.** (high confidence)

## Implications for our design
- Make the whole game one race between two things the player can see on the map: OUR colour spreading against ONE counter-force with ONE progress bar. Plague Inc.'s goal fits in a sentence ('Can you infect the world?' before the cure hits 100%). Rebel Inc. added inflation, corruption, reputation and support, and players called it 'obtuse', 'opaque' and 'wiki-dependent'. That is exactly the owner's complaint (a). Cap the main screen at: the map, one currency counter, our progress, the counter-force bar, a speed control, and a one-line news ticker.
- Show every choice as a change on the map within seconds, not as a score. Plague Inc.'s choices feel weighty because a trait visibly speeds up the spread, or gets you noticed and makes borders close. The owner's complaint (b) was '21 vs 23'. Our upgrades should change the map's behaviour (new routes light up, a climate band opens, a region resists) and have no visible score number.
- Keep the tension that makes Plague Inc. strategic, in our own words: grow quietly, or act boldly and wake the counter-force. Plague's whole trade-off is that severity gets you detected, and being too lethal burns out your hosts. Our theme needs the same two-sided lever, or the choices will feel pointless again.
- The map is the product and must be beautiful. Plague Inc. on console was called 'isn't much of a looker' with 'one-track visuals', which leaves room for us. Spend the art budget on one illustrated fictional world that changes colour, with weather, light, cities and routes, and has a few delightful animations (borders snapping shut, a region 'turning'). This answers complaint (c) and the owner judging with his eyes. Vaughan's 'clean, dispassionate DEFCON feel' shows that restraint reads as premium.
- Use a fictional world with no real states. Make it an Earth-like fictional planet, not real geography with the names removed. Reasons: (1) the owner's no-real-countries rule; (2) App Store guideline 1.1.2 says game 'enemies' cannot solely target a 'real government'; (3) Plague Inc. was removed in China and Rebel Inc.'s Afghan-like setting needed careful handling; (4) a different world shape avoids Plague's real-Earth trade dress. Keep the realism players can reason about intuitively: cold north, hot deserts, remote islands, rich research nations, poor crowded ones, sea and air routes. Plague's appeal leaned on 'viral realism'.
- Do not make it about disease, a real epidemic or a real war. Apple guideline 1.1.7 bans games that capitalise on current 'violent conflicts, terrorist attacks, and epidemics'. COVID-themed games were rejected in 2020, Pandemic's digital edition was delisted, and China pulled Plague Inc. A disease premise would also read as a Plague clone. Pick a 'spread' fantasy players already understand from films and stories, in the way Plague borrowed from Contagion. Possible directions to test with the owner's eyes: a living bloom reclaiming a dead world, a rumour or movement sweeping nations, an invasive species, a signal or light spreading, an alien seeding. Each needs a clear counter-force with one bar.
- Give the first minute five taps at most: name your thing, tap a starting region, tap the first pickup on the map, buy one upgrade, and watch the map react. That is how long Plague's tutorial takes. Do not start with a wall of text or a rules screen; put the one-line goal on screen at all times.
- Pay the currency out through taps on the map, so a fairly passive simulation still asks for attention every few seconds. Common Sense Media credits Plague's DNA bubbles for keeping eyes on the screen. Use our own collectible and visual, never 'DNA' or Plague's bubble look, and auto-collect when the player is away or in fast-forward, so it never feels like a chore.
- Make the upgrade tree visibly different from Plague's three hex tabs (Transmission, Symptoms, Abilities), for example one radial or branching growth tree drawn in the world's art style. Keep the rule that each purchase raises later costs, which forces commitment. Show a preview of each upgrade's effect on the map, not a number.
- Fix Plague's known pains on purpose. (1) Waiting: give pause, 2x and 4x speed to everyone from the start (Plague gated fast-forward behind a paid unlock on Android), and add a late-game 'finish' accelerator. (2) The Greenland or Madagascar problem: never let one region sealing itself make a game unwinnable; always give a counter-tool (for example a 'break the blockade' upgrade). (3) Repetition: vary runs with different starting regions, world modifiers and alternate win conditions, as Plague's special plagues do.
- Aim for a single game of about 20 to 40 minutes at normal speed (our inference; Plague has no official figure), saved automatically and resumable at any moment on phone. Vaughan designed Plague so players 'don't need to play constantly'. A whole game should end in an unmistakable win or loss screen showing the world map as it finished, which also makes the shareable picture.
- Content structure: 4 difficulty levels, winning unlocks the next 'type' with its own twist and win condition, then a set of authored scenarios that twist the rules. Plague's Steam version advertises 10 types and 23 scenarios. Our existing daily seeding can power a free 'world of the day' (Ndemic added a daily Outbreak mode in December 2025), but without named leaderboards: show personal bests and the share card only.
- Our no-user-generated-content rule removes Plague's Workshop long tail (10,000+ community scenarios). Plan to replace it with a steady cadence of authored scenarios and paid expansion packs, which also earn money, as Plague's $1.99 to $3.99 expansions and $5.99 Steam DLC show.
- Pricing that fits the evidence and our privacy rules. Steam: premium at $9.99 to $14.99 (Plague and Rebel are both $14.99), plus paid scenario or expansion DLC, and expect most sales to come from deep discounts. Phone: either a low premium price ($0.99 to $2.99, as Plague iOS, Rebel iOS and After Inc. charge), or free first world plus a one-time unlock, Polytopia-style with no ads. Third-party ads conflict with our 'strictly necessary cookies only, no tracking' rule. Web: a free playable first scenario via a link, then a one-time Stripe unlock using the existing token flow.
- Expect phone discovery to be the hard part. Premium mobile games were 4% of downloads in 2025, and Polytopia's GM names visibility as the biggest challenge. Plague grew by word of mouth because it was 'so different' and screenshot-able. The main screen must therefore be strikingly beautiful and readable in one store screenshot, and the end-of-game world map must make a share card people want to post.
- Keep the app wording careful and non-graphic. Plague is rated 9+ or 12+ partly because there is no gore, just dots and a skull icon, and Vaughan said 'graphic violence would mis-sell it'. A non-violent or abstract spread theme widens the audience and lowers the store-review risk.
- Expect revenue roughly evenly from iOS, Android and Steam, as Ndemic reports for Plague. Ship all three, with the web demo as the funnel. Do not count on consoles: Plague: Evolved on PS4 and Xbox One had only about 100k players by 2016, against over 1M copies on Steam.
- Reuse from the repo: the deterministic seeded RNG, the headless simulator with bot policies (rewritten for map balance, e.g. a target win rate per difficulty, and no unwinnable seeds caused by a single sealed region), the strict content validator (for regions, upgrades, scenarios and news lines), the Playwright harness, the Stripe-to-Worker unlock, the PWA, Tauri and Capacitor builds, daily seeding and the share-card renderer. The card-run engine and card content are a different game and should not be carried over.
- A news ticker is cheap and adds a lot of flavour without walls of text. One short headline at a time, about the fictional world reacting to what you do, with occasional jokes that fade out as the stakes rise. It is the only text the main loop needs; avoid paragraph pop-ups, which were a specific Rebel Inc. complaint.
- Lesson from Ndemic's own follow-ups: same studio, same UI pattern and heavy cross-promotion, yet Rebel Inc. and After Inc. reached roughly a tenth or less of Plague's Steam footprint. The premise and the clarity of the fantasy matter more than added systems, so put most of the concept effort into the one-sentence fantasy and the main-screen picture, not into mechanical depth.
