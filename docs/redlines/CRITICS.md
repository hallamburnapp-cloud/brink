# Three critics on the second draft (v5), before the owner saw it

Three independent readers were given `SPEC.md` (as first drafted), the first draft it replaced and the owner's review of that first draft, and told to tear in. Their findings are kept here in full; v5.1 of the spec answers them. Lenses: a senior systems designer (roguelikes; Balatro, Slay the Spire, Plague Inc, Rebel Inc, DEFCON); a scholar of deterrence and crisis bargaining who plays strategy games; a careful non-gamer who watches over a partner's shoulder.


## The systems designer

**Verdict.** This draft answers the review honestly, and the engine it found is real and must not be lost: an Opportunist that stops when a line is honoured and a Paranoid that escalates when it is, so the same act is cure or poison depending on who you face; settlement as the Federation's own willingness; the WORLD WAR ending that shows the chain. But as written it is solved in about five runs, not twenty: the four doctrines are a printed table whose counters the Intel Director reads out by day 10, three of the four reward the same firmness, each brake is a kill switch after which the bar fills by waiting, and pledging, the named core choice, is dominated by never pledging. The numbers do not close either: the treaty needs four or fewer regions fighting while the median game is specified to pass seven with no rule for a region to stop fighting, and the draft is both "every five days" and "five drafts" in a sixty-day game.

- **[blocker] Settlement, the war bar and region states.** Line 15: "A treaty with fighting in more than 4 regions is not offered." Line 74: "At 100% they sign and the game is won, that day." Balance gate, line 172: "the war bar passes 7 of 12 in the median game." Nothing in the spec returns a FIGHTING region to CONTESTED or CALM: the ladder comes down (line 64), regions do not. So the war bar is monotonic and, by the spec's own gate, the median game has seven regions fighting with no path back under four. The median game cannot end in TREATY; it ends FROZEN or WORLD WAR whatever the player does. Screen moment: the blue bar reaches 100% and nothing happens; the watcher says "she won" and the clock keeps running. The spec does not even say whether the bar stalls at 99 or sits at 100 unoffered.
  *Fix proposed:* Write de-escalation: a FIGHTING region drops to CONTESTED after N days when the ladder is at PRESSING or below and it is not being pressed; an accepted off-ramp ceasefires every fighting region in the claims it names. Then make the treaty gate a rate, not a count: "no region has fallen in the last seven days," with the war bar as the score. If a count stays, reconcile it with the gates (median peak must sit below the sign threshold) and cap the bar visibly with a reason the watcher can read: "SETTLEMENT 100% — they will not sign while five regions fight."

- **[blocker] The other side: doctrines, brakes and the loop.** Lines 49-54 are a four-row strategy guide: one line of "what stops it" per doctrine, and line 178 says the doctrine is known by about day 10, so by the third of five drafts the player is told which row they are in. Run it. Day 1 (doctrine unknown): honour inherited tests, because three of four doctrines reward firmness and only the Paranoid punishes it; take INTEL at draft 1, because knowing the row is worth more than any card. Day 10, read THE OTHER SIDE: Opportunist, pledge everything you can afford and honour (the spec's own gate at line 169 admits this wins over 60%); Paranoid, no force, no new pledges, fold the inherited ones early (fold is the Opportunist's trigger, not the Paranoid's, so folding is almost free here) and draft TALK; Revisionist, pledge the three visible claims and honour twice each, or concede one; Gambler, take the firm option twice. Day 30 in every case: the brake has fired, "what stops it" has stopped it, and settlement rises "every day they make no gains" (line 71), so the player presses fast and watches a bar fill. Four doctrines, four recipes, one run each to learn them: solved in about five runs. Red lines change which FORCE cards are forbidden, not what the best play is. This is a tree with four branches whose root node is "which doctrine," wearing a draft.
  *Fix proposed:* Five changes, all inside the constraints. (a) Intel never names a doctrine. THE OTHER SIDE reports observed behaviour only ("they have pressed four unpledged regions and tested none of ours"); the ending names it. (b) Brakes buy days, not the game: a brake drops the ladder one step and holds for five to eight days, then the doctrine resumes against a new target and the same brake costs more (the second honour costs more influence; the second reassurance reads as weakness unless credibility is above 60). (c) Split the rows two and two on the firm/soft axis: the Gambler doubles down when blocked and folds only to a hold followed by an off-ramp; the Revisionist stops only to a block plus a concession. (d) Cap settlement from "no gains" at about 60; the last 40 need an off-ramp with a visible concession (a claim granted, a pledge withdrawn, a fleet pulled back), so every run ends with a choice of what to give that differs by red lines, claims and pledges. That also designs the second half of line 38, "can save face," which currently costs nothing. (e) Let a doctrine drift once per run on a trigger (an Opportunist folded to twice becomes a Gambler; a Paranoid shown force for ten days acquires claims): eight behaviours from four tables, no new content.

- **[blocker] Pledges, credibility and deterrence.** Line 93: "Pledges are the deterrence: a line is only as good as your record of backing lines." Line 90: "Pledging raises its chance of being tested." Nothing in the spec says a pledge ever lowers the chance a region is pressed, at any credibility. Credibility (lines 67-69) goes up and down and is spoken by the Intel Director, but nothing states what it changes: not the ladder, not the press rate, not settlement, not the Coalition. Meanwhile line 68 rewards "when you say less than you do," which implies force without a pledge exists, and line 64 counts "two blocked pushes in a row" without requiring a pledge. If an unpledged region can be defended with a FORCE measure, a pledge is strictly worse than silence: it attracts tests, exposes you to the fold penalty, and protects nothing. The best player never pledges and blocks ad hoc, and no balance gate tests that bot (line 168 only tests "never pledges and never answers"). The design's named core choice, "which lines to draw" (line 19), is dominated by drawing none. This is the first draft's "deterrence has a trade-off" hole in its new clothes.
  *Fix proposed:* Give credibility teeth and make them the pledge's payoff: a pledged region is pressed less often when credibility is above a threshold (deterrence works) and more often when below (a bluff invites a call); the Opportunist probes each pledge once regardless. Force is available only through a pledge honoured, or through a FORCE measure that costs more and counts as overreach for the Coalition, so the pledge is the cheap credible way to defend and silence-then-force the expensive one. Cap pledges at three with a public withdrawal verb (credibility down a little, less than a fold), so "pledge everything" is impossible and "which three" is the choice; this also makes line 27's "six places" true. Add the gate: a bot that never pledges and blocks every press ad hoc wins at least ten points less often than the sensible bot against every doctrine.

- **[major] The cabinet: draft cadence and synergy density.** Line 99: "Every five days the Chief of Staff brings three cards." Lines 161 and 180: "the five drafts." Sixty days at one every five is twelve drafts; five drafts is one every twelve days. The two readings are different games. With twelve, the player sees 36 of 40 cards every run and the pool is not a pool. With five, the player sees 15 and makes five picks: a named pair (Hotline + Back-channel) is even available in about 13% of runs (15/40 x 14/39) and costs two of five picks to assemble; across the four named pairs, roughly 40% of runs offer any of them. There is no reroll, no sell, no removal, and no stated hand limit. Balatro's grip is dozens of offers with rerolls; Slay the Spire's is forty-plus picks with removal. Five picks and four named pairs is a hand of singletons, and "synergies" is a sentence in the spec, not a thing that happens on screen.
  *Fix proposed:* Pick one cadence and write it. Recommended: a draft every six days aligned to the phones (ten drafts) from a pool of sixty, offer three, take one; let influence buy a reroll (3) so the economy has a sink. Make the base synergies tag-based so every run has some (any two FORCE: honour costs 3 less; TALK + INTEL: the off-ramp acceptance range tightens; two HOME: income floor rises) and keep the named pairs as rare spikes. State the hand limit (six) and what leaves the hand (measures are spent when played; people and policies stay).

- **[major] Influence economy.** Lines 108-110 name sources and sinks and give no numbers: no starting value, no per-day rate, no card cost, no off-ramp cost, "a little influence" for passing a draft. The only figure in the whole economy is the example "Influence -12" for honouring (line 118). With about ten phones and influence "mainly from decisions," the economy is ten discrete events and cannot be checked. It also contains the spiral line 18 says is gone: honour costs influence; a player who cannot pay must fold; a fold makes the Federation "press harder there and everywhere" (line 91); more tests, more honours to pay for, broke, fold again. "A burning bloc is a wound, not a spiral" answers the old income spiral, not this one.
  *Fix proposed:* Write the numbers so the simulator has something to run: start 30; +2 a day base; each phone option between -15 and +10; cards 8 to 20; off-ramps 10 to 25; reroll 3; pass a draft +6. Make the honour cost fall with credibility (the first honour of a run costs 12, the third costs 6 at credibility 70) so the spiral has a brake that rewards the record of backing lines, and let HOME cards raise the base rate so the home front is the floor. Gate it: the sensible bot is below zero when tested in fewer than 10% of games.

- **[major] Undefined verbs: measures, off-ramps, hesitation, overreach, home front, bloc signatures, stakes.** The spec says what these mean and never how the player does them, which is exactly the shape of the first draft's claimed-not-designed trade-off. (1) Measures: "spent on measures" (line 110), but when and where does the player play Fleet to the Strait? At the draft, from the CABINET tab at any time, or only inside a phone? If any time, there is a third verb the watcher cannot see and the "one marker only" screen (line 14) is false. (2) Off-ramps: "an off-ramp they can present at home" (line 72), "off-ramps that offer nothing about the claims" (line 53), "spent on off-ramps": never defined as a card, a phone option or a tab action, never costed. (3) "Hesitation" (line 54) when the clock stops on every phone. (4) "Overreach" (line 81): which actions? (5) "The home front holds / cracks" (lines 67-68): no home-front state exists. (6) Other blocs "sign" (line 72): no condition, no value, and if three signatures are worth enough, the review's point 3 returns in miniature. (7) The Gambler "raises the stakes": raises what?
  *Fix proposed:* A measure is played only from a phone or a test, as an extra option that appears when its card is in hand, so the hand widens phone choices and the clock never runs while the player acts. An off-ramp is one verb on THE OTHER SIDE tab, available at PRESSING or above: choose one of three terms (recognise a claim, withdraw a pledge, pull a measure back), pay 10 to 25; the agent accepts or refuses by doctrine and credibility, the answer lands one to three days later and the ticker says so. Hesitation is the middle option on a test phone ("reinforce quietly, decide later"). Overreach is any FORCE measure played outside your bloc, or honouring at a claim. Home front is 0 to 100, moved by HOME cards and phone choices; below 30 income halves and credibility falls. A bloc signs when nothing in it has been pressed for ten days and is worth a fixed 10 settlement, so three signatures cannot pass 30. Stakes: the Gambler climbs a ladder step and presses one more region.

- **[major] The test: ring or phone.** Line 14: "tapping it [the ring] opens the decision and stops the clock." Line 114: a phone arrives "whenever the Federation tests a pledge," and phones stop the clock on arrival. These contradict. If the ring waits for a tap, what happens when the player does not tap? At fast speed a six-day window is 24 seconds. Does the region fall? Does the pledge fold silently, with the credibility crash that follows? That is the whack-a-mole the review killed, now with one mole and the highest stakes in the game, and the fast button becomes a trap.
  *Fix proposed:* A test is a phone: the clock stops when it arrives. The ring is the marker the phone draws on the map so the watcher knows where. If a timed element is wanted, make "say nothing yet" an explicit option on the test phone with its own stated cost, and have the Gambler read it as hesitation: a designed consequence, not a missed input.

- **[major] Real time between stops.** Line 85: "nothing spreads by itself." Phones every six days; drafts every five or twelve. Between stops the Federation at PROBING presses one or two regions and a pressed region "falls after a number of days." At roughly 12 seconds a day that is 60 to 70 seconds of a map on which one amber region may become two. The player presses fast and waits for the next popup: the review's point 5, "turn-based with friction," with longer gaps. Plague Inc holds because the map changes every few seconds; this map changes about twice a week.
  *Fix proposed:* Give the agent a visible body. The ladder is posture, and posture is observable in life: draw Federation force markers moving toward the regions it will press next, two days ahead, so the watcher sees it coming and the player can still pledge in time; let a contested region's dots densify day by day so "about to fall" is readable; tilt Farsouth's colour with the Coalition's lean; let the ticker carry one Federation move a day. Then real time earns its clock and the ring arrives where the watcher already expected it.

- **[major] The screen: ladder and causality hidden in a tab.** The ladder step (line 62) is the single state that decides how many regions fall and how fast, and it lives in THE OTHER SIDE tab (line 136), as does "why settlement moved last." The causal chain the design is built on, "they pressed there because she folded here," exists only as a scrolling 28 px ticker line and in the WORLD WAR ending (line 155), after the fact. During play the watcher sees amber appear and cannot connect it to anything. The design's own watcher sentence (line 42) needs "deciding whether to back them up" to be visible: the backing is in a phone, fine, but its effect is not.
  *Fix proposed:* Put the ladder word under the WAR bar (PROBING to GENERAL WAR); it is public posture in the fiction, not fog. When the Federation moves in response to the player (a fold, a red line crossed, an off-ramp accepted), flash the cause region and the response regions together for two seconds and pin the ticker line for the day ("After Shoal folded: pressing Vessary, Lintel"). Keep the fog where it belongs: doctrine, red lines, credibility.

- **[major] The slice and its gate.** Line 163: five testers, two plays each, count unprompted third plays; fewer than two of five and the design changes. Three problems. (a) It is noise: a game whose true third-play rate is 20% passes this bar 26% of the time, and one at 40% fails it 34% of the time (binomial, n=5). (b) It tests the wrong failure. The review's charge was "solved after two or three"; a third play after two is curiosity ("was it the other one?"), and with exactly two doctrines in the slice, run three is by construction a doctrine the tester has already beaten. The bar passes a game that is solved in three. (c) The slice list (lines 160-162) omits the simulator and bots, yet the gates at 166-174 are the only check that the numbers close; a cold test on a game whose median run is unwinnable (the first blocker) measures nothing. The review's own bar was five or more unprompted replays.
  *Fix proposed:* Run the simulator and pass the gates before any tester sits down. Give testers three plays each, not two, and count the unprompted fourth. After play three, ask each what the other side wanted and what they would do differently: a tester who recites the recipe and stops is the failure the review named; one who cannot and plays on is the pass. Bar: at least three of five play a fourth game, and at least two describe the Federation, not the cards, when asked what changed. Put three doctrines in the slice if at all possible so run three can differ; if only two, Opportunist and Paranoid is the right pair, since they are the engine.

- **[major] Variance: setup collisions are the run-definer and are left to chance.** Lines 143-144 draw doctrine, red lines, claims, inherited pledges and flashpoints independently. The runs anyone will remember are the ones where these collide: an inherited pledge in the Sound plus the red line "no Republic forces in the Sound" (honour crosses the line, fold loses credibility); a Revisionist claim on a pledged region; a Paranoid with a flashpoint on its own border. That collision is the commitment trap the review called the owner's edge (REVIEW line 35). Drawn independently it occurs in a fraction of runs; the rest are a straight identification exercise followed by the recipe.
  *Fix proposed:* The setup generator guarantees at least one collision per run (inherited pledge against a probable red line or a claim) and names it in the third line of the opening explanation in plain words: "You inherited a promise to Shoal. We think they will not tolerate a fleet there." The watcher knows the dilemma from day 1 and the run has a title.

- **[minor] Session length.** "Twelve to fifteen minutes" (line 24) is clock time: sixty days at about 12 seconds. Fifteen stops at 30 to 60 seconds of reading each (forty words, two or three costed options, a glance at THE OTHER SIDE) add 8 to 15 minutes: the session is 20 to 27 minutes, which is already Plague Inc's length. Open decision 4 ("ninety days would want...") is framed on the wrong number, and the spec never states seconds per day.
  *Fix proposed:* State the clock (12 s a day normal, 4 s fast) and budget the session as clock plus stops. If 15 minutes is the target, forty-five days or ten stops; if sixty days stays, say the session is twenty minutes.

- **[minor] The cabinet: INTEL as the dominant first pick.** Knowing the doctrine decides which half of the pool is live, so INTEL (Spymaster, Intercepts) is the right first draft in every run: "Alert level is a dominant first buy" again, in a different tag. Spymaster + Intercepts ("red lines known by day 10," line 106) stacks on top.
  *Fix proposed:* Mostly fixed by the doctrine blocker's (a): if intel never names the doctrine, its value drops to red lines. Also price intel in something visible: Intercepts is itself a red-line candidate ("their embassy knows"), Spymaster costs home front every week.

- **[minor] The cabinet: Hawk General + Reserves.** "Honouring a line costs no influence, but a Paranoid Federation climbs a step" (line 107) removes the only brake on the firm strategy against three of four doctrines. With the doctrine known on day 10 and this pair on offer, it is an auto-take that ends the decisions for the rest of the run.
  *Fix proposed:* Free honouring, but each honour raises the ladder a step against every doctrine, not only the Paranoid: calling up Reserves is mobilisation, and the other side reads mobilisation whoever they are.

- **[minor] Phones: numbers on hidden things.** Line 139: "Hidden things are spoken, never numbered, except as the Intel Director's ranges." Line 120, a phone option: "40 to 60% they read it as weakness." The rule reads as broken on the first phone.
  *Fix proposed:* Either every range on an option is in the Intel Director's voice and says so ("Intel: 40 to 60% they read it as weakness"), or options carry words only ("they may read it as weakness") and the ranges live in the tab.

- **[minor] Phones: arcs borrowed without levers.** Templates are "built from the brief's arcs (the blockade, the false alarm, the cables, the proxy, the cyberattack, the defector, the debris, the ultimatum, the summit, the satellite)" (line 125), but this design has no missile, satellite or cyber mechanics. The false alarm ("six launches... 30 to 50% sure," line 122) has no response that exists in the spec: what does "respond" do in a game of pledges, ladder steps and off-ramps?
  *Fix proposed:* Map each arc to one lever before writing templates: false alarm to a FORCE response that may cross "no missiles north of Edda Vale"; cables to the Sound's sea-lane red line; defector to a red line revealed with a confidence; ultimatum to humiliation and settlement. Drop any arc that has no lever.

- **[minor] Leader types.** "Unlocked by winning" (line 147): one win unlocks all three or one each? The Spymaster (a red line known on day 1) is plainly the strongest; the Hawk's "a Paranoid Federation starts a step higher" is a 25% penalty against a hidden draw and reads as a trap rather than a style.
  *Fix proposed:* One unlock per win in a fixed order (Dove, Hawk, Spymaster). Give the Spymaster a cost (credibility starts low: a spy is not trusted) and give the Hawk a payoff that is not doctrine-dependent (first honour of the run is free).

- **[minor] Endings: FROZEN.** Day 60 with two regions fighting and settlement at 90 is, in the fiction, peace kept; "graded low" flat (line 156) under a treaty signed with eleven of twelve regions burning is backwards, and a flat grade gives the watcher nothing to read in the last ten days of a close game.
  *Fix proposed:* Score FROZEN by regions fighting and settlement reached, below every TREATY but ordered, with its own sentence: "Nothing was signed. Two regions still fight. They came within a week of stopping."


## The escalation scholar

**Verdict.** This draft fixes the right thing: the Federation is an agent, commitment is the central choice, and the Opportunist/Paranoid pair is Jervis's deterrence-model-versus-spiral-model dilemma, the best possible spine for this game and the one thing that must not be lost (along with inherited pledges, "if they fear us, this is how it starts" printed on the honour choice, the humiliation rule, and the ending that shows the causal chain). But the model as written would not survive a scholar or a third run: a pledge never deters (it only invites a test you pay to pass), folding is bad against every doctrine (so the spiral half of the dilemma is absent and the slice collapses to a one-bit guessing game), a test is a single phone with no second round (no chicken, no 3am), the Federation's red lines are silent landmines rather than signals and the Federation never bluffs, and no region ever leaves FIGHTING while a treaty is barred above four, which silently kills the blue bar. Most fixes are cheap and inside the constraints; the slice should take the within-type dials, declared lines with bluffs, multi-round tests and the ladder word on screen, and refuse the research projects (two-sided misperception as a process, a domestic-politics model, Bayesian intel, four fully distinct doctrines).

- **[blocker] Pledges / the deterrence model (SPEC.md lines 89-93).** The spec says "Pledges are the deterrence" but no mechanic makes a pledge deter. The only stated effects are "Pledging raises its chance of being tested" and, at the test, honour (−12 influence) or fold (credibility falls). Nothing says a pledged region is pressed less, falls slower, or is tested less at high credibility. So a pledge is a provocation with a reputation reward: bait you pay to pass. Against the Opportunist, which "probes every line once", every pledge is a guaranteed −12 bill; six pledges is a 72-influence liability the player cannot see anywhere on screen. The dominant play is to pledge only what you can afford to honour once and treat lines as bait, never as shields. Screen moment: a white-outlined PLEDGED region turns amber anyway, every single time, and the watcher says "why does she keep promising things if it just makes them attack there?" The Schelling point that a credible commitment is one that is not tested is absent.
  *Fix proposed:* Make the test rate a function of credibility and stake: at credibility ≥ 60 a pledged region is pressed at half the rate of an unpledged one and falls at half speed when pressed; below 40 a pledge doubles the test rate (the bluff is called). Print the direction on the pledge confirm: "At our standing this line deters" / "this line invites". Define "say less than you do" (line 67) mechanically: force sent to an unpledged contested region is a quiet honour (+credibility, no fold exposure), so the player chooses between loud lines and quiet deeds. Show exposure in the WORLD tab: "4 lines drawn · honouring one costs about 12 · you have 30".

- **[blocker] The Federation agent / doctrines (lines 49-54, 91, 169, 178).** With the slice's two doctrines the game is one bit. Opportunist counter: pledge and honour (the gate at line 169 says this wins >60%). Paranoid counter: never deploy, reassure (line 52). Fold is universally bad ("the Federation presses harder there and everywhere", line 91), so against a Paranoid the player gets none of the spiral model's upside for backing down; the one real dilemma (a fold calms them but costs credibility) cannot occur. Once the Intel Director says "Paranoid, 80%" around day 10 (line 178), the remaining 50 days are a recipe. A good player reads the tell on run 2 (a Paranoid complains about your forces in the ticker; an Opportunist probes the unpledged Scatter) and the game is solved on run 3, which is exactly the review's original verdict with better nouns. Jervis's point is that you never get certainty and that your own conduct changes what they are; here the type is fixed and knowable.
  *Fix proposed:* (a) Doctrine-dependent fold: near a Paranoid a fold is a step DOWN the ladder and −credibility; near an Opportunist a step UP; the phone prints "Credibility falls · if they are scared, this calms them; if they are hungry, it feeds them". (b) Every Federation has both reflexes at weights drawn per run: jumpiness 0.2-0.8 (how much force near its border it tolerates) and appetite 0.2-0.8 (how hard it probes undefended regions); the doctrine label names the dominant one, so the right amount of firmness is a dial found by feel, not a switch. (c) Intel confidence ceiling about 80%. (d) Add the cartoon gate: an oracle bot told the doctrine on day 1 wins no more than 15 points above the sensible bot; if it wins 90%, the types are cartoons and the dials must widen.

- **[blocker] The race / region state machine (lines 15, 84-87, 95-96).** Lines 84-87 define CALM → CONTESTED → FIGHTING and say "Only the Federation's moves and your choices change a region", but no path out of FIGHTING is given anywhere. Line 15: "A treaty with fighting in more than 4 regions is not offered." Line 74: "At 100% they sign." WAR ends at 12 (line 95). So with 5 to 11 regions fighting the settlement bar is either silently capped or meaningless, and the game is unwinnable without saying so: a player on day 30 with 6 fighting and settlement at 70% is playing for FROZEN and does not know it. This is the review's death spiral (REVIEW.md line 31) in new clothes, and it breaks the two-bar race, the one thing the review said to keep, because one bar stops meaning anything. Also line 87 "or at once on an escalation" implies every pressed region falls together on a step up: three or four regions going red in one day with no decision in between, a cliff with no phone.
  *Fix proposed:* Define recovery: a FIGHTING region drops to CONTESTED when the ladder steps down, or when an accepted off-ramp names it; a CONTESTED region returns to CALM after N days unpressed. On an escalation only the ringed region falls at once; the others accelerate. Make the cap visible: above 4 fighting the blue bar greys with the words "no treaty while 6 fight" so the watcher sees why the bar has stopped. Add a gate: of the sensible bot's losses, fewer than 20% occur with settlement above 80%.

- **[major] Red lines and the Federation's own credibility (lines 56-60, 102).** Red lines are hidden, silent until crossed, and crossing is an automatic step up "within a day", identical for every doctrine. On a first run the player drafts Fleet to the Strait, the ladder jumps, and the headline explains afterwards. That is a hidden rule with a punishment attached, not a crisis: the decision moment never happened, and the watcher sees the map go red for a reason nobody on screen gave in advance. In crisis bargaining the adversary declares lines and your problem is whether they mean it. Here the Federation never declares, never bluffs, and has no credibility of its own, so the model is one-sided: your resolve is a number, theirs does not exist. Schelling's whole subject is two parties each trying to make threats believable.
  *Fix proposed:* Each true red line is signalled before it can be crossed (a diplomatic-note phone or a ticker speech: "Wintermast: Republic forces in the Sound would be a grave matter"), and the Federation also declares one or two bluff lines per run. Intel reports "they say the Sound is a line; 40 to 60% they mean it". Crossing a bluff costs nothing and raises your credibility; crossing a real one climbs the ladder with the step size by doctrine (Paranoid two steps on proximity lines, Opportunist one). Pullback uncrosses a line at a credibility cost. This is cheap: a list of eight with a real/bluff flag and two template lines each, and it turns landmines into the call-their-bluff decision the game is supposedly about.

- **[major] The 3am phone / tests are one-shot (lines 89-92, 114-121).** A test is a single phone: honour or fold, then it is over. Nothing lets the Federation refuse to back down after you honour. The actual 3am horror, you sent the fleet and they did not stop, cannot happen. The Gambler's entire concept ("raises the stakes", line 54) requires a second round to exist. With the clock stopped and two or three choices with printed odds, the phone as written is the first draft's popup with ranges on it, and the review's "turn-based with friction" still applies. There is also no cost to deciding slowly: the one decision real crisis managers dread, whether to wait for confirmation, is not a choice.
  *Fix proposed:* A test is a sequence of up to three rounds. Round one: honour / fold / wait. If honoured, the Federation backs down with a probability by doctrine and ladder step (Opportunist at PROBING ~90%; Gambler at MOBILISED ~40%); otherwise round two: "They have not moved. Reinforce (ladder +1, −influence) / Hold (a day passes; 50 to 70% they blink) / Pull back (a fold, worse than folding at once)". Always offer WAIT with its risk printed ("a day; Vessary 30 to 50% falls; confirmation may arrive"). When the Hawk General and Dove Foreign Minister are both in hand, each adds a one-line opinion to the phone, so the drafted cabinet acquires a voice for two lines of text per template.

- **[major] The ladder, the cost of war and the false alarm (lines 62-65, 71-74, 122).** Settlement rises "as the ladder comes down" and falls when they gain; the Federation never fears GENERAL WAR, where "the war bar fills in days" and the agent has nothing to lose. So there is no bargaining range created by the cost of war (Fearon, Powell), no mutual search for an exit at the top (the Cuban moment), and deliberate brinkmanship, climbing to force a settlement, is impossible rather than merely dangerous. The stability-instability paradox cannot appear because the top of the ladder is a fast loss, not a shared terror. The false alarm ("six launches... 30 to 50% sure it is real") has no coupling to the ladder: at PROBING it is incoherent, and at MOBILISED it should be the most dangerous moment in the game.
  *Fix proposed:* At MOBILISED and FIGHTING both sides' fear adds to settlement daily (+2 / +3), but each day at those steps carries a chance of an accidental step up (3% / 6%): the threat that leaves something to chance. The false-alarm template's chance of being real is 0 at PROBING, 10% at MOBILISED, 25% at FIGHTING, and the player's over-reaction (alert, launch posture) is the Paranoid trigger. Hotline in hand resolves the alarm ("Wintermast picks up; it was a flock"), which is what hotlines are for; move its synergy from "off-ramps always land" (line 105) to this.

- **[major] Off-ramps and the settlement maths (lines 38, 53, 71-74, 110).** An off-ramp is a purchase of settlement with a doctrine risk; it names nothing. The Revisionist needs "an off-ramp that mentions a claim" (line 53), so the slot exists in principle, but the Munich dilemma, give them Vessary and end it or refuse and fight, is never put on the table as a choice with a stated price. Line 38 says they sign when they "stop winning" AND have "a way to stop"; the maths at lines 71-74 only requires the first: "up every day they make no gains" lets the fortress player (block everything, offer nothing) reach 100% by time alone if the daily increment is 2 or more over 50 days.
  *Fix proposed:* An off-ramp names its content from a slot: a pledge you will drop, a region you will not pledge, a withdrawal, a summit without preconditions, a claim conceded. Each prints the read by doctrine as a range ("if they are hungry, 50 to 70% this feeds them"). Cap settlement at 75% until one off-ramp has been accepted and grey the top quarter of the blue bar with "they need a way out" so the watcher sees the second half of the thesis. A concession lowers Coalition lean (line 81), a cost that already exists; print it on the choice.

- **[major] The cabinet / draft cadence (lines 17, 100, 161, 180).** Line 100 says "Every five days the Chief of Staff brings three cards"; lines 17, 161 and 180 say "the five drafts". Sixty days at one draft per five days is twelve drafts: a twelve-card hand, 36 of 40 cards seen per game, every pair synergy trivially assembled, the pool exhausted on run one, which is REVIEW.md point 5 again. Five drafts is a five-card hand with fifteen cards seen. These are different games and the balance gates cannot be read until one is chosen. The gate at line 174 (every card drafted in at least 10% of games) is also miscalibrated: with five picks from forty, uniform drafting is 12.5% per card, so the floor is nearly automatic for weak cards and nearly unreachable once any card is clearly strong.
  *Fix proposed:* Pick one and fix the text. Five drafts every twelve days keeps the hand tight and the pool fresh for eight runs; if the owner wants the stop cadence, fold drafts into phones as line 24 already proposes. Re-state the card gate as a band (every card drafted in 8 to 25% of the sensible bot's games) so it catches both dead cards and auto-picks.

- **[major] The screen / the watcher test (lines 40-42, 131-137).** The ladder step, the doctrine guess and their read of you live in the third panel tab. The design's own test is that a watcher can say what is happening; the single most important fact about the other side, PROBING versus MOBILISED, is behind a tap. The watcher sees red regions and a blue bar but not that the Federation has mobilised, so "a race of settlement against war" reads as two bars and an invisible agent, and the moment the game is about (the other side changing its temperature in response to you) has no screen presence.
  *Fix proposed:* Put the ladder word under the race bars in the Federation's colour, changing with a ticker line and a sound ("WINTERMAST: MOBILISED"). Surface the read of you as a periodic ticker line ("Wintermast thinks you will fold"). Keep the tab for the detail.

- **[major] Ambiguity and the player's face (lines 67, 72-73, 89-91, 103-107).** The player has exactly one kind of commitment, a public binary pledge, and one kind of retreat, a public fold. There is no vague warning, no private assurance, no quiet redeployment. The spec hints at "say less than you do" (line 67) but offers no ambiguous instrument, so the value and cost of ambiguity, the central trade-off of commitment strategy, never arises. The Federation gets face-saving (an off-ramp "they can present at home", humiliation at line 73); the player gets none. The humiliation rule is a trap the player can only trigger, not a lever they can choose to pull or not.
  *Fix proposed:* Two levels of line: WARN (private, via Back-channel; small test-rate effect; small fold cost) and PLEDGE (public; the full mechanic). Three exits from a test: honour; fold publicly (full credibility loss, home front −); redeploy quietly (half credibility loss, −influence, needs Press blackout or Back-channel in hand). Apply loud/quiet to blocks too: a block with the Press Secretary in hand raises the home front but humiliates them (settlement −); a quiet block lets them climb down. All of this uses cards already on the list.

- **[major] Alliance entrapment / the Coalition (lines 57, 78-82, 92).** Inherited pledges as "alliance obligations you did not choose" are the right idea and must stay; they are what gives the first week its dilemma against an unknown doctrine. But the ally is inert and the Coalition only "leans"; it never does anything. Entrapment is when an ally's own provocation triggers your pledge (chain-ganging); moral hazard is when your guarantee makes it bolder. Neither can happen, and the "no alliance with Farsouth" red line already on the list (line 57) is a loaded trap that nothing can fire.
  *Fix proposed:* One template per inherited pledge: "Shoal has seized a Federation trawler. Wintermast demands it back. Disown them (−credibility, the pledge weakens) / Back them (the test comes early) / Lean on them privately (Back-channel; 50 to 70% they comply)". Give Farsouth one action of its own per run after you court it (a fleet into the Sound, attributed to you in the ticker) that can cross the "no alliance with Farsouth" line on your behalf. Add one or two concrete courting actions so the Coalition's lean is something the player does, not weather.

- **[major] Gambler and Revisionist doctrines (lines 53-54, 160).** Gambler: "raises the stakes whenever you hesitate", but the clock stops on every phone (line 114), so there is no hesitation in game time; the trigger has no mechanical hook. Revisionist: "presses them whatever you do" and "What stops it: being blocked twice at a claim" contradict each other. The slice (line 160) rightly excludes both; the full design should not inherit them as written.
  *Fix proposed:* Keep them out of the slice. For the full game define hesitation as choosing WAIT, answering a FORCE test with a TALK card, or a split (honouring one of two simultaneous tests). Revisionist settles only when each claim has been blocked twice or one claim is conceded; conceding lowers Coalition lean, which is the Munich price stated on the choice.

- **[minor] Fog text: the credibility sentence (line 69).** "They think you'll fold. We're 60 to 80% sure of that." A watcher cannot tell whether 60-80 is the confidence in the read or the credibility value itself, and the design rule at line 139 (hidden things spoken, never numbered) is being bent for a state rather than an event.
  *Fix proposed:* Separate the read from the confidence: "They think you'll fold" as the headline, confidence in words ("we are fairly sure" / "we are almost certain"). Reserve numeric ranges for events ("30 to 50% it is real"), not for states.

- **[minor] Fog: how intel confidence grows (lines 47, 59, 178).** "Sharpens over the game", "near certainty by day 20 with the right cards", "unknown until about day 10": if confidence is a clock, the fog is fake and run-3 players will simply wait for day 10 before committing to anything. The spec also says the doctrine is "a rule of behaviour the player can learn by watching" (line 46) yet proposes it be unknown until day 10; those are different claims and the first is the one worth keeping.
  *Fix proposed:* Confidence = the count of diagnostic events the Federation has produced (a probe on an unpledged region, a complaint about your forces, a claim named in a speech) plus INTEL cards; a Federation that has done little stays unread. Give each doctrine a visible opening tell from day 1 so the attentive player can guess before intel confirms. Cheap, honest, and it rewards reading the ticker, which is what the design wants.

- **[minor] Endings: FROZEN as a safe harbour (line 156).** FROZEN is "graded low" but is not a loss. A player on day 45 with 9 fighting and the blue bar capped can stall: offer nothing, honour nothing, wait for day 60. If FROZEN scores above WORLD WAR, stalling is the rational endgame and it is dull to watch.
  *Fix proposed:* Score FROZEN as a loss with the day as the only consolation, or have a Federation at FIGHTING for ten days auto-climb so the clock cannot be run out. Add a gate: fewer than 10% of the sensible bot's games end FROZEN.

- **[minor] Balance gates: the bots that actually threaten the design (lines 166-174).** The gates cover the passive player, the pledge-everything player, a sensible bot, five fixed hands and card uptake. They do not cover the strategies a good player will actually try: the oracle (doctrine known on day 1, the cartoon test), the silent hawk (no pledges, force on every press, exploiting "say less than you do"), the fortress (block everything, offer nothing), and the appeaser (fold everything, offer everything), which against a Paranoid should do well on the ladder and badly on credibility and Coalition lean.
  *Fix proposed:* Add the four bots with the band each must land in, per doctrine. Oracle ≤ sensible + 15 points; silent hawk and fortress must each lose to at least one doctrine in more than 60% of games; appeaser must beat the passive bot against the Paranoid and lose badly against the Opportunist.

- **[minor] The home front (lines 67-68, 109).** "When the home front holds / cracks" moves credibility, HOME cards exist, and influence comes partly from it, but there is no state, no display and no trigger defined. As written the player cannot see or move the thing that is supposedly one of three inputs to their credibility.
  *Fix proposed:* One hidden 0-100 with three spoken states (steady / restless / cracking), moved by public folds, by long FIGHTING, by loud blocks (up) and by HOME cards; shown as an occasional ticker line and one sentence in the WORLD tab. Do not build audience costs beyond this dial in the slice.

- **[minor] Slice scope: cheap versus research project.** Cheap and belonging in the slice: declared red lines with bluffs; test rate as a function of credibility; within-type jumpiness and appetite dials; multi-round tests with WAIT; the ladder fear bonus and accident chance; off-ramp content slots and the 75% cap; the ladder word on screen; one ally-acts-up template; region recovery. Research projects in disguise that would sink the slice: a general two-sided misperception process (the Federation probabilistically misreading every action through its own intel model); a domestic-politics model with opposition and elections; commitment-specific credibility weighted by intrinsic interest (the Press/Mercer debate); Bayesian inference for the Intel Director; four fully distinct doctrines with learned tells.
  *Fix proposed:* Keep misperception as the two doctrine reflexes plus the ladder-coupled false alarm; keep the home front as one dial; keep intel as event counting with a ceiling; keep the slice at two doctrines with per-run dials. Write this list into the slice section so the next draft does not drift toward the research projects while chasing realism.


## The non-gamer watcher

**Verdict.** This draft answers the review's real complaint: the Federation now thinks, nothing spreads by itself, and the Intel Director's ranged sentences ("They think you'll fold. We're 60 to 80% sure of that"), the hatched/dotted two-channel map, the white promise turning amber, the ticker's delayed consequences ("Three days ago the tankers turned back. The Sound remembers.") and choice lines like "if they fear us, this is how it starts" are the best things on the page and must survive whatever changes. But the maths does not close (no region ever stops fighting, the treaty is barred above four fighting regions while the median game is meant to pass seven, and quiet days reward handing them what they want), the player's own moves are not drawn on the board, and the stop count is back at the cadence the review killed. Over a shoulder, the watcher can see what the enemy does but cannot say who the enemy is, what the player just did, or why the blue bar moved.

- **[blocker] End state: region states, the four-region rule, the balance gates.** Region states are CALM, CONTESTED, FIGHTING and the only transitions written are downward: 'A contested region falls to fighting when it is pressed unanswered... or at once on an escalation.' Nothing ever takes a region out of FIGHTING: not a ladder step down, not an off-ramp, not 'two blocked pushes in a row'. Meanwhile 'A treaty with fighting in more than 4 regions is not offered' and the gate wants 'the war bar passes 7 of 12 in the median game' with a median treaty on day 40 to 50. Those three lines cannot all be true: the median game reaches 7 fighting regions, can never get back under 5, and so can never be offered a treaty. On screen: the blue bar reaches 100% ('At 100% they sign and the game is won, that day') while five regions burn, and either the game contradicts its own rule and signs, or the bar sits at the right-hand edge and nothing happens for twenty days until FROZEN. A watcher says 'the blue bar is full, why hasn't she won?' and nothing on the screen answers.
  *Fix proposed:* Write the downward transitions: a FIGHTING region becomes CONTESTED when the ladder steps down or when the Federation accepts a way out that names it (ticker: 'Guns quiet at Vessary'), and CONTESTED becomes CALM after a set number of quiet days; the WAR bar then falls with it and the race runs both ways. Make the four-region rule visible instead of silent: a marked notch on the TREATY bar with 'They will not sign while 5 are fighting' in the ticker the day it binds, or drop the rule and let the score count fighting regions at signing. Re-derive the gate as 'median peak 7, median at signing 4 or fewer'.

- **[blocker] Settlement rule, ladder triggers and gate 1: appeasement wins.** 'Up every day they make no gains' plus the ladder's up-triggers ('a red line crossed, a pledge that folded, the doctrine's trigger') means a player who does nothing is never punished by two of the four doctrines. A Revisionist 'has three claims and presses them whatever you do': the passive player loses three regions (WAR 3 of 12), crosses no line, folds no pledge, offers no off-ramp (so no 'read as stalling' trigger), and the ladder never climbs. From then on every day is a day with no gains, settlement rises daily, fighting is under 4, and the treaty is signed. Against a Paranoid, whose trigger is 'force near its borders', doing nothing is the brake, so the same happens after the first flashpoints fall. The gate 'a player who never pledges and never answers loses by day 40 in more than 80% of games against every doctrine' is unreachable as written, and the explanation's 'The world goes to war if you let them win' is false in the maths: letting them win is the easiest win path. A good player finds this on run two; a watcher sees a player who sits still and gets a treaty.
  *Fix proposed:* Give every doctrine the appetite rule that sentence 3 of the explanation already promises: a region taken unanswered is an up-step for all four (gains breed pushes), and a Federation above PRESSING gains settlement only from blocks, ways out and other blocs' signatures, never from quiet days. A conceded claim costs settlement for a week, not a day. Then the Revisionist's three claims are three steps up the ladder for the passive player, the Paranoid presses on when it is not blocked, and gate 1 can hold.

- **[blocker] The three-sentence explanation and the spec's own watcher sentence.** The spec's own test sentence is wrong about its own screen: 'the red side keeps pushing'. Red is FIGHTING, not the Federation; the Cap's regions are slate like everyone else's, and nothing on the map says they are the enemy (only the Republic gets a flag, on Edda Vale). A watcher will point at the first red region and say 'that's them', and be told it is an island the Federation is attacking. Sentence 1 says 'the Federation', the map says 'the Cap', the hint headline says 'Wintermast': three names for one side, none lettered on the board. And the design's declared centre ('Commitments and credibility are the core choice'; a doctrine 'the player can learn by watching') is absent from all three sentences: nothing tells the player or the watcher that the enemy has a character to be worked out, which is the thing the whole redesign exists to add.
  *Fix proposed:* Two flags: the Republic's on Edda Vale, the Federation's on Wintermast, with THE FEDERATION lettered across the Cap during the explanation. One name on screen everywhere (the Federation; Wintermast only as the place). Sentence 2: 'You lead the Republic. Work out what kind of enemy this is, promise to defend what you can hold, and keep your word when they test you.' Sentence 3: 'Give them a way to stop and they sign. Let them win, or back them into a corner, and it is war.' Then the watcher's sentence is true: 'the Federation, the flag up top, keeps pushing; she's working out what they're like and promising to defend places; the blue bar is them agreeing to stop.'

- **[major] The map: the player's forces are invisible.** 'One marker only: a ring where the Federation is testing you.' The map shows the enemy's pressure (amber, red) and the player's promises (white) but none of the player's deployments. 'Fleet to the Strait', 'Reserves called up', the brigade sent when a promise is honoured: none is drawn. Yet these are exactly the moves that cross red lines ('no Republic forces in the Sound') and set off a Paranoid. Screen moment: the player drafts a FORCE card; nothing changes on the board; a day later three regions go amber and the ticker says 'Wintermast calls the deployment an act of aggression'. The watcher cannot say 'she sent ships there and that's what set them off' because there are no ships there. The review praised the first draft's 'build that pays off, visible on the map'; this draft removed the only visible build and kept the invisible one.
  *Fix proposed:* A second marker class, still no bubbles and nothing to tap: a small white chevron (land) or anchor (sea) on any region where Republic force is deployed, drawn when the card or the honour resolves, removed on Pullback. Red-line crossings and Paranoid climbs then have a visible cause, the Director can point at it ('That anchor is what they are shouting about'), and the WORLD WAR ending can draw its chain through it.

- **[major] Pledging: the main verb is one sentence.** 'You may pledge a region' is the whole specification of the player's primary verb. Not written: how (tap a region? a button? a phone?), what it costs (nothing is listed), how many (no cap), where (a Cap region? one already fighting?), and whether every press on a pledged region is a test. 'Pledging raises its chance of being tested' implies tests are separate events, so a promised region can turn amber with no phone at all, which makes the white line meaningless to a watcher. With no cost and no cap, pledge-everything is free until tested, and against an Opportunist that 'probes every line once' every promise becomes a clock-stopping phone. Watcher moment: 'she tapped the map and a white line appeared, what does that mean?' The label PLEDGED does not answer 'pledged to whom, to do what?'
  *Fix proposed:* Tap a region, hold, PROMISE TO DEFEND. A little influence a day per live promise (the garrison), capped at three, allowed only on CALM or UNDER PRESSURE regions outside the Cap. A press on a promised region is the test, every time, and the phone fires the day it turns amber. Label the outline WE DEFEND, not PLEDGED.

- **[major] Stops per game: the count is undercounted and the cadence is the one the review killed.** The spec counts 'ten phones plus five drafts in twelve to fifteen minutes is one stop a minute', but its own lines add more. 'Every five days the Chief of Staff brings three cards' is twelve drafts in sixty days, not five (the two numbers contradict each other on the page). Phones are 'about ten a game... plus one whenever the Federation tests a pledge', and an Opportunist 'probes every line once', so three or four promises add three or four stops. The median game ends day 40 to 50, which is 8 to 10 minutes of running clock at 12 s a day: 10 phones + 8 to 10 drafts + 3 to 4 tests = 21 to 24 stops, one every 22 to 28 seconds, the cadence the review called 'turn-based with friction' (it was 24 s). At fast (4 s a day) it is one stop every 8 to 10 seconds and the fast button means nothing again. For the watcher each stop covers the map for 20 to 40 seconds of reading, so the board they are meant to 'watch and read' is hidden for roughly half the game and their narration collapses to 'she's reading something'.
  *Fix proposed:* Fix the cadence or the count: five drafts means every twelve days, or front-loaded on days 1, 6, 11, 16, 21 like a shop that closes. Fold the drafts into the phones as the spec's own fallback says: the Chief of Staff's phone carries the three cards as its choices. Make promise tests the only unscheduled stop. Target eleven to thirteen stops in a median game (one every 40 to 45 s of running clock) and show the next stop's day in the top bar ('next call: day 14') so the watcher can see the rhythm.

- **[major] The ring, the clock and what 'hesitation' means.** Line 14: 'tapping [the ring] opens the decision and stops the clock', so the clock runs until tapped. Line 114: 'plus one [phone] whenever the Federation tests a pledge. The clock stops', so it stops on its own. These disagree, and the gap is the whole Gambler doctrine, which 'raises the stakes whenever you hesitate': if the clock stops on every decision, hesitation cannot exist in game time; if the ring sits on a running clock, nothing says what an untapped ring costs, whether it expires, or whether ignoring it counts as a fold. Watcher moment: a ring pulses on Shoal, the player is reading the CABINET tab, days tick past. 'What's she waiting for? Is that bad?' No one can say, including the player.
  *Fix proposed:* Pick one and write it. Recommended: a test is a phone and stops the clock; the ring marks where and stays on the map for the rest of that day as the record. Then define hesitation for the Gambler as something visible: choosing the half-measure option on a test, or taking more than a set number of real seconds on the phone, with the Director saying so ('They are watching how long we take').

- **[major] The first week with inherited promises.** The player starts with 'nought to two pledges... alliance obligations you did not choose', while the explanation says 'Draw your lines'. Day 1 on screen: two white outlines the player did not draw and no sentence about them. The first test comes before any information: the doctrine is 'unknown until about day 10', red lines are at 40%, and an Opportunist probes early, so the hardest choice of the run is blind. If the inherited promise is in the Sound and a red line is 'no Republic forces in the Sound', honouring it (what the explanation told the player to do) is the crossing, and the step up arrives with a headline the player cannot connect to their choice: the honour line warns of the Paranoid ('if they fear us, this is how it starts') but not of the red line. There is no way to drop an inherited promise before it is tested; the only exits are honour or fold. And the WORLD WAR ending says 'the line you drew was tested and you folded' about a line the player inherited; the player will say 'I didn't draw it.'
  *Fix proposed:* Day 1 ticker and a first phone from the Old Ambassador: 'Two promises came with the job: Shoal and Vessary. They know we made them.' Draw the outlines in while it is said. Give the Director a day-1 read as two candidates ('Greedy or scared; we'll know by day ten'). Print the red-line suspicion on any honour choice that would put force where intel suspects a line ('40% this is one of their lines'). Add a third verb: WITHDRAW a promise quietly before it is tested (they believe you a little less; a fold costs far more). Ending text: 'the promise you inherited at Shoal was tested and you backed down.'

- **[major] Words on screen.** The jargon is the document's and several of its words would be lettered on the phone. Doctrine, ladder, settlement, pledge, off-ramp, credibility, contested, honour/fold, and the doctrine names (Revisionist above all, Opportunist too) are words a non-gamer has to ask about. 'Fighting' is used three ways (a region state, a ladder step, and what the WAR bar counts). The ladder step, the single most important enemy state (it 'sets how many regions the Federation presses at once and how fast a pressed region falls'), lives inside THE OTHER SIDE tab. 'Settlement' to most people is a village or a payout. 'Fold' is poker. The explanation itself uses the plain word 'back' ('decide which to back when they are tested'), then the buttons would say honour and fold. A choice line reading 'Credibility falls' is a design term, not a sentence a watcher can repeat.
  *Fix proposed:* SETTLEMENT bar → TREATY (the bar is them agreeing to stop; 'their willingness' goes in the WORLD tab). PLEDGED → WE DEFEND. Honour/fold → BACK IT / BACK DOWN. CONTESTED → UNDER PRESSURE. Off-ramp → 'a way out'. Credibility → 'they believe us more' / 'they believe us less' on the choice lines. Doctrine → 'what they're like', with names that are sentences: 'Takes what's unguarded', 'Scared of us', 'Wants three places', 'Pushes when we blink'. The ladder → 'They are:' and one word under the WAR bar on the main screen: TESTING / PUSHING / MASSING / FIGHTING / ALL-OUT. Keep 'fighting' for regions only.

- **[major] Why the blue bar moved: invisible movers.** Settlement goes 'down when the Coalition leans their way', up 'when another bloc signs (isolation)', down 'when they are humiliated'. None of these has a place on the map: Farsouth's lean ('a swing... if it leans all the way it joins the Federation's side and the Sound falls faster') is not drawn, other blocs' signatures are not drawn, and 'why settlement moved last' is tucked into THE OTHER SIDE tab. The ticker's remit ('the Federation's moves, your moves' effects, the world's reaction') does not mention the bar. Screen moment: the blue bar drops twelve points overnight with nothing new amber; the watcher asks why and the player has to open a tab to find out, and may not know either.
  *Fix proposed:* Every settlement move of more than a couple of points gets a ticker line in plain words the same day ('Farsouth edges towards Wintermast: they're less willing'; 'Three quiet days: they're more willing'). Draw Farsouth's lean as a tint on its bloc from blue-grey to red-grey, and a small signature mark on each bloc that has signed. Keep the last three ticker lines readable in the WORLD tab so a watcher who looked away can catch up.

- **[major] The blue bar is an exact readout of a hidden mind.** 'Hidden things are spoken, never numbered, except as the Intel Director's ranges', yet the Federation's willingness to settle, the most hidden thing in the design, is an exact live percentage that moves the day something happens. A good player uses it as a free, instant, perfect probe: play Hotline on day 6 and watch the bar. Up means Paranoid (reassurance is its brake); down means Revisionist ('off-ramps that offer nothing about the claims' are read as stalling). One card, one day, and the doctrine is narrowed to two, faster and surer than the Intel Director. The fog the review asked for leaks out through the one number on the screen.
  *Fix proposed:* Make the bar intel's estimate, not the truth: it moves in daily steps with a two- or three-day lag (the design already has this idea: 'consequences can land days later') and its leading edge is soft to show the band. The true value stays hidden until it reaches 100 and they sign. The race stays readable to a watcher; the probe stops working.

- **[major] Four doctrines, four scripts, and intel that removes the fog on schedule.** The doctrine table prints the counter beside each doctrine ('What stops it'), intel names the doctrine by about day 10, and 'Spymaster + Intercepts' knows the red lines by day 10. INTEL and HOME cards have no printed cost or risk (only 'every FORCE card prints its risk... every TALK card prints its cost'), so they are strictly good and the sensible draft is always INTEL first, then the listed counter. After four wins the player has met every script. This is the review's 'solved in two or three runs' with the number raised to four; the hidden red lines are the only thing left to vary, and intel removes those on a timetable too.
  *Fix proposed:* Print a risk on INTEL as well: the Spymaster can be wrong (a 'near-certain' red line is wrong one time in six and the Director says so), and Intercepts can be caught ('they know we're listening': a step up for a Paranoid). Mix doctrines: a primary and a secondary trait (Scared-and-pushes-when-we-blink; Greedy-with-three-places), so four becomes twelve behaviours and the counter is never one move. Make setup guarantee a conflict: every world contains at least one collision between the doctrine's brake and an inherited promise or a claim (a Paranoid plus an inherited promise in the Sound), so the right answer costs something every run.

- **[minor] Names and the 'six places' promise.** The draft says the watcher 'learns six places, not thirty', but its own examples use Shoal, Vessary, Lintel, Wintermast, Edda Vale, the Cap, Highmarch, the Scatter, the Sound, Farsouth and 'the Strait' (not one of the five blocs, so an eleventh place). Wintermast appears only inside a headline and is never introduced. 'Claims' are a Revisionist trait ('Has three claims') yet setup 'draws... the claims' for every doctrine, so three named places are learned even when they mean nothing.
  *Fix proposed:* Letter on the map only the Federation's claims (when the doctrine has them), the player's promises and the two capitals; everything else is 'a Sound port' or 'an island in the Scatter' in the ticker. Make 'the Strait' a feature of the Sound or cut it. Give every doctrine a meaning for the claims (the Opportunist probes them first; the Paranoid fears force near them), or draw claims only for the Revisionist.

- **[minor] The false alarm and honest ranges.** 'Early warning shows six launches. Nothing else confirms it. We are 30 to 50% sure it is real.' The spec never says what happens if it is real. If real means launches, a player who waits loses on a coin the Director called 30 to 50%: the ending is a dice roll and the watcher sees it as one ('she waited and it just ended'). And nothing guarantees the ranges are honest; if the true chance can fall outside the range the Director gives, the one instrument the player is told to trust is lying.
  *Fix proposed:* Bound the real case: below GENERAL WAR a 'real' alarm is a test or an exercise, so the cost is in the response (a step up if you answer with force, 'they believe us less' if you panic in public), never an instant loss. Write the calibration rule into the content validator: the hidden probability must lie inside the Director's stated range on every template.

- **[minor] Synergies that delete the doubt.** 'Hotline + Back-channel (your off-ramps always land)' removes the uncertainty about ways out, and 'Fleet to the Strait + a pledge in the Scatter (a test there resolves without a fight)' removes the test.
