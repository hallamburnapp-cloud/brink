# The study behind the design

Research on Balatro and on what the owner likes, an audit of this repository, five designs, three judges, the ranking.

A dev.to synthesis cites the same interview as '7M+ units'. PocketGamer.biz reports Playstack's 7.1m units across its catalogue in 2024. _(source: https://app.podwise.ai/dashboard/episodes/5362601 ; https://dev.to/prince_t_research/the-road-to-7-million-how-balatro-actually-reached-the-world-18mb ; https://www.pocketgamer.biz/playstack-revenue-surged-52-in-h1-2025-to-307m; confidence low)_

AppMagic later estimated about 3.1M mobile downloads and $21.3M mobile revenue (period not stated) and over $9.3M net by early 2025.** Raijin tracker page; Wikipedia and PocketGamer.biz citing AppMagic; Pocket Tactics (updated 9 Jul 2026) citing AppMagic; Game World Observer. _(source: https://raijin.gg/app/2379780/Balatro ; https://www.pocketgamer.biz/balatro-nears-44m-on-mobile-amid-a-sudden-spending-surge ; https://www.pockettactics.com/premium-mobile-games-increase ; https://gameworldobserver.com/?p=27715; confidence medium)_
- **Steam player counts: peak concurrent players were about 37,977 on 10 Mar 2024 and 38,506 on 27 Dec 2024. The all-time peak was 43,905 on 5 Jan 2025. In mid-2026 the game still averaged about 7,500 daily concurrent players on Steam, with a snapshot of about 8,400 online.** Game World Observer citing SteamDB; tracker.gg lists 43,881; Raijin's July 2026 average is about 7,500. SteamPulse's figure of 17,123 conflicts with the others and is likely an error. _(source: https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/12/28/balatro-record-peak-ccu-38-5k-steam-crazy-december ; https://tracker.gg/population/steam/2379780 ; https://raijin.gg/app/2379780/Balatro; confidence medium)_
- **Reception: 98% positive on Steam ('Overwhelmingly Positive') from 105,041 English reviews, and about 190k reviews across all languages. Metacritic scores are PC 90, Xbox Series X/S 95, PS5 90 and Switch 90. 100% of critics on OpenCritic recommend it.** Steam store page (fetched Oct 2026); SteamPulse gives 189,944 total reviews at 98%; Wikipedia gives the review aggregates. _(source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/2379780/Balatro/ ; https://www.steampulse.org/game/2379780 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro; confidence high)_
- **Awards. The Game Awards 2024: won Best Independent Game, Best Debut Indie Game and Best Mobile Game, and was nominated for Game of the Year and Best Game Direction. It was the first game by a solo creator to be nominated for GOTY. GDC Awards: Game of the Year, Best Debut, Best Design and the Innovation Award. DICE: Outstanding Achievement for an Independent Game, Mobile Game of the Year, and Strategy/Simulation Game of the Year. BAFTA: Debut Game. Golden Joysticks: Best Indie and Breakthrough. Apple Design Award: Delight and Fun.** Wikipedia awards table; NPR's TGA winners list; The Globe and Mail on the solo-creator GOTY nomination. _(source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro ; https://www.npr.org/2024/12/13/g-s1-38032/2024-game-awards-winners-reveals-astro-bot ; https://theglobeandmail.com/arts/article-inside-the-stunning-success-of-balatro-the-video-game-sensation-made; confidence high)_
- **Origin: a solo developer (LocalThunk, Canada) wrote it in Lua with LÖVE, starting 13 Dec 2021. It began as an online version of the shedding game Big Two ('Big Cheat'). He pivoted to a single-player score-attack game after watching Northernlion stream Luck be a Landlord: 'that whole score attack concept really fuelled the direction I wanted to go.' CHIP x MULT was in the very first prototype, before Jokers existed.** Timeline blog: 'The game had the CHIP X MULT mechanic already'. The prototype had no Jokers, no blind selection, and random enhancements as upgrades. _(source: https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro; confidence high)_
- **Build order of the systems: Jokers and boss blinds came in Feb 2022; an economy and something close to the final structure by Jun 2022; vouchers by Feb 2023; ascension-style stakes were borrowed from Slay the Spire in Oct 2023. He tested and cut a separate reroll currency and a 'golden seal'. The plan was 120 Jokers, raised to 150 after Playstack misspoke ('instead of correcting them I just made 30 more Jokers').** Timeline blog entries; LocalThunk's post on X. _(source: https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh ; https://x.com/LocalThunk/status/1851133286363574286; confidence high)_
- **He deliberately avoided playing other roguelikes while designing: 'I wanted to make mistakes, I wanted to reinvent the wheel.' He first played Slay the Spire in May 2023, about 18 months in, while working on controller support.** Timeline blog and Rogueliker interview (7 Mar 2024). _(source: https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh ; https://rogueliker.com/balatro-interview/; confidence high)_
- **The poker theme was added afterwards as onboarding, not as the core: 'The poker theme was slapped on top of the game I was already creating mainly as an onboarding tool.' Balatro has 'almost no mechanical similarities to poker apart from the hand rankings.' He called it 'my modern indie take on solitaire with a poker coat of paint.'** Direct quotes in the Rogueliker interview. _(source: https://rogueliker.com/balatro-interview/; confidence high)_
- **Intended feel is solitaire: low stakes, no player character, no health, no enemies. It was meant to be 'evergreen, comforting, and enjoyable in a very low-stakes way', a 'couple of runs' on a lazy afternoon, and 'jazz solitaire'. He says stakes, unlocks, achievements and challenges exist to push players into mechanics they would otherwise miss, not to pad playtime.** LocalThunk blog post 'Solitaire' (26 Feb 2025). _(source: https://localthunk.com/blog/solitaire; confidence high)_
- **He dislikes 'gamery' theming: 'I dislike the gamery language of fantasy and combat that seem to be way overrepresented in video games... I wanted to lean on different verbiage and visuals.'** TouchArcade interview, 18 Mar 2024. _(source: https://toucharcade.com/?p=320511; confidence high)_
- **Balance philosophy: tune by feel and keep tuning. He only intervenes when a card 'cannibalises all the adjacent strategies around it', and 'almost all balance changes can be made by changing a number.' He admits 'a lot of randomness, possibly too much, but the metastrategy for Balatro is around mitigating risk.' Community players drove the balancing: 'the demo and beta players knew more about how to play the game properly than I did.'** Rogueliker interview; timeline blog. _(source: https://rogueliker.com/balatro-interview/ ; https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh; confidence high)_
- **LocalThunk used a '1 hour retention metric' as his rubric for whether the hook worked, and says it improved after the first public demo. Playstack's 2023 press release said closed-beta players had a median playtime of over ten hours.** GameDiscoverCo newsletter (18 Mar 2024). The beta playtime is reported via a Playstack press release found in search results. _(source: https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-balatro-hit-1-million-sales-in ; https://gamespress.com/Playstack-Goes-All-In-on-Developer-LocalThunks-Balatro-in-Latest-Publi; confidence medium)_
- **Before Balatro, LocalThunk spent years on an unshipped land-grab strategy game, which he calls 'the template' for his hobby. Inven Global describes it as inspired by Risk and Europa Universalis. The Balatro designer's roots are in map-based strategy.** 'Bad Grades' blog post (20 Feb 2026); Inven Global anniversary coverage. _(source: https://localthunk.com/blog/bad-grades ; https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/20243/balatro-2nd-anniversary-update-localthunk-shares-development-story-confirms-version-11-progress; confidence medium)_
- **Run structure: 8 antes, each with three blinds (Small at 1x the base target, Big at 1.5x, Boss usually 2x). Small and Big blinds can be skipped for a Tag reward; Boss blinds cannot. A run is won by beating the Showdown boss at ante 8, so a full run is up to 24 rounds. Endless mode continues after that.** Balatro Wiki 'Blinds and Antes'; Wikipedia gameplay section. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Blinds_and_Antes ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro; confidence high)_
- **Base score targets per ante on the base stake are 300, 800, 2,000, 5,000, 11,000, 20,000, 35,000 and 50,000, a 167x rise over 8 antes. Higher stakes steepen the curve: Green reaches 100,000 at ante 8 and Purple reaches 200,000.** Balatro Wiki table of base chip requirements. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Blinds_and_Antes; confidence high)_
- **Per-round resources: 4 hands (plays), 3 discards, a hand size of 8, up to 5 cards played per hand, and up to 5 cards per discard. There are 2 consumable slots and 5 Joker slots by default.** Balatro Wiki pages on Hands, Discards, Hand Size, Consumables and Jokers. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Hands ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Discards ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Hand_Size ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Consumables ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Jokers; confidence high)_
- **Score = Chips x Mult. Each hand type sets a base, and harder hands pay far more: High Card 5x1, Pair 10x2, Two Pair 20x2, Three of a Kind 30x3, Straight 30x4, Flush 35x4, Full House 40x4, Four of a Kind 60x7, Straight Flush 100x8. Secret hands go higher: Five of a Kind 120x12, Flush House 140x14, Flush Five 160x16. Only the scoring cards add their chips (number cards = face value, face cards = 10, Ace = 11). The gap between choices is multiples (Pair base 20 vs Straight Flush base 800), not a few percent.** Balatro Wiki 'Poker Hands' and 'Chips' pages. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Poker_Hands ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Chips; confidence high)_
- **Planet cards permanently level a hand type for the rest of the run (e.g. Pair +15 chips and +1 mult per level; Straight +30 and +3; Straight Flush +40 and +4). The Black Hole spectral card upgrades every hand. Levelling is what turns a hand into a build identity ('my Flush run').** Balatro Wiki 'Poker Hands' table; Spectral Cards page. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Poker_Hands ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Spectral_Cards; confidence high)_
- **Scoring order is strict and visible. Boss effect first, then 'on played' Jokers. Each scoring card then resolves left to right: its chips, then enhancement, seal and edition, then 'on scored' Jokers, then any retriggers. Held-in-hand effects come next, then Jokers left to right (Foil and Holo editions, then independent Jokers, then Polychrome). Order changes the result: +4 Mult placed before an X2 gives 40 x ((4+4) x 2) = 640, while placed after it gives 40 x ((4 x 2) + 4) = 480.** Balatro Wiki 'Activation sequence' and 'Mult' pages. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Activation_sequence ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Mult; confidence high)_
- **Jokers: 150 in total (61 common, 64 uncommon, 20 rare, 5 legendary), with 105 available on a first run and 45 unlocked through play. Shop rarity odds are 70%, 25% and 5%; legendaries come only from The Soul. Effect types are +Chips, +Mult, XMult, Chips and Mult, retrigger, economy, and 'effect' (rule-benders). Editions add a second layer: Foil +50 chips, Holographic +10 mult, Polychrome x1.5 mult, Negative +1 slot.** Examples: Joker +4 Mult; Sly Joker +50 chips if the hand contains a Pair; Cavendish X3; Hack retriggers each played 2-5; Dusk retriggers all cards on the final hand; Ride the Bus gains +1 Mult per consecutive hand without a face card (scaling); Golden Joker $4 per round (economy); Four Fingers makes Flushes and Straights from 4 cards; Pareidolia makes all cards face cards (rule-benders). _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Jokers ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Mult; confidence high)_
- **Synergies emerge because simple effects multiply through each other and through deck composition. Baron gives X1.5 Mult per King held in hand, so 2 Kings give 2.25x. Mime retriggers held cards. Steel Kings with a Red Seal plus Mime and a Blueprint copy reach about 25.6x, and doubled Baron and Mime reach about 130x. Blueprint 'copies ability of Joker to the right', so position on the rail matters.** Balatro Wiki Baron page (compounding worked out per trigger) and Blueprint page. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Baron ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Blueprint; confidence high)_
- **Shop: 2 random card slots, 2 booster-pack slots and 1 voucher slot. Card-slot weights are Joker 20, Tarot 4, Planet 4. Prices: Jokers $1-6 common, $4-8 uncommon, $7-10 rare; Tarot or Planet $3; packs $4/$6/$8; voucher $10. Rerolls start at $5, rise $1 each time, and reset in the next shop. Selling returns floor(cost/2). The first shop of a run always contains a Joker pack.** Balatro Wiki 'The Shop'. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/The_Shop; confidence high)_
- **Economy: runs start with $4. Blind rewards are $3, $4 and $5 ($8 for the Showdown), plus $1 per unused hand. Interest pays $1 per $5 held, capped at $5 (so $25 maxes it out). The Seed Money and Money Tree vouchers raise the cap to $10 and $20. This makes saving versus spending a constant decision.** Balatro Wiki 'Money' and 'Vouchers'. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Money ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Vouchers; confidence high)_
- **Consumables and permanent upgrades: 22 Tarots edit the deck (enhance cards, change suits or ranks, create Jokers or Planets, make money). 18 Spectrals are high-risk and high-reward: Wraith creates a Rare Joker but sets money to $0, Hex adds Polychrome to one Joker and destroys the rest, Ankh copies one Joker and destroys the rest, The Soul creates a Legendary. 32 Vouchers come as 16 base/upgrade pairs at $10 each, e.g. +1 hand, +1 discard, +1 shop slot, Antimatter +1 Joker slot, Hieroglyph -1 Ante at the cost of -1 hand.** Balatro Wiki Tarot, Spectral and Voucher pages. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Tarot_Cards ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Spectral_Cards ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Vouchers; confidence high)_
- **Boss blinds: 23 regular bosses plus 5 Showdown bosses. Each changes one rule: The Mouth allows only one hand type, The Psychic forces 5-card plays, The Wall sets a 4x target, Violet Vessel 6x, The Needle 1x but only one hand. Bosses are what keep a single dominant build in check.** Balatro Wiki 'Blinds and Antes'; cardgamer.com beginner guide. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Blinds_and_Antes ; https://cardgamer.com/games/getting-started-with-balatro/; confidence high)_
- **Meta progression: 15 decks, each a rule change. Examples: Red +1 discard; Black +1 Joker slot but -1 hand; Painted +2 hand size but -1 Joker slot; Plasma balances Chips and Mult but doubles targets; Erratic randomises the deck. Decks unlock by discovering collection items or winning. Each deck has 8 cumulative stakes: Red (no Small blind reward), Green (faster scaling), Black (Eternal Jokers), Blue (-1 discard), Purple (faster scaling), Orange (Perishable Jokers), Gold (Rental Jokers).** Balatro Wiki Decks and Stakes pages. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Decks ; https://balatrowiki.org/w/Stakes; confidence high)_
- **Endless mode's targets grow faster than exponential: ante 9 110,000; ante 10 560,000; ante 11 7.2M; ante 12 300M; ante 13 4.7e10; ante 16 8.6e20. At ante 39 the target exceeds the double-precision limit and becomes 'naneinf'. Crashing the number format is effectively the game's true ending.** Balatro Wiki table and notes; PC Gamer headline: 'Balatro, like Tetris before it, can only be finished by scoring so high it crashes the game'. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Blinds_and_Antes ; https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/turns-out-that-balatro-like-tetris-before-it-can-only-be-finished-by-scoring-so-high-it-crashes-the-game/; confidence high)_
- **How long a run takes in real time is not documented officially. Guides and review sites estimate 15-30, 30-40 or 30-60 minutes. A first win often takes many hours: one writer took 33 hours.** Aggregated estimates from guide and review sites; cardgamer.com. _(source: https://www.twoaveragegamers.com/short-roguelikes-finish-run-before-bedtime/ ; https://cardgamer.com/games/getting-started-with-balatro/; confidence low)_
- **Why non-poker players can read the score: there is one score against one visible target, and only two numbers (chips and mult) are multiplied. When you select cards, the game names the hand you have made. A 'Run Info' screen lists every hand and its level. The hand ladder is a familiar ranking that the poker theme teaches for free, and the store page hook is 'play illegal poker hands'. The flame appears while you are still choosing cards if the hand will beat the target, and grows the more it overshoots.** Rogueliker interview (theme as an onboarding tool). Steam store short description. Steam community answers say flames appear 'when the chipsXmult scored (or about to be scored) is greater than the Blind's chips' and grow 'the more the Blind is exceeded', and that 'the game will also tell you what hand you have when you select your cards' (player reports, not official). _(source: https://rogueliker.com/balatro-interview/ ; https://store.steampowered.com/app/2379780/Balatro/ ; https://steamcommunity.com/app/2379780/discussions/0/604143740583441031 ; https://steamcommunity.com/app/2379780/discussions/0/7336985412171719293; confidence medium)_
- **Balatro deliberately does NOT preview a hand's final score. LocalThunk: 'My personal belief is that the game is more fun when you set up your Rube Goldberg machine and watch it go.' He also argues a preview would clutter the UI, make players brute-force every combination, and turn a game about 'vibing with cards' into a spreadsheet. Mark Brown (GMTK) calls this a 'cursed problem': hardcore players pull out calculators, spreadsheets and overlay sites.** GMTK Substack, 2 Apr 2024. LocalThunk did add a deck-peek feature after testers tracked cards by hand, but refused a score preview as encroaching on 'the DNA of the game'. _(source: https://gmtk.substack.com/p/balatros-cursed-design-problem; confidence high)_
- **The juice. After you press play, the numbers tick up with escalating sound. Each card and Joker 'steps forward in turn' to add its points. A big multiplier sets the score on fire and the fire 'burns hotter and hotter with each multiplication'. The flame was a friend's suggestion that LocalThunk first disliked, then said 'really fit perfectly'.** GMTK description of the scoring sequence; LocalThunk timeline blog on the flame. _(source: https://gmtk.substack.com/p/balatros-cursed-design-problem ; https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh; confidence high)_
- **Audio and colour: the soundtrack is by Luis Clemente ('LouisF'), hired on Fiverr in March 2023, the only money LocalThunk planned to spend. Reviewers single out 'crunchy, deeply satisfying' sound effects. Fan analyses describe chips in blue and mult in red, rising pitch per scored card, and rolling counters. Those details come from fan analysis, not the developer.** Wikipedia and the timeline blog for the composer; a gamegeeker review for the SFX; Blake Crosley's fan breakdown for colour and pitch (its specifics are unsourced). _(source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro ; https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh ; https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro; confidence medium)_
- **Why it feels like 'breaking the game': XMult multiplies the existing multiplier, so stacked XMult, retriggers and copies grow exponentially while targets grow on a fixed curve. Late-game scores dwarf the targets, and endless mode lets players push until the number format fails. I found no direct LocalThunk quote saying he 'allowed' broken builds. His stated policy is to nerf only cards that 'cannibalise' other strategies, which leaves room for overpowered combos.** Balatro Wiki Mult and Baron pages for the compounding; Rogueliker for the balance policy; Frank Lantz on 'astronomical scores' that overwhelm the scoring function. _(source: https://balatrowiki.org/w/Mult ; https://rogueliker.com/balatro-interview/ ; https://franklantz.substack.com/p/playing-balatro; confidence medium)_
- **Criticism, randomness and agency. LocalThunk admits 'possibly too much' randomness. Frank Lantz (Donkeyspace, 29 Mar 2024) writes that 'the outcome of any one individual game session is only loosely correlated to the strategic choices you made', calls it 'a slot machine', and notes that excess score is ignored ('it doesn't affect the outcome at all') and that win rate is not tracked. Some reviewers call the loop repetitive or say there is usually one obvious best hand.** Lantz essay; Rogueliker interview; reviews from Turn Based Lovers and Movies Games and Tech; Steam negative reviews. _(source: https://franklantz.substack.com/p/playing-balatro ; https://rogueliker.com/balatro-interview/ ; https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/balatro-a-poker-roguelite-impressions/ ; https://moviesgamesandtech.com/2024/03/03/review-balatro/; confidence high)_
- **Criticism, gambling imagery. PEGI moved the game from 3+ to 18+ for 'prominent gambling imagery', and it was briefly pulled from sale in some territories. An appeal brought it down to 12+ in Feb 2025. YouTube age-restricted many Balatro videos in Mar 2025. The App Store lists 'simulated gambling'. LocalThunk wrote into his will that the IP 'may never be sold or licensed to any gambling company or casino' (6 Aug 2024).** Wikipedia PEGI section; Push Square report of the X post; App Store listing. _(source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro ; https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2024/08/balatro-creator-ensures-ip-wont-ever-be-used-for-gambling ; https://apps.apple.com/us/app/balatro/id6502453075; confidence high)_
- **Criticism, retention. One analysis comparing Steam concurrent players from March to August 2024 found Balatro's retention weaker than The Binding of Isaac's and Slay the Spire's. This is a secondary analysis.** GIGAZINE summary of Brothers in Gaming's analysis. _(source: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250207-balatro-successful-launch-strategy/; confidence low)_
- **How it spread before launch: wishlists went from 48 (end of May 2023) to 2,440 (end of June) after a Dan Gheesling stream, to 28,661 (end of July) after Northernlion played the demo, to 208,401 on launch day. Press outreach failed ('not going to generate clicks'). Playstack built a hit list of streamers who played Slay the Spire, Super Auto Pets and Isaac, and the pitch that worked was 'it's a Poker roguelike, play for five minutes and come back to me in three hours.'** LocalThunk timeline wishlist table; Playstack's Wout van Halderen at MIGS 2025, reported by Thumbsticks; GDC 2025 talk listing. _(source: https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh ; https://www.thumbsticks.com/poker-pixels-and-persuasion-the-marketing-bets-behind-balatro/ ; https://gdcvault.com/play/1035036/-Balatro-Turning-Low-Resolution; confidence high)_
- **Demo and community: a content-limited demo with 'no round limit on play' replaced the round-capped demo and reached 100k Steam downloads. A 24-hour streamer tournament ('Jimbo's Invitational') peaked at 5,600 viewers. Streamers sent viewers to a Discord where players shared combos and ran tournaments ('we let them be loud'). PC was an estimated 80% of the first million, and the US (33%) and China (26%) led Steam sales to 1 Mar 2024.** GameDiscoverCo newsletter (18 Mar 2024); Thumbsticks MIGS report. _(source: https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-balatro-hit-1-million-sales-in ; https://www.thumbsticks.com/poker-pixels-and-persuasion-the-marketing-bets-behind-balatro/; confidence medium)_
- **The second wave: in December 2024, Game Awards nominations and wins, two sales events, a Markiplier video and holiday buying drove a record player peak. Sales went from 3.5M to 5M within weeks. ### Lessons for our design

- Show exactly two numbers multiplied, against one target. Balatro's whole score readout is Chips x Mult = score versus a blind target, and that is why non-poker players can read it. BRINK needs one base number and one multiplier, colour-coded, against a single crisis target in big type. Do not show meters, bars or secondary resources during the scoring moment. Use our own words, never 'chips', 'mult', 'blind', 'ante' or 'joker'.
- The map must be the hand. A binary choice cannot form a combo; Balatro works because you pick up to 5 of 8 dealt cards and the pattern they form sets the base values. Direct translation: each round a set of map regions are 'live' (dealt, say 8). The player selects up to 5, and the geographic shape they form is the 'hand': a connected chain, a ring or encirclement, a full bloc, a coastline, a corridor across a sea, two separate clusters, and so on. Each selected region also adds its own value, the way card ranks add chips. Luck be a Landlord, Balatro's own inspiration, shows that adjacency on a board can carry this whole loop.
- Use geographic patterns that need no teaching, the way poker hands needed none. Adjacency, lines, rings and 'all of one bloc' read instantly on a map. Keep a ladder of about 9-12 named patterns with base values spread by multiples: Balatro runs from High Card 5x1 to Straight Flush 100x8, a 160x spread. The difference between choices must be 5x to 50x, never 21 vs 23. Name the pattern and show its base the moment the player selects regions. Add 2-3 secret patterns (Balatro's Five of a Kind and Flush Five) that only appear once the map has been modified.
- Turn deck-building into map-building, so the map visibly reacts. Balatro's Tarots edit the deck: change suits, enhance cards, add seals, destroy cards. Our equivalents edit the board: flip a region's bloc (suit), fortify a region (enhancement), lay a cable or rail that makes two distant regions count as adjacent (rule-bender), annex or erase a region. Every edit should stay visible on the war-room map for the rest of the run. That makes the map a record of your build, which is the owner's 'map that reacts'.
- Pieces are the jokers: a rail of about 5 war-room assets that fire left to right. Cover the full Balatro range: flat base, flat multiplier, multiplier-times (x1.5 to x3), retriggers ('score each coastal region twice'), scaling ('gains +1 each crisis you clear without using a bloc'), economy, copiers ('copies the asset to its right'), and rule-benders ('4-region rings count as 5'; 'every region counts as a capital'). Let the player reorder the rail, because order matters (Balatro's 640 vs 480 example). Use rarity odds near 70/25/5 with legendaries from a rare source only. Launch with roughly 60, since Balatro shipped 150 and 105 of those were available on a first run.
- Add a permanent levelling track per pattern, Balatro's Planets: for example a 'briefing' that adds base and multiplier to 'Ring' for the rest of the run. Levels give a run its identity ('this is my Corridor run'), and the shop has to keep offering them.
- Copy the shape of the economy, not its skin. Money per crisis cleared plus $1 per unused order. Interest at 1 per 5 held, capped, with vouchers that raise the cap. A shop of 2 random assets, 2 packs and 1 permanent upgrade. Rerolls that start cheap and rise by 1 each time, resetting each shop. Selling at half price. Interest is the cheapest way to create 'my nerve made this happen': saving, rerolling or buying now is a real decision every shop.
- Per-round constraint is where the decisions live: about 4 plays and 3 redeploys (discards) per crisis, with a hand of about 8 live regions. Pressure comes from limited plays against a target, not from text choices.
- Target curve and structure: 8 escalation stages, each with three crises (minor at 1x, major at 1.5x, summit or boss at about 2x). The addendum's 300 / 900 / 2,500 / 7,000 / 20,000 has a similar shape to Balatro's 300 to 50,000 over 8 antes. Let minor and major crises be skipped for a reward tag. Give each boss crisis one rule twist ('blackout: no sea regions score'; 'summit: only one pattern type allowed') so a single dominant build cannot coast. Add harder stakes per starting doctrine (Balatro's 8 stakes x 15 decks) for long-tail replay.
- Let the game break. Multiplier-times effects, retriggers and copiers must be able to compound (Baron and Mime reach about 130x), and endless mode should scale faster than exponentially until the number display gives up. Balatro's naneinf ending became a talking point. Nerf only a piece that 'cannibalises adjacent strategies' (LocalThunk's rule), never one that is simply strong. Keep a local best score only, with no named leaderboards (Online Safety Act constraint).
- Make the scoring moment the best moment in the game. It drives Balatro's appeal, and its flame was a late addition. On our map: each selected region lights up in sequence with a rising tone, the pattern line draws across the map, each asset on the rail steps forward and flashes its contribution, the base and multiplier counters tick up, then slam together. The score catches fire when it will clear the target and burns hotter the more it overshoots. Scale screen shake and sound to the result. Prototype and tune this sequence before writing most of the content.
- Choose the information model deliberately. Balatro shows the hand name and the 'will this clear?' flame but hides the final number, so the player watches the machine run; GMTK calls that a 'cursed problem' because hardcore players use calculators. For a broad phone audience, show the pattern, its base, and a clear/no-clear indicator before committing. Keep the final total as the reveal. Consider an exact preview as a late unlock or accessibility option rather than the default.
- Answer Balatro's known criticisms on purpose. Excess score is ignored: convert overkill into something visible, such as bonus money or a lasting map effect. Win rate is not tracked: show local stats. Too much randomness: give risk-mitigation tools (redeploys, rerolls, a peek at upcoming live regions), because LocalThunk says the metastrategy is managing risk. Gambling imagery cost Balatro a PEGI 18 and a 'simulated gambling' App Store label; a war-room theme avoids that, but the fiction must stay non-graphic and fictional to keep ratings low.
- Treat the theme as onboarding. LocalThunk 'slapped' poker on as an onboarding tool and avoided 'gamery' combat language. The war room should do the same job: a command table, a lit fictional world map, pins, cables and a ticking clock, which anyone grasps in seconds. Avoid military jargon and combat verbs. Score with influence and stabilisation language rather than destruction, so 'numbers go up' never reads as 'more war'. Keep the solitaire feel: calm, a couple of runs and done, with instant restart.
- Market lessons. Premium single price with no IAP works across platforms ($14.99 PC and console, $9.99 mobile, an Apple Arcade edition), and mobile delivered about half of Balatro's 5M by Jan 2025. A generous demo with no round limit beat trailers and press. Find streamers who already play roguelike builders and pitch 'play for five minutes, come back in three hours'. Use LocalThunk's '1 hour retention' as the playtest rubric. Write a one-line hook as sharp as 'play illegal poker hands'.
- Do not clone. No playing cards, poker hands, jesters or jokers, chips/mult wording, blinds or antes, CRT pixel styling, or mascot. Borrow only the mechanics: a two-number product against a rising target, a slot rail of ordered modifiers, permanent pattern levels, board editing, an interest economy, boss rule twists, and stakes. The hand becomes map geometry, the deck becomes the fictional world map, and the table becomes the war room.

### Lessons for our design

- Make the map the hand. Every spatial score-attack hit uses a SMALL, FIXED, fully visible board: Luck be a Landlord's 20 slots, Into the Breach's 8x8, Gambonanza's 'tiny board', a backpack grid, an island. Use a fictional world of roughly 12–20 regions that fits one landscape phone screen with no pan or zoom. Each region is a slot, and borders and sea lanes are the adjacency wires that make combos. The map 'reacts' because your pieces physically sit on it, light up and change it.
- One number against one target, shown in one place. Balatro has chips x mult against a blind, Luck be a Landlord has rent due, Nubby has a quota, CloverPit has debt. Show one running total (e.g. LEVERAGE) and one target (the flashpoint). Show no meters or bars; the only other numbers on screen are the pop-ups each piece makes where it sits.
- Score by SWEEPING the map, so the map is the Rube Goldberg machine. On 'Execute', resolve regions in a fixed, visible order, as a pulse travelling along the links or a sweep line across the table. Each piece pops '+base' or 'xmult' on its own region, the total climbs in one place, then slams against the target, with shake and sound scaled to the result. This sequential reveal is also the tutorial.
- Preview locally, hide the total. Islanders shows a building's points before you place it, and that keeps placement legible. Balatro hides the turn total to keep the suspense. Do both: while dragging a piece, show its immediate effect on that region and its neighbours ('+30 here', 'x2 with Port next door'). Never pre-compute the whole turn, and never put two near-identical totals side by side; v0.2's '21 vs 23' was the opposite of this.
- Give every decision a multiplicative swing. Placement should change outcomes by x2, x3, retrigger or chain, not +2. Build archetypes around spatial rules a player can count at a glance: adjacency, clusters of the same type, connected chains, coastal or corner regions, a capital. Dorfromantik found counting goals the only ones that were 'intuitive and easy-to-communicate'.
- Keep agency on the board every round. Ballionaire was criticised because players 'can only hit the drop button and watch'. The player's turn should be placing, moving, upgrading or sacrificing pieces on regions before Execute. Borrow Into the Breach's telegraphing: the next flashpoint marks on the map which regions it hits or which ones score double, so placement has a reason that differs each round. That is the 'boss blind'.
- Let the shop sell the BOARD as well as pieces. Gambonanza sells board tiles (gold squares, hook squares) next to pieces and rule-benders; Islanders unlocks new islands. Region upgrades ('Fortified: pieces here retrigger', 'Hub: links to every coast') make the map visibly change over a run, which serves the owner's 'world map that reacts' anchor.
- Make it breakable on purpose. Nubby's developer found 'breaking the game was the best part' and made integer overflow the goal; LocalThunk wants players able to break Balatro but keep meaningful choices. Include retrigger, scaling and propagating-mult pieces and legendaries that rewrite adjacency, plus endless mode with abbreviated huge numbers.
- Clarity beats cool. Into the Breach would 'sacrifice cool ideas for the sake of clarity every time' and cut about 60% of its design, including city-building and research trees. Cut any layer that is not 'pieces on regions make the number', such as economy, diplomacy meters or advisor text. Each piece needs a short rule, an icon and a distinct visual pop at its location; Ballionaire's visual cues were 'not as instantly understandable as the sound cues'.
- Keep the tone low-stakes and non-graphic, as Balatro does: no health, no enemies, 'as low stakes as a crossword'. The war room abstracts the world into tokens on a table. Tension lives in the number versus the brink (the target), never in casualties.
- War-room look: a dark command table with a glowing 'big board' map (DEFCON), where colour carries meaning, not decoration (Frozen Synapse). Draw minimal geography: coasts, region borders and a few labels, as Mini Metro keeps only rivers and stations. Make pieces read as physical tokens or diorama objects you would keep on a shelf (Hitman GO), with tactile in-fiction touches such as a lever for Execute or a stamped target (Highfleet, Papers, Please). Use them sparingly, because dense in-fiction UI reads as daunting. Let the scoring sweep generate the music, as Mini Metro's audio does.
- Design for the phone first, not as a port. Plan landscape first: maps are wide, and Balatro and Plague Inc. run landscape. Keep all regions visible, the piece tray along the bottom, large touch targets, drag-to-place with haptics, and secondary info collapsible. Into the Breach mobile had to hide information and enlarge buttons. Twilight Struggle shows what happens when a card covers the map.
- Avoid gambling imagery (cards, chips, slots, dice-as-betting). It keeps us clear of Balatro's look and names, and of the PEGI 18 and Google Play simulated-gambling problems that hit Balatro and Luck be a Landlord.
- Positioning: no Balatro-like I could find uses a world map. That makes the map a differentiator in a crowded subgenre (5.1% of 2025 roguelike deckbuilders reached 1,000 reviews; Bramble Royale failed for lack of 'a marketable hook'). But the winners all used an object players already understand (slot machine, plinko, chess, coin pusher, dice), so the map must read as a GAME BOARD in one glance, with pieces on regions and a number climbing.
- Store page line one names the object and promises to break it, in the pattern of 'The poker roguelike...' and 'a tactical roguelike that sets chess pieces on a tiny board...'. For example: 'The war-room roguelike: place pieces on a world map, chain them into combos, and push one number past the brink.'
- Trailer and first three seconds: open on the map mid-sweep, with regions lighting in sequence, pop-ups on regions and the total slamming past the target. No logos, no cinematic, readable muted, HUD visible. Build a deliberate six-second loop, because Steam auto-cuts six-second microtrailers from the first trailer and Nubby grew on 7–10 second clips. DEFCON's opener (dark map plus one red countdown) and Luck be a Landlord's (the target first) are the models.
- Go to market through a demo and streamers, with no named leaderboards. Balatro's demo had no round limit (100k downloads in three months); Nubby, Balatro and Gambonanza were all lifted by streamers and 'crazy builds'. Offer seeded daily runs and shareable result images and text so the social layer lives off-platform, which stays compliant with the UK Online Safety Act.
- Price and platform precedent: Steam $4.99–$14.99 (Nubby $4.99, CloverPit $9.99, Balatro and Gambonanza $14.99); mobile premium $5.99–$9.99 with no IAP (Gambonanza, Balatro). Mobile took about 35% of Gambonanza's early units, but premium is only 4% of mobile downloads, so mobile is a strong second channel, not the whole plan.
- Meta layer: unlocks, stake levels, challenges and a compendium as LocalThunk's 'checklist to work through', with instant restart. Tune on one-hour retention, as LocalThunk did, and cut any mechanic that doesn't raise it.

### Build new

### What v0.2 got wrong in code and UI

- The two choices of a card could not differ much by construction. computeLeverage (/home/user/brink/src/engine/leverage.ts) scores both sides against the same state from before the choice, so mult and the escalation multiplier are shared. Measured over 400 heuristic runs of content-crisis (21,314 cards): both sides had the identical mult on 89.7% of cards. The median gap between the two totals was 14 points, or 2.1% of the act's target (p90 7.2%). On 28.8% of cards the bigger total was less than 1.25x the smaller. The owner's '12x1x1.76 = 21 vs 13x1x1.76 = 23' is the typical case, not an outlier (screenshot /home/user/brink/docs/playtests/dove-PT-DOVE-1-week-three.png).
- The base number was not a design decision. deriveBase (leverage.ts) computes it from the size of the choice's meter effects, clamped to 4-40, with x1.35 for escalatory tags and x0.9 for diplomatic ones. DECISIONS D-061 says this was done so the 300+ cards written before the scoring existed would 'score sensibly'. The scoring was bolted onto a deck written as a Reigns meter game, so no choice was ever written to be a scoring move.
- The bigger number pointed at losing. When the two sides differ in escalation, the higher-scoring side is the more escalatory one 83.1% of the time (14,342 cards measured), and escalation 100 ends the run in nuclear war. 64% of all leverage scored came from the escalation multiplier (the ESC_CURVE in leverage.ts), not from the build. The visible score therefore rewarded walking toward the loss condition, and the heuristic bot ended 83% of runs in nuclear war (BALANCE.md iteration 9). Following the numbers was a trap, which reads as 'nothing I did seemed to matter'.
- There were two win conditions, and the one on screen was not the real one. Missing the target never ended a run: settleAnte (/home/user/brink/src/engine/run.ts about lines 1097-1120) only plays a 'bluff called' card (screenshot dove-PT-DOVE-1-bluff-called.png: 429/450, the run continues). The real way to win was surviving ~70 cards with four meters off both edges and escalation below 100. The heuristic bot won 6.8% of runs. The intro (Intro.tsx at commit 8cb4886^) taught four rules covering meters, a base x mult x escalation formula, hidden values and odds. That is the 'no idea how to win' complaint.
- The build hardly existed for half the run and was invisible when it did. The heuristic bot held a median of 0 pieces in Week One, 1 in Week Two and 2 in Week Three (probe), so most choices showed 'x1' mult. On the run screen (/home/user/brink/src/ui/screens/Run.tsx at 8cb4886^) the held pieces appear only inside a menu opened by tapping the grey 'THE REPUBLIC · WEEK THREE' label. Balatro keeps the jokers on screen at all times. Piece growth was reported as a bottom toast ('X grows: +0.5 mult'), not on the piece.
- The tally hid who did what. Tally.tsx animates only the aggregates BASE x MULT x ESC = SCORED. The breakdown already carries baseTerms, multAddTerms, multMultTerms and retriggerTerms with each piece's name, but they are never rendered, so the player never saw 'Hawk General +8, Madman x3' fire. With a median total of about 38, most tallies ran for 0.8 s with no shake (doShake only fires at 400 or more in /home/user/brink/src/ui/store.ts). Each card then queued up to two more modal overlays (odds roll 1.5-4 s, accident 2.8 s) plus banners. The juice existed but had almost nothing to celebrate.
- The choice preview spoiled the payoff and invited trivial arithmetic. ChoiceButtons (/home/user/brink/src/ui/components/Card.tsx) prints the full final formula and total under both prose answers in 10 px mono ('12x1x1.76 = 21'). The total was precomputed by design (DECISIONS D-060, 'no hidden surprises in the tally'), so there was no reveal. The 1.76 was the danger meter in disguise and the 'x1' admitted the build did nothing. Next to two sentences of policy the numbers asked the player to compare 21 against 23.
- The target had no visible path. At Week Three the ante bar read 0 / 1,200 for a 16-card act while the choices were worth 21-23 (16 x 23 = 368). The only routes to the target were pushing escalation toward x5-x12 or buying pieces. The screen explained neither, and the bar was always red until met.
- There was too much on one screen and too much text. One phone column (max-w-[480px] in App.tsx) carried: seat and act, political capital, score, the ante bar, five bars where four lose at both edges and one at the top, a four-part hidden-values line in words, a countdown timer, an accident banner with a percentage, a 3-5 sentence card with a silhouette portrait, two formula buttons, order and charge chips, and '← drag →'. Banners at top-[22%] covered the meter labels and the hidden line (both screenshots). The Hawk General's rule deliberately hid escalation costs as '?'.
- The pieces were written as rule bundles, not jokers. Each of the 64 has about 3.5 modifiers spread over meters, odds, card weights, timers, drifts and rules, with mechanics strings of 125 characters on average and up to 288. The shop (/home/user/brink/src/ui/screens/Shop.tsx) listed them as paragraphs, with rarity in 9 px text, selling by tapping name chips and tag removal through a <select>. Only 8 pieces have xmult, 3 retrigger and 9 scale. The resolver deliberately makes order irrelevant (D-010, D-060), so arranging the build was never a skill.
- Shops came too rarely to feel like building. Acts were 14-16 cards with one mid-act and one end-of-act shop, about 6.5 shops in roughly 70 cards (BALANCE.md), against Balatro's shop after every blind. Capital ran 4-9 per ante, prices were 3-8 and rerolls 2+, so a typical visit bought one piece.
- The 'break the game' promise never happened. Target T5 (3% of heuristic runs reach 100x the final target) failed at 0 of 20,000 (iteration 9: median 5,080, p99 36,737, max 533,791). The random bot's median score (1,843) beat the greedy bot's (821) because the score tracked danger, not decisions (/home/user/brink/docs/sim-report-iter9.md).
- The world never reacted. Outcomes were bar deltas and paragraphs. Nothing on the screen was a place, so a decision had nothing visible to change, which is the one world element the owner has now kept.
- It looked unfinished: silhouette placeholder portraits (/home/user/brink/src/ui/art/Portrait.tsx), system fonts, a CRT overlay and cards of Le Carré-register prose with invented proper nouns.

### Risks

- Sunk-cost pull. Adapting /home/user/brink/src/engine/run.ts, the 451 cards or the Card/Meters UI would bring back a card game with five bars. Extract only the shop, the ante and reward functions, the term attribution, the rules combiner and the RNG, and retire the rest: night.ts, Bookings, flashpoints, accidents, warnings, the meters, the endings, Card.tsx, Meters.tsx, Dials.tsx, RollOverlay and AccidentOverlay.
- The formation mechanic is the whole bet and it is untested. A placement on a region graph can be ambiguous (which formation did I make?) or combinatorially murky. It needs a small fixed set of named shapes, live detection as the selection changes, and a paper or greybox prototype plus simulator proof (a choice-spread target) before art. If the best play is not obviously several times the median, '21 vs 23' returns in a new form.
- Clone exposure. The mechanics can be learned from Balatro, but its vocabulary cannot ship: the brief's own words 'chips', 'mult', 'ante', 'blind', 'joker', 'planet' and 'tarot' are all Balatro UI terms, and a poker-hand table or the joker card look would read as a reskin (and invite App Store 4.1 copycat problems). It needs our own terms and an original card and table look.
- Phone legibility. A war room with a full map, a piece rail, a score, a target, counters and a hand of 7-8 commands on a 390x844 portrait screen is tight. Regions under 44 pt need zoom, which breaks the at-a-glance read. Cap the region count (~24-36) and prove the layout with a painted screen before code.
- The map painters cannot be the runtime renderer. Measured at about 9 s per paint (off-switch, 390x844, DPR 2), they are untyped IIFE scripts with fixed seeds and a phone-frame coordinate box. Their mask uses jittered-Voronoi edges and must be baked crisp or taps will hit the wrong region. Final art is still likely commissioned (the prior study estimated £5k-13k).
- Number scale and determinism. Endless Balatro-style scores pass 1e10, and float xmult chains can round differently across browser engines, which would break a shared daily seed. Score in integers or fixed point with rounding at each step, show e-notation, and add a cross-engine hash test.
- Balance is harder with exponential scoring. v0.2 never produced a broken run (T5: 0%). The new engine needs the breaker bot from day one, plus targets per stake and per archetype, or the 'numbers go up' promise fails again quietly.
- Tone and rating. Piece names such as Madman Theory, The Button, Launch on Warning, Second Strike and Deadman Switch, plus nuclear flavour, raise age ratings and store scrutiny. The owner kept only 'a world map that reacts', not the slide toward WWIII. Keep it non-graphic and decide whether the nuclear-named pieces stay.
- Fictional geography. Region names from the mockups (Rimeholt, Glassharbour, Tallowmere...) need a gazetteer and trademark check. FORBIDDEN_WORDS in /home/user/brink/src/content/validate.ts has only about 60 terms.
- The channels are still unbuilt. Tauri and Capacitor are configuration only (no native projects, no packages, no icons), the Worker's wrangler.toml has REPLACE placeholders, and three separate purchase paths (Stripe, store billing, Steam) each need their own tests.
- Owner fit. Four directions chosen by the team were rejected, so the owner should see a painted war-room screen and a greybox playable round of the formation-and-cascade loop before full production. The 'map that reacts' must visibly come from scoring (regions lighting up as they score), not decorative tint.

## The five designs

### BRINK (store subtitle: The War-Room Roguelike)

> Pin operations onto a world map, link them into chains and rings, and watch one number explode past the crisis.

**Fantasy.** It is midnight in the war room of a made-up world: five continents around an Inner Sea, always one bad night away from disaster. You are the duty chief at the map table.

Crises arrive as red pins on the map, each with a number: the LEVERAGE you need before everyone stands down. You answer by pinning operations onto the map: fleets, trade deals, envoys, intelligence and airlifts. Shapes score. Two neighbours is a start. A chain down a coastline is better. A ring around a capital is better still. Behind you, a desk of assets (a tide table, a hotline, a lighthouse model, a ledger) multiplies every deploy.

Get through eight hours of crises and dawn comes up over a calm map. Then do it again with a stranger desk.

You are not a president and there is no war. Nobody is hurt and nothing is destroyed. The number measures how much calm you made, so a bigger number always means a safer world. The only tension is the number against the target and the clock on the wall. In feel it is closer to solitaire than to a war game.

The run is one night. Hour 1 is 00:00. You win at 08:00, when the desk lamp gives way to grey-gold daylight on the table. Endless mode is "Overtime".

**The board.** ONE FIXED FICTIONAL WORLD: 30 REGIONS ON 5 CONTINENTS ROUND AN INNER SEA
The world is drawn square so the whole map fits a phone screen (390 x 400 pt) with no pan and no zoom, and fits the centre of a landscape screen. There is no real geography, and every name is fictional (a gazetteer and trademark check is still owed; see risks). Capitals are marked with a star.

- VESSARY (west, 9 regions): Quillon, Glassharbour, Ashkettle, Vessary (capital), Saltreach, Morrow Downs, Edda Vale, Fennick, Larkspire.
- HOLLIN (north, 6): Rimeholt, Coldspit, Pale Isles, Hollin Cap (capital), Wenmoor, Hollow Ness.
- TALLOWMERE (east, 7): Harrowmark, Kelpmouth, Lintel, Tallowmere (capital), Dunmarrow, Sundry Reach, Brackwater.
- THE SHOALS (centre, 3 islands): Tern Isle, Gull Rock, Lantern Key (capital).
- BRINDLE (south, 5): Brindle Coast, Marram, Southfold, Hearth (capital), Copperfall.

REGION PROPERTIES (each region has at most three)
- Continent (5).
- COAST (23 regions) or INLAND (7).
- CAPITAL (5).
- INNER SEA coast: 14 regions face the central sea (Glassharbour, Saltreach, Fennick, Wenmoor, Hollow Ness, Lintel, Sundry Reach, the 3 Shoals, Brindle Coast, Marram). This matters for one asset, Ferry Service.

ADJACENCY ("touching")
Two regions touch if they share a land border or are joined by a dotted SEA LANE. There are 10 lanes:
- Rimeholt-Quillon, Hollow Ness-Harrowmark, Glassharbour-Tern Isle, Lintel-Gull Rock, Lantern Key-Marram, Larkspire-Brindle Coast and Brackwater-Southfold join different continents.
- Tern-Gull, Gull-Lantern and Tern-Lantern join the Shoals to each other.

Every region has at most ONE lane to another continent. That guarantees no connected group of 5 can touch all five continents on the starting map, which is what makes the WORLD CHAIN pattern a secret you have to build towards.

Sample adjacency (used in the scoring examples):
- Vessary (capital) touches Quillon, Glassharbour, Ashkettle, Saltreach, Morrow Downs and Edda Vale.
- Quillon touches Ashkettle, Glassharbour, Vessary and, by lane, Rimeholt.
- Glassharbour touches Quillon, Vessary, Saltreach and, by lane, Tern Isle.
- Saltreach touches Glassharbour, Vessary, Morrow Downs and Fennick.
- Morrow Downs touches Vessary, Saltreach, Fennick and Edda Vale.
- Edda Vale touches Vessary, Morrow Downs, Fennick and Larkspire.
- Fennick touches Saltreach, Morrow Downs, Edda Vale and Larkspire.
- Larkspire touches Fennick, Edda Vale and, by lane, Brindle Coast.
- Tallowmere (capital, inland) touches exactly Harrowmark, Lintel, Dunmarrow and Sundry Reach.
- Kelpmouth touches Harrowmark and Dunmarrow.
There are about 52 touching pairs in all, so a random hand of 8 usually holds 2-3 touching pairs.

THE DECK IS THE MAP
You start with 34 operation slips:
- One slip per region (30). Each has a TYPE and a STRENGTH: capitals and great ports 8-9, large regions 6-7, small coasts 4-5, islands 2-3.
- Types: 8 Fleet, 8 Trade, 7 Intel and 7 Envoy. Every capital is Envoy.
- Plus 4 AIR slips (strength 3), which have no home region.
Tap the deck counter and the map becomes the deck view: one dot on each region for every slip of it still in the deck. Copies show as two dots and retired slips leave the region hollow.

PERMANENT MARKS (the map is your build)
These are added in the shop and stay on the map for the whole run:
- FORTIFIED (hatched border): +15 Strength each time an op scores there.
- BEACON (small lamp): +6 Momentum each time an op scores there.
- RELAY (double ring): ops there score twice.
- MINT (gold dot): +2 Funds each time an op scores there.
- HUB (spokes): touches every region on its continent.
- STRONGHOLD (tower): any number of ops may share it.
- CABLE (a brass wire drawn between any two regions): they touch from then on.
- DARK (grey, at higher difficulty only): the region cannot take ops.
- SETTLED (small cosmetic stamp): a crisis was defused here.
A region holds one mark at a time; Cables do not count as marks.

READING IT ON A PHONE
- Regions average about 50 x 50 pt. The three islands are drawn at about 30 pt, but their tap areas are baked as 48 pt halos in the hit mask.
- Pins are 28 pt with 44 pt hit circles.
- Labels are minimal: continent names in small caps, plus capital stars. A region's name appears only when you select its slip or long-press the region.
- Colour carries meaning: pale land, ink-blue sea, a lighter Inner Sea and dotted lanes. Type colours appear only on pins and on lit regions.

**The verb.** DEPLOY. Each turn you choose up to 5 of the 8 operation slips in your hand and pin them on the map. Every slip belongs to a place, so tapping it drops a brass pin on that region. You can tap the glowing pins on the map directly, which is the fast way, or tap the slips in the hand.

AIR slips have no home. You DRAG them onto any empty region, or onto a Stronghold. That is the one free placement in a turn, and it is usually how you close the gap in a ring, add the fifth region to a chain or reach the hot spot.

As you pick, a red thread joins your pins in the shape they make. The pattern name and its base appear at once (for example "CHAIN 30 × 3"). Pins that would extend the shape glow slightly brighter. Then you press DEPLOY (4 per crisis) and watch it score.

The alternative is RECALL (3 per crisis): pick up to 5 slips, send them back and draw replacements. It is how you fish for the region that finishes a ring.

Between deploys you can drag assets along the desk rail to change their order, and order matters. Between crises, in the shop, you edit the map itself:
- Drag a Map Order onto a region to Fortify it or make it a Relay.
- Drag a Cable between two regions.
- Retire, copy or recolour slips.

On PC you can click pins, or press keys 1-8 to select, Space to deploy and R to recall. On a phone everything can be done with one thumb.

**Scoring.** THE FORMULA
LEVERAGE = STRENGTH (cyan) × MOMENTUM (red-orange), rounded down. Each deploy's Leverage is added to the crisis total, and the crisis needs total ≥ target.

ORDER (strict, and visible in the animation)
1. PATTERN: Strength and Momentum start at the pattern's base plus its levels.
2. SUMMIT rule, if it changes bases (e.g. The Cold Shoulder halves them).
3. SWEEP: the scoring ops resolve in map order, west to east (ties go north first). For each op:
   a. Add its Strength.
   b. Apply its region's mark (Fortified +15 Strength, Beacon +6 Momentum, Mint +2 Funds).
   c. Desk assets that say "each ... op" fire, left to right.
   d. If the op scores again (hot spot, Relay, a retrigger asset), repeat a-c.
4. HAND: assets that count slips still in your hand.
5. DESK: every other asset effect, left to right, as printed (+Strength, +Momentum, ×Momentum).
6. SLAM: Strength × Momentum.

NUMBER RULES
- Strength is a whole number.
- Momentum keeps one decimal, and after every × it is rounded down to one decimal.
- Assets that grow update only after the slam.
- The engine works in integers (Momentum is stored in tenths), so a seed gives identical scores on every browser and device.
- Display: full digits up to 999,999, then 1.24M / 619.8M / 4.70B / 1.20T, then 3.1e15 style, and ∞ beyond about 1.8e308.

PREVIEW (what you see before you commit)
- The pattern name and its base, e.g. "RING 100 × 9".
- The assets that will fire lift slightly on the rail.
- The target stamp warms if this deploy will defuse the crisis.
The total itself is never shown in advance; it is the reveal. An exact forecast is an accessibility option, unlocked after the first win.

EXAMPLE 1. EARLY GAME: Hour 1, Minor crisis, target 300, empty desk
Hand: Edda Vale (Trade 6), Morrow Downs (Envoy 7), Saltreach (Fleet 5), Ashkettle (Intel 5), Coldspit (Trade 4), Kelpmouth (Trade 4), Dunmarrow (Trade 5), Hearth (Envoy 9).
Deploy Edda Vale, Morrow Downs and Saltreach (Edda Vale touches Morrow Downs, which touches Saltreach): CHAIN.
- Pattern: Strength 30, Momentum 3.
- Sweep: Edda Vale +6 → 36. Morrow Downs +7 → 43. Saltreach +5 → 48.
- Desk: empty.
- Slam: 48 × 3 = 144. The bar reads 144 / 300.
The other options in the same hand:
- LONE (Hearth alone): (5 + 9) × 1 = 14.
- LINK (Edda Vale + Morrow Downs): (12 + 6 + 7) × 2 = 50.
- TWO LINKS (Edda Vale-Morrow Downs + Kelpmouth-Dunmarrow): (20 + 6 + 7 + 4 + 5) × 2 = 84.
So the choices are 14 / 50 / 84 / 144, a 10× spread.
The same deploy one crisis later with THE NIGHT SHIFT (+4 Momentum) on the desk: Strength 48; Momentum 3 + 4 = 7; 48 × 7 = 336. That beats 300 in one deploy (ONE-SHOT +2 Funds).

WHAT COULD MAKE IT FALL FLAT (honestly)
1. Poker hands come pre-learned by hundreds of millions of people; our patterns do not. Neighbours, chains and rings are intuitive, but the 12-step ladder has to be learned. If it doesn't stick, players will deploy whatever the thread happens to show.
2. Slips are tied to regions, so a scattered hand can feel like nothing to do (Lone and Link only). That would be "21 vs 23" again in a new form. Air slips, recalls and the hot spot exist to prevent it, and the simulator must prove it.
3. Too much freedom could also kill the decision. If Air or Ferry Service make Rings and Spreads routine, every hand has one obvious answer.
4. A sweep across a map may feel slower and less punchy than Balatro's cards stepping forward. The cascade must be prototyped and tuned before any content is written.
5. Coasts are 23 of 30 regions, so a Fleet coast build may dominate. Balance has to watch how often each piece appears in winning builds.
6. The theme could read as cold or militaristic to the broad audience Balatro reached. Tone, palette and the "defuse" framing carry a lot of weight.
7. Balatro itself is criticised as too random and "a slot machine". We inherit that and must provide risk tools: recalls, the Map Lamp upgrade, Retire and local win-rate stats.

### BRINK (working title; fallback "Night Lines", because a 2011 video game called "Brink" may block the trademark)

> Connect glowing cities on a world map into lines and loops, stack assets on your desk, and watch one number blow past the brink. (Store line one: "The route-drawing roguelike: link cities across a war-room map, chain them into combos, and break the numbers.")

**Fantasy.** You work the night shift at the big map table in a war room. The world on the table is fictional. Five continents, thirty cities, and every night the pressure rises a little more. You don't move armies. You run lines: envoys, ferries, cables and airlifts, strung like lit thread between cities that are awake tonight. Every line you send pulls the world back from the brink, and the number for that pull is your LEVERAGE.

Each crisis puts a PRESSURE number on the board. Your job is to beat it in four lines. Between crises you spend Funds at the briefing table. You buy assets (a harbour master, a switchboard, an old ambassador) that sit on your desk and fire on every line. You buy orders that redraw the map itself: fortify a city, open a new sea lane, move a border. By the end of a run the table is covered in your work: thick worn lanes, stamped cities, brass marks, a network that is yours. The numbers on it have gone silly.

The tone is calm and non-graphic, the solitaire mood of Balatro. There are no units, weapons, casualties or real places. The only danger is a red haze of pressure at the edge of the map. If it wins, the screen says "The world tipped" and you start again in one tap.

**The board.** A fictional world of 5 continents, 30 cities and 53 links, drawn as a backlit plotting table at night. The cities are the hand and the links are the rules for how cards in that hand combine.

Each CITY has three things:
- a continent, which is obvious because it is a separate landmass;
- a value from 2 to 10, printed on its pin when lit, which is the Reach it adds;
- at most one kind: Capital ★ (one per continent), Port ⚓ (on a sea lane) or Hub ✈ (on an air corridor).

Each continent's values sum to 30.

THE CAP (north strip): Rimeholt ★10, Hollin Cap ✈6, Pale Isles ⚓5, Northwick Spit ⚓4, Frostmere 3, Skerrow 2. Seven land links form a long chain with two short cuts, so this is the long-line coast.

PHONE READ. The world is drawn in a near-square frame (390x400 pt in portrait), so the same map works in portrait and landscape without panning or zooming. Cities sit at least 60 px apart and each has a 44 pt touch circle. Lit cities show a large glowing pin with the value on it and the name underneath. Dim cities are small brass dots with no label. **The verb.** TAP THE LIT CITIES THAT JOIN UP, THEN SEND THE LINE.

1. Each crisis, 8 cities light up on the map. They are dealt from your deck of city pins, which starts with one pin per city. The lit cities are your hand, and they sit on the map rather than in a tray.
2. Tap up to 5 lit cities, or swipe through them. If they can be joined along the map's links, with each stop linked to the next, the game draws the line itself. If they can also close back on themselves, it closes the loop. It then names the shape with its base, for example "TRIANGLE 30 × 4". You choose a set and the game finds the route, the way Balatro spots a straight whatever order you pick the cards in. The pulse starts from the first city you tapped, which matters for advanced players.
3. Press SEND to score the line, or press SWAP to put the selected cities out and light new ones from your deck. If the selection doesn't join up, the readout says "Doesn't join" and only SWAP works.

Sent cities go dim for the rest of the crisis and the hand refills to 8. Every link a line uses gains 1 traffic for good. Two cities can be linked by land, by a ferry or sea lane, or by an air corridor, so routes run through straits, airports and island chains.

Why tracing on the map and not a hand of segment cards: a tray of cards on a phone covers the map (Twilight Struggle's problem), and it puts the decision somewhere other than the world. Here the whole decision happens on the map, and every mechanic is something you can see there: adjacency, crossing water, closing a loop, staying on one continent.

**Scoring.** LEVERAGE = REACH (blue) × SWAY (orange). Reach is a whole number. Sway is kept to one decimal and rounded down after every × step. Totals are exact integers (BigInt), so a daily seed scores identically on every browser.

ORDER OF SCORING:
0. The Flashpoint rule applies, if there is one (for example, sea lanes closed).
1. Shape base: Reach and Sway are set to the shape's values at its current level.
2. THE PULSE runs along the line, starting from the first stop. At each STOP:
   - add the city's value to Reach;
   - apply its marks (Fortified +20 Reach, Beacon +4 Sway, Flare ×2 Sway);
   - fire the desk assets tagged [pulse] that care about this stop, left to right.
   A retrigger (Twin pin, Echo Seal, Relay Station, The Switchboard) runs that whole stop step again.
   Then the LINK to the next stop: add its traffic to Reach, then fire the [pulse] assets about that link (sea lane, air corridor, land). Ferry Service repeats sea-lane steps. A loop scores its closing link too.
3. The assets tagged [after] fire once, left to right on the desk.
4. SLAM: Leverage = Reach × Sway, added to the crisis total.
5. Each link the line used gains +1 traffic for later lines.

The preview before you commit shows only the shape name, its base, and a "HOLDS" glow on SEND if this line will clear the crisis. The final total is the reveal.

### BRINK

> Join the dots on a glowing world map to calm a crisis. Every pattern you pin multiplies, and the numbers get ridiculous.
Store page line one: "The war-room roguelike: pin regions on a world map, join them into patterns, and push one number past the brink."

**Fantasy.** You run the night desk of a war room on the worst night a fictional world has had. The room is dark. In the middle is a back-lit glass plotting table showing a world of five blocs. Crises flare up across it every hour, from 22:00 until dawn. You do not send armies. You push pins into the map: envoys, aid, trade offers, broadcasts, quiet phone calls. Pins that touch join up with a taut thread. Joined pins form patterns (a Chain, a Front, a Network, a ring that Surrounds a region), and patterns multiply into LEVERAGE, your grip on the night. Push Leverage past a crisis's target and the flashpoint on the map cools from red to blue. Hold the world together until 06:00 and the sun comes up over the map.

Over the night your desk fills with odd, well-loved objects: a red telephone, a photocopier, a foghorn, a paper crown, a golden pin. Each one quietly bends the arithmetic. You also redraw the world itself. You string cables across oceans, persuade regions to switch blocs, merge two provinces into one, promote a fishing town to capital, and stamp beacons and relays onto the map. By dawn the world on the table is the one you built, and the screenshot of it is your run.

Tone: calm, wry, low stakes, like a solitaire game played at 3 a.m. Nobody is hurt and nothing explodes. When you fail, the world "tips over the brink", which you see as the map's lights going out one region at a time. The numbers measure how steady the world is, not how much damage you did.

**The board.** THE BIG BOARD: one fictional world of 30 regions in 5 blocs of 6. Each bloc starts as one continent. The map is drawn inside a nearly square frame (about 1 : 1.03), so it fills a portrait phone at full width with no pan and no zoom.

Each region has:
- Bloc: a colour plus a glyph, so colour is never the only signal. Highmarch is teal with a circle. The Cap is sky blue with a triangle. The Scatter is gold with a diamond. Sundry is coral with a square. Farsouth is violet with a hexagon.
- Weight, 2 to 10: a printed number. It is added to Influence when that region scores, the way a card's rank does.
- Port (anchor icon), on 14 regions. A port is any region with a sea lane.
- Capital (star icon): one per bloc, always weight 10.

The starting world (weight, P = port, * = capital):
- HIGHMARCH (west): Rimeholt 3, Vessary 5P, Quillon 4, Edda Vale 10*, Ashkettle 6P, Morrow Downs 7P
- THE CAP (north): Northwake 2P, Hollin 10*, Glassharbour 6, Wenmoor 4, Frostmere 3, Kellmark 8P
- THE SCATTER (central islands joined by causeways): Tern Isle 2P, Gullrest 3, Lantern Key 10*, Shoal 4P, Driftholm 5, Pebbleby 6P
- SUNDRY (east): Kelpmouth 4P, Tallowmere 10*, Dunmarrow 5, Brackwater 8, Lintel 6P, Orrinth 7P
- FARSOUTH (south): Iceward 3, Brindle Coast 7P, Saltwhite 2, Marram 10*, Hearth 4, Floe End 8P

Routes: 45 land borders, plus 7 dashed sea lanes: Vessary–Northwake, Morrow Downs–Tern Isle, Ashkettle–Brindle Coast, Kellmark–Kelpmouth, Shoal–Lintel, Pebbleby–Marram and Orrinth–Floe End. That makes 52 joins. Each region has 3.5 neighbours on average (minimum 2, maximum 5). The whole adjacency rule is one sentence: regions that share a border or a dashed sea lane are JOINED. Players can later add cables, which also join regions.

No sea lane chains into another, because no region has two lanes. So on the starting map you cannot join five pins across five blocs. That is what keeps the secret pattern "Around the World" locked until you edit the map.

The map is the deck. The 30 regions are your "cards". Each crisis the map lights 8 of them at random, and editing regions (stamps, recolouring, merging, retiring) is your deck-building.

How it reads on a phone: in portrait the map gets 390 x 404 pt. The average region is about 5,200 pt², roughly 72 x 72 pt. A small island gets a minimum 44 pt hit circle, and a tap snaps to the nearest lit region centre. Unlit regions show only their glyph and weight. Lit regions also show their name in 9 pt. Holding a finger on any region shows its name, weight, stamp and neighbours.

Fiction check: every name is invented and no real country or state appears. The names come from the v0.2 mockup list, adjusted. All of them still need a gazetteer and trademark check, because several echo tiny real places. Later updates can add other worlds (different maps), each of which plays like a new deck.

**The verb.** PIN, then SWEEP.

During a crisis:
1. Eight regions are lit, glowing warm white on the dark table.
2. Tap lit regions to push a pin into them, up to 5 pins. Tap a pin again to lift it. Dragging a pin from the pin box works too.
3. As soon as two pins touch, a thread snaps tight between them. The readout under the map names the best pattern your pins make and shows its two base numbers, for example "FRONT 50 x 4". Pins that are not part of the pattern turn grey ("loose").
4. Then use the same selection with one of two buttons:
   - SWEEP: score these pins. A scan line crosses the map from west to east and every scoring region fires in turn.
   - SWAP: dim these regions and light new ones. This is how you hunt for a better shape.

Between crises (at the Briefing):
- Drag assets along the Desk rail to change their order. They fire left to right, so order is a skill.
- Buy and sell.
- Tap an order, then tap regions on the map to stamp, recolour, merge or cable them.

PC: click to pin; Space = Sweep, S = Swap. Controller and Steam Deck: the d-pad hops between lit regions from west to east, A = pin.

In short: you choose where to push influence, and the shape your pins make is the move.

**Scoring.** FORMULA: LEVERAGE = INFLUENCE (blue) x MOMENTUM (amber). Each sweep's Leverage adds to the crisis total, which is measured against one target.

EXACT ORDER (it is also the animation order):
1. Summit twist, if any (for example, Recount halves the pattern base).
2. Pattern base: Influence and Momentum start at the pattern's base plus its levels.
3. PIN SWEEP, west to east by region centre (ties go north first). For each scoring pin:
   a) add the region's weight to Influence, plus 10 for each Calm laurel;
   b) apply the region's stamp: Fortified +20 Influence, Beacon +4 Momentum, Relay x1.5 Momentum, Treasury +3 capital;
   c) fire every Desk asset that has a "when scored" rule, left to right;
   d) repeat a) to c) once for each retrigger (flashpoint, Echo stamp, Megaphone, Foghorn, Carbon Paper and so on).
4. STANDBY SWEEP, west to east, over lit regions you did not pin: Reserve stamps (x1.5 Momentum) and standby assets such as Standing Orders.
5. DESK SWEEP, left to right: each asset's per-sweep rule (+Influence, +Momentum, xMomentum). The Photocopier fires a copy of its neighbour.
6. Leverage = Influence x Momentum.

Number handling: Influence is a whole number. Momentum is kept to one decimal place, rounded half up after every x step. Leverage is rounded down. Above 2^53 the engine switches to a deterministic mantissa-and-exponent number. Scores display in e-notation from 1e9.

### BRINK

> Join up regions on a world map to score, seat a war room of odd experts who multiply every move, and push one number past breaking point.

**Fantasy.** You run the war room of a made-up world called the Ring: four lands around one inner sea. You are not a president or a general, you have no country and nobody gets hurt. You are the person at the head of a lit plotting table at night.

Crises flare up on the map one after another. Each one has a PRESSURE number. You answer it by committing forces in shapes across the map: a line down a coast, a whole continent held, one force in every corner of the world. Forces are wooden counters, one for each region. Your STAFF sit around the table: an Admiral with a brass telescope, a Governor with a wax seal, a Deputy with a sheet of carbon paper. They multiply what each shape is worth. You don't destroy anything. You out-weigh the crisis: get LEVERAGE past PRESSURE and the crisis calms.

As the run goes on you also redraw the world. You lay cables between distant regions, crown new capitals, fortify ports, move borders and switch off regions you don't need. Every crisis you settle leaves a brass pin in the map. By the last stage the map has become your build, drawn onto the world, and no two runs leave the same map.

The tone is calm and tactile, closer to playing solitaire at a beautiful table than to a war game. The war room does the job poker did for Balatro: it is a frame anyone understands in a second. A map, pins, counters, a number and a target.

**The board.** THE RING: one fictional world of 25 regions in 5 theatres. It is drawn as a single square map, so it fits a phone held upright with no panning or zooming.

SHAPE
- Four continents form a ring around one inner sea. Each continent is a strip of 5 regions. The sea is a cross of 5 sea zones.
- At the four corners, land bridges join each continent to its neighbours.
- Each continent's Port touches its own sea zone. The Deep, in the middle, touches the other four sea zones.
- So the land is a loop of 20 regions and the sea is the short cut through the middle.

THEATRES AND SWEEP ORDER
Theatres always resolve in an order printed on the map: 1 NORTH, 2 WEST, 3 SEA, 4 EAST, 5 SOUTH. Inside each theatre, regions resolve in their printed order, 1 to 5. Nothing about the order is hidden or random.

REGIONS
Each region has:
- a WORTH (3 to 10): the Force it adds when it scores;
- a theatre colour;
- at most one trait: Capital (star), Port (anchor), Industry (gear) or Sea zone (waves).
The 8 regions at the ends of the land bridges are also FRONTIER regions.

Order, name, worth, trait:

THE BOARD CARRIES OVER
Every change goes on this same map and stays for the whole run: stamps, cables, crowns, moved borders, recruited counters, switched-off regions and pins.

**The verb.** TAP A SHAPE, THEN COMMIT.

Each round you hold 8 READY forces. They are wooden counters in a rack at the bottom of the screen. Each belongs to one region and shows its short name, its worth and its theatre colour. The same 8 also glow as pins on the map.

1. SELECT: tap up to 5 counters, either in the rack or as pins on the map. Each tap:
   - lights the region;
   - draws the shape you are making across the map in pencil;
   - updates the formation line, which names the shape and its base: "LINK 12 x 2", then "LINE 25 x 3", then "FRONT 70 x 6".
   Selected regions that are not part of the shape turn grey (they won't score). Staff who will fire on the chosen regions glow on the rail. So you see who joins in, but not the total.

2. COMMIT or SWAP:
   - COMMIT: the counters slide off the rack onto their regions and the sweep scores the play, theatre by theatre.
   - SWAP: send back up to 5 selected counters and draw replacements from your bag.

3. BETWEEN CRISES (the briefing):
   - buy staff and drag them into seats (order matters);
   - drag Orders straight onto map regions to change the map;
   - sell what you don't need.

The one free placement: AIRLIFT counters (a wild counter added by an Order) are dragged onto any empty region you choose. That is the literal "commit a force into the theatre you need".

That is the whole verb. There are no text choices, no meters, no movement rules and no timers.

**Scoring.** FORCE x NERVE = LEVERAGE.
- FORCE (cyan) is how much you committed: the formation base plus each scoring region's worth, plus staff.
- NERVE (amber) is how bold the play is: the formation base plus staff.
Each play's leverage adds to the crisis total, which has to reach the PRESSURE.

ORDER (fixed, and always shown on screen)
1. Formation base: Force and Nerve from the formation table, plus Doctrine levels.
2. The flashpoint rule applies (for example, Storm makes sea zones score nothing).
3. THE SWEEP. Theatres go in order 1 NORTH, 2 WEST, 3 SEA, 4 EAST, 5 SOUTH; a theatre with no scoring region is skipped. Inside a theatre, each scoring region goes in printed order:
   a. add its worth to Force (worth includes Fortify and +5 per pin);
   b. its stamps fire (Beacon +6 Nerve, Gild +$2);
   c. every staff with an "each X that scores" effect fires, left seat to right;
   d. if the region scores again (Double Time, Signals Officer, Convoy Master, Drill Sergeant, Night Watch, Historian), repeat a to c once for each extra time.
   After a theatre's last region, any "after each theatre" staff fire (for example, Liaison).
4. THE RAIL: staff with flat effects (+Force, +Nerve, x Nerve) fire left seat to right.
5. LEVERAGE = Force x Nerve, rounded down once at the end. Force is always a whole number. Nerve keeps its decimals along the way.
Because + and x apply in seat order, where you seat staff is a skill (Example 2). Doubles are deterministic across browsers for + and x, so daily seeds replay identically. No logs or powers are used in scoring.

PREVIEW
While you select, you see the formation, its base, which regions will score and which staff will fire. You do not see the total: the total is the reveal. An exact-total preview unlocks after the first win as an accessibility setting.

### BRINK: Draw the Line (working title; keeps the BRINK name)

> Draw one straight line across a world map. Every glowing city it touches adds up, and the right objects on your desk turn a single line into millions.

**Fantasy.** You sit at the head of the table in a war room. The game never shows your name or face, only your hands, a grease pencil and a long brass ruler. In front of you is a round glass table showing a whole fictional world from above its pole. Trouble shows up as glowing hot spots in its cities. The room gives you one way to answer: lay the ruler across the world and draw one straight line.

A line is an initiative running straight across the world: a trade corridor, an airlift, a broadcast or a summit route. Every hot spot it passes through is settled and adds to your Influence. Each crisis has a Pressure number. Beat it before your pencils run out and the room relaxes. Run out first and the crisis tips over the brink, which ends the run.

The run lasts eight Days, each with a Morning, an Afternoon and a Night crisis, and the crises get heavier. You change the world to meet them:
- You ink colours across borders so that whole blocs line up.
- You raise towns into capitals.
- You strengthen a corridor so it pays out every time.
- You line the desk in front of you with objects (a red telephone, a spirit level, tracing paper) that turn one line into a chain reaction.

Nothing on the table is ever attacked or destroyed. The game is about pulling the world into line, not blowing it up, and it stays non-graphic. By the end of a run the glass is covered in your ink: a drawing of every decision you made, which is also your share image.

**The board.** THE BOARD: a round, fictional world seen from above its pole, drawn as the glass top of a round plotting table. On a phone it is a 366 pt disc. The round shape fits portrait and landscape with the same art, and it reads as 'the whole world' at a glance.

36 REGIONS ON 5 LANDMASSES

- The North, the East, the South and the West: four outer landmasses at the compass points, 7 regions each.
- Seas: the Inner Sea, a ring between the Crown and the outer four, and four Straits between the outer landmasses.

EACH REGION HAS THREE FACTS, ALL VISIBLE WITHOUT TEXT
1. Colour (its bloc). There are four: Azure (circle glyph), Jade (diamond), Rose (square) and Violet (triangle). Each colour has 9 regions: 6 on its home landmass (North = Azure, East = Jade, South = Rose, West = Violet), 1 outpost on the next landmass clockwise, and 2 in the Crown. The region fill is a soft tint; a lit pin carries the bright colour and its glyph, so the board works for colour-blind players.

**The verb.** DRAW ONE STRAIGHT LINE ACROSS THE WORLD.

1. Touch anywhere on the board and drag. A straight line runs through the point you touched and your finger, extended from rim to rim. It ignores borders and seas, and it cannot bend unless a piece allows it. It is not a route through neighbours: two things decide it, its angle and its position.
2. Every lit pin the line touches lifts off the map with a light haptic tick and joins the line. A pin counts if the line passes within 9 pt of its centre on a 366 pt board (about 5% of the radius). Magnet snapping pulls the line through any pin within 14 pt, as long as that does not drop another pin.
3. The first 5 pins counted from the end you started at are the ones that score. Five pencil notches on the strip show this cap. Extra lit pins the line crosses are shown struck through and stay lit.
4. Direction matters. Pins score in the order the pencil reaches them, so pieces that act on the first or last pin care where you start. A ⇄ button on the strip flips the direction.
5. When you lift your finger the line stays. Two brass handles on the rim let you nudge either end. The strip names what you have made, for example 'TRIO 40 × 4'. Tap DRAW to commit.
6. The other action is Refresh. Tap up to 5 lit pins you don't want (each gets a red cross) and press REFRESH. They go dark and new pins light from the pile.
7. Assist mode, which is also how the very first crisis works: tap two pins and the line runs through both.

Mouse: click and drag; Q/E rotate the line 1°; the arrow keys shift it.
Controller: left stick sets the angle, right stick the position; A draws, X refreshes.

WHY THIS VERB
- It is one gesture everyone knows: a ruler laid on a map.
- It uses the map's real geometry: which places line up, and across which seas.
- It gives a real choice. In my simulation (see the_hand) a deal offers about 40 different lines, each picking up a different set of pins, and the best is usually 3 to 6 times better than the median. A binary card could never offer that.
- It creates a puzzle Balatro's hand does not have. You cannot pick any subset you like; a wrong-coloured pin sitting on your line is a problem to solve, by changing the angle, refreshing it away or editing the map.

**Scoring.** FORMULA: INFLUENCE = REACH × PULL. Reach is the base (ice-white box) and Pull is the multiplier (gold box). Only those two numbers are multiplied, against one target, Pressure.

ORDER (fixed, and shown in the animation)
0. Night twist filters first. Example: under Storm Front, pins on the stormed landmass are skipped.
1. Pattern: Reach and Pull start at the pattern's base for its current level.
2. Pins, in drawing order. For each pin:
 a. add its size to Reach (town +5, city +10, capital +20);
 b. apply the region's marks (Fortify +15 Reach, Spotlight +4 Pull, Gild +3 Funds);
 c. pieces that act per pin fire, desk left to right;
 d. repeats (Double Pin mark, Carbon Paper, Parish Bell, Loudhailer, or crossing the same pin again after a bounce) run a to c again for that pin.
3. Pieces that act after the line fire, desk left to right: +Reach, +Pull and ×Pull, exactly in the order they sit. Because +4 placed before ×2 beats +4 placed after it, arranging the desk is a skill.
4. Influence = floor(Reach × Pull). Pull is kept in tenths: ×1.5 steps round down to 0.1, stored as integer tenths so every browser gets identical results.
5. The line's Influence is added to the crisis total.

- Pin 1, North capital: Reach +20 = 220. Tracing Paper (as Red Pen) ×2 = 40; Red Pen ×2 = 80. Loudhailer repeat: Reach +20 = 240; Pull ×2 ×2 = 320.
- Pin 2, promoted capital: Reach 260; Pull 640, then 1,280. Repeat: Reach 280; Pull 2,560, then 5,120.
- Pin 3, Crown city: Reach +10 = 290.
- Pin 4, South capital: Reach 310; Pull 10,240, then 20,480. Repeat: Reach 330; Pull 40,960, then 81,920. (Check: 20 × 4^6 = 81,920.)
- Pin 5, South town: Reach +5 = 335.
- After the line: Brass Ruler +40, Reach 375. Typewriter ×3, Pull 245,760.
- Influence: 375 × 245,760 = 92,160,000, exactly 288× the Pressure.
Endless follow-up: swap Brass Ruler for the Legendary Hall of Mirrors, whose three bounces let the line cross all 5 pins twice. That gives 12 capital passes:
- Pull = 20 × 4^12 = 335,544,320; ×3 = 1,006,632,960.
- Reach = 200 + 12 × 20 + 2 × 10 + 2 × 5 = 470.
- Influence = 470 × 1,006,632,960 = 473,117,491,200 (about 4.7e11). That beats the endless Day 12 Morning target (about 9.4 billion) 50 times over, but not Day 13's 2.3e12.

**Why it is balatro compelling.** WHERE THE ONE-MORE-RUN PULL COMES FROM
1. Every deal is a small visible puzzle with an obvious 'almost'. You can see a 4-pin Azure line if only that Rose pin weren't in the way, and Refresh is right there. Balatro's flush draw becomes a geometric draw you can see on the map.
2. A run gets an identity fast. One Briefing and one Ink stamp and it is 'my Azure run'. The map shows that identity, so you care about this run's board in a way a deck list never does.
3. The breaking moments are visible and explosive. Per-pin ×Pull pieces compound per pin and per repeat (Example 3 multiplies Pull by 4 six times, ending at 92 million against 320,000). Bounces make the line ricochet around a glowing world while the counter goes mad. That is the clip streamers post.
4. Nerve without a meter: save for interest or spend; keep the Hourglass streak or Refresh; skip a crisis for a Favour; open a Sealed Order.
5. Short, complete units. A Day takes about 5 minutes and a run about 35, with one-tap restart, autosave after every line, a Daily Map and an 'Off the Chart' ending to chase.
6. Unlocks are a checklist (maps, Tension levels, feats) that pushes you into strategies you would otherwise skip, which is LocalThunk's stated purpose for them.

WHAT COULD MAKE IT FALL FLAT (honestly)
- Finger-hunting. Because the pattern name updates live, players may slide the line around until the best name appears, with no thought. Balatro has the same 'obvious best hand' complaint. The depth then has to come from Refresh, saving alignments, desk order, twists and pieces that reward a 'worse' line (Pocket Watch, Typewriter). If that isn't enough it will feel like a geometry worksheet.
- Geometry frustration. A blocking pin feels unfair rather than interesting.
- If the cascade's sound and motion are merely competent, the game loses its heart. Balatro's flame came late; ours needs tuning first.
- Too many plain '+Pull' pieces would flatten the middle of a run.
- The war room is presentation. An owner who imagined statecraft may find this a puzzle-builder in uniform.
- None of this is proven until people play it. LocalThunk's one-hour retention rubric should judge the greybox.

### I judged these as a designer who has shipped a Balatro-like. First, the shared problem. All five are the same Balatro skeleton with different maps. They use nearly identical numbers: 4 plays, 3 redraws, a hand of 8, a 5-piece cap, 5 desk slots, interest of 1 per 5 capped at 5, 70/25/5 rarities, 8 stages of 3 rounds at 1x/1.5x/2x, and a first target of 300. So the real differences are only three things: what the hand is, how readable the scoring sweep is, and how the map records the build. None of the five has been played by a person.

I re-ran the simulation scripts the designs cite:
- **Design 2 reproduces exactly.** The best play is a median 6.2x the median legal play, and at least 3x it in 98.7% of deals. But Link or Line is the best play in 57% of fresh deals, and the best score swings 15x between deals (p10 42, p90 630). Early deal luck is large, so swaps have to carry the decisions.
- **Design 4 reproduces.** Spread plus Pincer are the best play in 65% of racks, so plays will feel samey.
- **Design 5 reproduces.** Its best shapes are the most evenly spread and deal luck is lowest (best score p10 165, p90 320). But its choice spread is the lowest (4.3x), and the line verb removes the choice of which pins to play.
- **Design 3 cannot be reproduced.** Its map file in the scratchpad has been overwritten by Design 4's, so its numbers stand only as stated, and it admits Worldwide is the best play in 34% of deals.
- **Design 1 has no simulation at all.** Its map links are only partly listed (about 52 'touching pairs', only 10 regions sampled), so its Ring and Spread odds are unknown.

**Design 2 is best** because its hand is the most native to a map. You join lit cities into routes and loops, which every Mini Metro and Ticket to Ride player already reads. Scoring runs along the line you drew, so the cascade reads like Balatro's cards stepping forward, without the arbitrary west-to-east sweep of Designs 1, 3 and 4. The connections themselves score: each stop scores, and each land, sea or air link scores and can retrigger, which roughly doubles the hooks for pieces. Traffic is a run-long scaling that wears your network into the board, which is the strongest literal 'world map that reacts'. It also has the best evidence.

Its weaknesses are real:
- When a set of cities can be joined more than one way, nothing says which route the game picks. That matters for sea-lane builds, so the rule must be fixed (show the route and let the player flip it).
- 53 links on a 390 x 400 point map is dense.
- Its war-room fantasy is the weakest of the four map-pin designs.
- 'Brink' has a trademark problem.

**Design 3 is a close second.** It is the most complete Balatro translation: lit cities you leave unpinned can still fire (Standby), the equivalent of Balatro's held-in-hand effects, and changing the map unlocks secret patterns, as thinning the deck enables flushes. But colour starts out identical to continent, Worldwide dominates, and it has the slowest cascade.

**Design 1** has the best war-room fantasy: one night, a lamp that follows the clock, dawn as the win. Its operation types are independent of geography, which is the right two-axis mapping (like rank and suit). But it is unmeasured, and its coast-heavy map invites Fleet dominance.

**Design 4** is the clearest and cheapest to build, with a rack of counters and a printed scoring order. But its world is a schematic ring rather than a map, its naming is militaristic (Purge, Black Budget, Field Marshal), and Spread and Pincer win 65% of racks.

**Design 5** is the most original and has the best share image (every line drawn builds up as ink). But a straight line takes every lit pin in its path, so you lose Balatro's choice of which cards to play. Sliding a line until the best name appears is search rather than decision, and a 9-point touch tolerance risks frustration on phones.

Recommendation: build Design 2's hand with the grafts below. Paper-prototype it, then build a 2-week greybox with the breaker bot, and put a painted screen in front of the owner before anything else.

### Premium roguelike publisher (Steam and mobile). My lens: the first 3 seconds of the trailer, the store capsule, portrait-phone readability, the first minute, whether a wide phone/Steam/web audience would buy it, and the build risk for a small web-stack team. What I found:
1) Design 2 (sim/freq.py): the best play is 6.2x the median play in 98.7% of deals, which confirms the claim. But the best play is only a Link or a Line in 57% of deals. The median best play is 76, with p10 = 42 and p90 = 630. So early crises are small shapes with a lot of deal luck.
2) Design 4 (spread.py): confirmed at 12.8x the median play and 2.09x the next-best shape. But SPREAD and PINCER are the best play in 65% of racks, so play will feel samey.

- Designs 1, 3 and 4 map almost one-to-one onto the poker ladder: Two Links = Two Pair; Pincer and Chain & Link = Full House; Continent and Bloc = Flush; Fortress and Massed = Five of a Kind. That is real App Store 4.1 'copycat' and press 'reskin' exposure. Designs 2 and 5 escape most of it.
- 'Brink' collides with Splash Damage's 2011 game. This applies to all five designs, but only Design 2 flags it.
- None has been played by a human yet. All five correctly ask for a greybox first.
