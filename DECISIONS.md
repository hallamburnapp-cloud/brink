# DECISIONS.md

Open design decisions, chosen for fun and finishability, logged as they were made.

## Project home
- **D-001 Repository.** The brief says "this empty repo", but the session was attached to four existing repositories and creating a new one was refused (GitHub App integration: 403). BRINK is built as a fully self-contained project at `brink/` and pushed to the designated branch `claude/brink-game-build-0csh6q` of `flashpoint-2027`. It does not read, import or depend on anything outside `brink/`. Moving it to its own repository is one command (`git subtree split -P brink -b brink-main`). See BLOCKERS.md.

## Engine
- **D-010 Modifier order.** Sum additive modifiers, then multiply, then scale by act × difficulty, then round half away from zero, then clamp. Addition-before-multiplication makes acquisition order irrelevant, which makes builds legible. An additive modifier can never flip an effect's sign (it becomes 0 instead) so pieces can soften costs but never turn a cost into a benefit.
- **D-011 Scaling applies to costs only.** Act and difficulty scaling multiplies costs (negative meter deltas, positive escalation) but not benefits, otherwise late-game healing pieces would grow with the pressure they are meant to resist.
- **D-012 Hidden values are never scaled.** Trust, intel and commitment are the slow story of the run; act pressure is expressed through the visible meters and the intel shift.
- **D-013 Integer meters, fractional drifts.** Meters stay integers. A drift of −0.25 per card is applied as a −1 with probability 0.25 from the seeded RNG, which keeps state small and replay exact.
- **D-014 Odds situational terms.** Rolls tagged `adversary` gain (trust − 50)/200, `intel` rolls gain (intel − 50)/200, `alliance` rolls gain (commitment − 50)/250. Hidden values therefore decide flashpoints without ever being printed.
- **D-015 Near miss = within 5 points.** Displayed as "Missed by 3%" or "Held by 2%".
- **D-016 Warning truth decided on draw.** So the UI can never leak it, and the seed alone fixes it.
- **D-017 Arc pacing.** At most two arcs with pending follow-ups; entry cards of further arcs are weighted ×0.25 until one resolves.
- **D-018 Offers.** Three pieces, one per pool when possible, weighted by `offer_weight`, filtered by seat, unlock state and `excludes`. No currency in v1.
- **D-019 Stand-down threshold.** Escalation ≤ 35 when the Endgame flashpoint ends.
- **D-020 The moment it went wrong.** For meter endings, the card in the last ten that pushed hardest toward the fatal edge; for stand-downs, the card that pulled escalation down the most in the whole run; otherwise the last card.
- **D-021 Timeout side.** Authored per card (default right); Pre-delegation overrides it to the military option.
- **D-022 Minimal Deterrence ceiling.** Escalation is capped at 95 while held: you cannot drift into war, only be pushed by a forced flashpoint ending. Balanced by ×1.5 military losses and weaker deterrence.

## Brinkmanship scaling (the redesign)
- **D-060 Leverage order.** base adds → mult adds → mult multipliers → escalation multiplier → retriggers. Addition before multiplication keeps piece order irrelevant; retriggers multiply the whole result so they read as "score it again". The preview equals the score (computed on the pre-choice state) so there are no hidden surprises in the tally.
- **D-061 Derived base.** Authors may omit `base`; the compiler derives it from the size of the move so the 300+ cards written before the redesign score sensibly. Hand-tuned bases override.
- **D-062 Escalation curve.** As briefed: ×1 to 29, ×2 at 50, ×5 at 80, ×12 at 95, ×20 at 99, linear between. Endless adds +30% per act so scores keep climbing.
- **D-063 Accidents live above 50.** Chance 4%→45% from 50 to 99, attached to 75% of ordinary cards there, shown as a percentage before the choice; severity ×1→×2. Flashpoint and bluff cards never carry accidents (they are already the danger).
- **D-064 Antes count ordinary cards only.** Flashpoint leverage goes to the score, not the ante, so the ante is decided before the flashpoint and the bluff card can precede it.
- **D-065 Ante rewards.** 4 + act capital for meeting, +1 per extra 50% (max +4), "smashed" at 2×; missing plays a generic bluff card (six authored) or the flashpoint's own.
- **D-066 Shop economy.** Prices by rarity 3/5/8/12, starting capital 4, reroll 2 rising by 1, sell 50%, tag removal 4 once per visit, cap 6 pieces / 2 orders. A mid-act shop opens at the halfway card (acts of 8+ cards) so the first act is not pieceless.
- **D-067 Act 1 target 250** rather than 300 so a pieceless first act at moderate escalation can still meet it; later targets as briefed, to be tuned by the simulator.
- **D-068 Endless is opt-in after a win** and repeats flashpoints; the daily record is filed at the first ending, so continuing never changes a Daily result.
- **D-069 Deadman Switch is once per run** and leaves escalation at 70 (or the floor if higher), so a Madman run keeps its multipliers but not its immunity.
- **D-070 Rules that are levels merge by max, discounts by product, counts by sum** so duplicate rules from two pieces behave sensibly.
- **D-071 The engine publishes outcome flags** (`accident:*`, `ante:*`, `peak:*`, `deadman:fired`, `endless`; CONTENT.md §4.6) instead of exposing run stats to conditions. Endings and cards condition on the same vocabulary, the validator can whitelist it, and the flag list is the whole contract.
- **D-072 Accident endings outrank story endings.** When an accident took escalation to 100 (`accident:fatal`), the accident is the story, so the four accident wars sit at priority 45; story nuclear endings live at 20–40 and the generic fallback at 0.
- **D-074 Cooling is an act knob, not a card.** Escalation cools 0.5 per ordinary card above 25 (`cooling` in rules.yaml). Without it a neutral deck drifted +1.05 escalation per card and every calm build still died at 100; with it the ladder has to be climbed on purpose, which is the design, and the knob is one number per act instead of 700 card edits.
- **D-075 Flashpoint numbers are ±8..±16, not ±12..±30.** The old band made a five-card flashpoint unsurvivable from a healthy state on either path; the engine's act scaling (×1.1..×1.5) still makes late flashpoints bite.
- **D-076 A missed ante is not a death spiral.** Every act after the first pays a 2 PC stipend and a near miss (≥ 50% of the target) pays 2 PC consolation, so a build that stumbles in Week Two can still grow; the bluff card and the lost reward remain the punishment.
- **D-077 The heuristic bot is calibrated, not fitted.** Its escalation pricing, wall (80 unless it holds a brink piece), odds expectation and forced-ending avoidance were changed because it made trades no player would (signing a resignation letter because it had no printed cost); every such change is logged in BALANCE.md and none reads the content it plays against.
- **D-073 Endless never ends by run_end.** Every endless act reopens the shop; the run ends only by a meter hitting 0 or 100, and two endless-only removed endings (public and military at 100) say what happens to a leader who will not stop.

## The night (the redesign, 2026-09-29)
- **D-078 Two rulesets, one engine.** `daily` and `night` run the simple ruleset (no accidents, antes, shop, pieces, orders, leverage display or endless); `endless` and `challenge` keep the Expert game untouched. One `RunState`, one replay path, one content set; `rulesetFor(mode)` is the only switch. Rebuilding a second engine for a wide audience would have thrown away 315 tests and a balanced deck for no player-visible gain.
- **D-079 Nothing on the run screen is a number except the clock.** Five glyph dials with a fill and a colour, preview dots for the tilted choice, odds as a word (Likely ≥ 0.7, Even ≥ 0.5, Risky), percentages only on the crisis cards, and a roll overlay without a scale. The Expert HUD is the same components with the numbers on.
- **D-080 Seven minutes of the clock per card; 21 cards, then the crisis.** 3:00am to 6:00am at seven minutes a card is 25 cards; five acts of 5/5/5/5/1 spend 21 on ordinary cards and leave the crisis's own cards to run the clock to 5:59, and dawn is 6:00 exactly. The day counter still ticks underneath (0.1 per card) so the Expert record and the moment logic keep working.
- **D-081 The crisis only at the end.** One flashpoint per night, weighted by the arcs the night touched, so the shareable strip ends in a spike rather than five. The four earlier acts have `flashpoint: false`; the first draft with a flashpoint per act made the night twelve minutes long and killed the random bot before 4:00 every time.
- **D-082 Dawn is named by the dials.** Two run-end endings above the flashpoint outcomes: A Quiet Dawn (every dial in the middle and danger below 40) and A Red Dawn (danger 75+), so the night's shape, not only its last card, picks the dawn; the flashpoint-specific dawns fill the middle. Added when the calm bot saw The Empty Chair at Vellmar in 36.5% of its nights (N4).
- **D-083 The danger dial moves the crisis odds.** `resolveOdds` gains `(50 − danger) / 250` on every roll of the night's flashpoint (±0.2 between an empty and a full dial), in the night only. Measured before the link: the calm bot arrived at the crisis with danger at a median of 67 and the crisis killed 44% of the nights that reached it regardless of how they had gone; after it, arriving at 30–49 gives 72% dawn and 85+ gives 20%, so the visible dial is the lever a player can learn without a tutorial. Hidden values still shift odds as in Expert.
- **D-084 The night's drifts and scales.** Office costs ×1.6 → ×2.6 across the five acts (the deck was authored for a 70-card run; the night is 21), escalation scale ×1.0 throughout, cooling 0.3 per ordinary card, no recovery. The first night draft removed both drifts and ramped escalation to ×1.3, which sent every careful night into the crisis at danger 80+; cooling 0.3 with a flat escalation scale puts the median arrival at 59 and lets a player who protects the danger dial arrive calm.
- **D-085 The night's targets replace T1–T5 for the simple ruleset.** N1 calm bot dawn 45–65%, N2 random bot dawn < 10%, N3 average night 2–4 minutes at 7 s a card, N4 no ending in more than 35% of calm nights, N5 the crisis ends 25–45% of the calm nights that reach it. The first draft of REDESIGN.md said 30–45% for N1 and was revised before any tuning to it: a daily people return to is one a careful player wins about half the time, and the bot reads the dials better than a person will. N5 was first written as "share of falls in the crisis", which for a bot that never falls on a dial is always ~95% and measures the bot, not the design; it now measures the crisis's own kill rate.
- **D-086 The voice contract is a validator rule, not a style guide.** Card text ≤ 2 sentences and ≤ 180 characters, choices ≤ 34, outcomes ≤ 160, endings ≤ 2 paragraphs and ≤ 420, compendium ≤ 120, a banned-jargon list, no ellipses in choices; `BRINK_VOICE=strict` turns the warnings into errors and becomes the CI default once the rewrite lands. Speakers are shown role first ("Your General"), name second.
- **D-087 The record counts nights and dawns.** `Stats.nights` and `Stats.dawns` are kept apart from Expert's runs and days; the night-by-night history shows Dawn or the time of the fall. Best-days and the piece statistics stay Expert-only.
- **D-088 Unlock copy says "Night after night", never "Endless".** Endless is an Expert mode, not the product; the one purchase is described by what it gives (any night, any seat, seeds, Expert) on the paywall, the unlock page, Settings and Privacy.

## The hotel (the third redesign, 2026-10-07)
- **D-089 The setting changed; the brand, the engine and the loop did not.** The owner twice said the night did not work for a wide audience. A panel (four critiques, six concepts, three judges, one synthesis; `docs/step-back/`) found the ceiling was the theme, not the mechanics: a nuclear-crisis premise reads as a strategy game to the people who would never download one, every ending is a flavour of catastrophe, and five dials of state read as a dashboard. All three judges chose a hotel variant. BRINK is now the night desk at The Brink Hotel: 3am, the phone, the clock, two sentences a card, the same night for everyone. The crisis game is preserved whole at tag `v0.3.0-night` and as the `content-crisis/` pack; the Expert ruleset stays in the code and on no screen.
- **D-090 Four bars, one-sided.** GUESTS, STAFF, MONEY, THE BUILDING; a bar fails only at 0, and 100 is clamped and seats a comedy card (`rules.night.one_sided`, `full_cards`). Escalation is not used (`use_escalation: false`), so there is no fifth dial and no dice at the climax. Horizontal bars filling from the left are the one progress grammar everyone already reads; "empty ends the night" needs no tutorial.
- **D-091 Bookings: the night has a situation.** A Booking pins an opener at 3:00, three beats in slot windows and a head in the five o'clock hour into the queue at run start (`seatBooking`), and the rest is drawn from the pool. Twelve at launch. The Booking is the share object's noun ("BRINK #212 · THE SWAN") and what Home can promise for tomorrow (`bookingForSeed` replicates the first draw).
- **D-092 A review, not an ending.** The night ends in a review from a guest: stars, a byline, a quote, two paragraphs. Review endings choose their band with `conditions.stars`; a bar on the floor is a one-star review stamped WALKOUT / STAFF WALKED / CLOSED BY THE OWNER / CLOSED BY THE FIRE BRIGADE with the time. Stars come from the mean of the four bars (`starsFor`), never shown as a number.
- **D-093 The reply line.** Every choice carries `reply`, the world's one-line answer, shown for 1.1 s under the bars with the speaker's role; odds cards show the outcome text that was authored for every gamble and never displayed. The critiques' first complaint about the night was that consequences were invisible; this is the cheapest fix that is also the funniest part of the card.
- **D-094 The night wears on (`rules.night.drift`).** Every answered card costs each bar 1.5, scaled like the cards. Without it the careful bot reached 6:00 on 100% of nights and the random bot on 90% (the cards alone are a fair trade); with drift 1.5 and the acts' effect scale at 1.4 the careful bot reaches 6:00 on about 80% and the random bot on 12–17%, which is the game: attention matters, a bad night is the minority outcome for an attentive player. Chosen from a 34-cell sweep (`tools/sweep-hotel.ts`, BALANCE.md "The hotel").
- **D-095 The star bands and the bar colours read from where a careful night ends.** A careful night ends with the four bars' mean near 30 of 100 (p10 20, p90 39 on the finished pack), so five stars is a mean of 41 and up, four 34, three 27, two 21; brass is a bar at 41 or above, amber at 21 or below, red at 12 or below (`BAR_BANDS`, `STAR_BANDS`). The first bands (78/62/46/30) were written for bars that stayed high and gave the careful bot three stars 73% of the time; the bands were fitted once on the half-written pack (38/31/24/18) and moved up three when the finished pack's comedy pass and pool landed. The bars visibly drain through the night, which is the drama; the stars judge the ending against the night, not against full.
- **D-096 The targets were revised with reasons, not fitted silently.** B1 was written as 60–75% careful dawn before tuning and raised to 75–90% because a random tapper must fall most nights (B2 15–25%) and the cards give a careful/random gap of about 65 points, so 60–75% careful would need a random rate near zero. B3 was written over all nights and is now measured among nights that reach 6:00 (5/25/40/20/10), because the falls are B1's business and at 78% dawn one star could never be below 22%.
- **D-097 The Brass Plate without a typed name.** HOTEL.md's purchase put the player's name on the plate and on every shared review. The brief forbids user-generated content, and a free-text field whose output appears on a BRINK-branded image is one. The plate ships as: choose the night you work, the Guest Book in full, every past Tonight again from the Archive, a brass badge on shared reviews. The name is an owner decision and is listed in the report, not built.
- **D-098 The first night is a practice night.** A player's first open shows one button, CLOCK IN, and gets The Swan at the first-night scale (0.85) with a one-line rule under the bars; Tonight appears after it. A tutorial that is also tonight's one attempt teaches by punishing; a practice night costs two minutes and makes the second screen the social one.
- **D-100 Eighteen cards of ten minutes, exactly.** The clock is the only number on screen, so it has to be honest: the night ends at `night.cards` whatever is still queued (a comedy card seated on the last card never shows) and every hotel card takes ten minutes (the validator warns otherwise). The first draft gave each Booking's head twenty minutes for weight; the harness then showed a nineteen-card night ending at 6:10. The head's weight is in its writing and its place in the five o'clock hour, not in the clock.
- **D-099 Home from anywhere, and the night waits.** `← HOME` at 44px on every screen, every screen change a history entry so the phone's back button walks back through the app and keeps the night; Tonight cannot be abandoned, only left and picked back up at the same clock. Shipped before the redesign because it held under any direction.

## The map game (the fourth direction, 2026-10-13)
- **D-101 Ask before redesigning, and let the owner choose by eye.** After the hotel the owner said "this game is crap and nothing like what I want it to be. Think again before you redesign." Three redesigns had each been chosen by us and each kept the text-card swipe core. Four direct questions found what was missing: the game in the owner's head is Plague Inc style (a world map that reacts as you make decisions, a longer strategy game with a clear win or loss), the setting is open, it must sell on phones, Steam and the web, and the first build failed on all four counts (couldn't tell what to do, choices felt pointless, looked unfinished, wrong for the market). A design study (`docs/map-concepts/`) researched Plague Inc. and the market, generated six map-strategy concepts, scored them against the owner's answers rather than a critic's taste, and shortlisted three: NEVERSPRING (be the winter covering a round world before its people light the Furnace), REWILD (be the wild turning a grey world green), OFF SWITCH (stop a polite AI taking over a night-time world). Each was painted as finished phone and PC screens. The owner picks; no game code changes until they do. The card-swipe game stays in the repository as it is.
- **D-102 Balatro on a world map, in a war room.** The owner turned down the three map concepts ("none of these are doing it for me; fundamentally, we are nowhere near what the original aims were") and named three anchors: a world map that reacts, Balatro-style building, and a world map in a war room as the main screen. Every earlier version lacked a hand of things to combine, so choices could not stack (21 vs 23). The new design makes the map the hand: each crisis lights eight of thirty regions, you pin up to five, and the shape the pins make (link, chain, front, surround, network, bloc) is the pattern; LEVERAGE = INFLUENCE × MOMENTUM against a target; five staff seats fire left to right; Orders and Playbooks change the map for good (brass pins, cables, defections). The design, the full prototype spec and a Python reference implementation of the rules (with worked examples that the TypeScript port must reproduce) are in `docs/war/`. Next: a short playable slice for the owner to try on a phone, and the owner's yes before the full game.
- **D-103 The slice is judged by play, not by stills.** Four critics (a new player, an art director, a game-feel reviewer, QA) played the built slice on a phone-sized screen with real taps, two fixers and an integrator worked through what they found, and three of the critics played it again before a final fix. What changed from the spec on their advice, on purpose: first-night lines sit on a linen strip over the map and never cover the goal, which the Big Board always shows with the sweeps left; the flashpoint ring hugs its region with a depleting arc instead of a 72 pt circle; a held beat before the slam; the slam's loudness follows the outcome (a clear is always loud) rather than log10 of the product; the Big Board rolls like an odometer and never shows more than the real total; scored regions stay lit through a clear; the sweep's sum stays on the readout with a LAST SWEEP receipt; a double tap cannot skip the first payoff; the phone's back button opens the menu. The rules and numbers did not change.
- **D-104 Early difficulty is the owner's call.** With the first Briefing's fixed staff (Duty Officer +4 Momentum, Harbourmaster), sweeping single regions clears the 22:00 Standoff almost always and the Gridlock Summit more than half the time, so the first night does not yet force bigger shapes. The simulator gates pass and the targets are the spec's; whether to raise the first night's Standoff and Summit (for example to 550 and 900) waits for the owner to play it.
- **D-105 Win the World.** After playing the slice the owner said: "it's interesting, but it doesn't make any sense at all. Need to simplify this, have an explanation at the start... there is no end goal." They chose the goal "win the world" (Plague Inc style: settled crises turn regions your colour for good, war spreads red on failure, win when the whole map is yours, lose if war takes too much) and a game of about 15 minutes. Three designers proposed simplified games with simulations, three judges scored them against the owner's words, and the winner was rebuilt from the slice's code: turn all 30 regions gold before war holds 10; each turn a crisis flares beside your gold with a target; pin up to five lit regions that touch (the crisis doubles); score = their numbers added up × how many you pinned, then staff left to right; beat the target and the crisis turns gold (beat it by more, win more); miss and war takes it; every third turn war spreads by itself; hire staff every second turn. Cut: patterns and levels, Influence and Momentum, sweeps, hours and twists, Orders, Playbooks, folders and ten staff. A three-screen explanation and a coached first game teach it; GOLD n/30 and WAR n/10 are always on screen; the SEND label says what will happen, so nobody has to calculate. Spec, reference and decision record: docs/war/v2/.
- **D-106 Pin it to claim it.** Critics found that extra regions from a big win went to the regions nearest the crisis, not the ones the player pinned, so pinning a region war was about to take could still lose it. Now the crisis is claimed first, then the pinned regions that are not gold, then the nearest others. All seventeen simulator gates still pass unretuned.
- **D-107 Losses come from choices.** Critics found games lost through turns where no send on the table could win, and every CEASEFIRE ending on such a deal. Now: the shop warns a turn early when the line-up falls behind the next target; a deal no send can win is announced the moment it is dealt ('NO SEND CAN WIN · SWAP', or '· SEND TO PASS' when SWAP cannot help); a deal that only a different seat order can win says 'REORDER STAFF TO WIN' and the seats to move beckon (the check covers every distinct seating); the final push allows three misses before a CEASEFIRE, shown as lamps, and its crises are drawn from the red regions a fresh deal can win when there are any. The deal rule and the numbers are unchanged; all seventeen simulator gates pass (the first-game p90 sits exactly at its 18-minute limit, the lever list is in SPEC section 17).
- **D-108 Thirty Days.** The owner played Win the World with their partner: "Too many visuals, my partner has no clue what is going on. You've not understood the brief. Create something new along the lines I've told you. Ask for confirmation before doing if you're unsure." Asked four questions, they confirmed: the story is the original brief's (you lead a great power as the world slides towards war; stop it); each turn is mostly one message with two choices, sometimes a tap on the map; the Balatro-style scoring stays in the background; you win early by calming the world to zero or by surviving to day 30, and lose if danger fills. The new game, specified in `docs/thirty-days/SPEC.md`: thirty days, one adviser card a day, a world map whose regions run calm, tense, crisis, war and whose heat is the one DANGER gauge; twelve advisers and policies picked every five days that make calming choices reach further (the building, shown only as the map cooling in a wave and a hint line in words); four things on screen and nothing else. The war-room and hotel code stay dormant.
- **D-109 The night has words.** The over-the-shoulder critic's one blocker on the first Thirty Days build: DANGER climbed after almost every "Danger down" tap with nothing to say why, so the buttons looked like they lied. A day now plays in three beats: what the button did, one OVERNIGHT line in plain words with its own DANGER +N, then the next card. Tomorrow's card is dealt at night so its news is part of the night; nothing floats unexplained. Every button's first words are the truth about DANGER (down / down here / up / unchanged / down if it works (N%)); when the authored line would be untrue today the engine composes the line, and a whole-game sweep in the tests fails the build on any button that lies.
- **D-110 Red is the threat.** The feel critics found the night's random amber flares erased the day's work and red almost never appeared. Pressure moved from flares to red that spreads: from day 6 a crisis left standing a night may turn red, two crises side by side tip each other, flares fade through the month, and a region calmed today never flares that night. Red is now seen in about three sensible games in four, first on day 13 at the median; the WAR ending names where the fighting began, and that region is red on the final map.
- **D-111 Endings are earned.** STAND-DOWN from day 10 and only once the gauge has read 30 (the spec's "after day 5" gave a day-6 win over a map that was never in danger); HELD graded cleanly, held or barely. Balance gate G8 reworded from "the gauge above 60" to "red on the map before the end, or the gauge above 60", because the gauge-only reading was met only through a late amber wash that read as noise over the shoulder; the owner may ask for the gauge alone back. A ninth gate: a hawk who builds on force holds in 40 to 65%, after the five force and intelligence pieces were rewritten (a force build had lost 92% of games).
- **D-112 Map days from the live map.** Authored map cards named fixed places that were often meaningless on the day. A map card now names a set (sea, home, abroad, allies, posts, any) and the engine picks the hottest places in it at deal time, labels each DANGER first, and deals the card only when at least two places would lower DANGER.
- **D-113 Treaty Against War (the sixth design).** The owner on Thirty Days: "Honestly, I don't think you're getting it. I think we need a wholesale redesign. It looks crap, doesn't make sense, and certainly doesn't do what we want it to do." Asked what it is closest to in the hand: "Like Plague Inc: interactive, got a deep goal to it, addictive, a map." Then, in their words: the purpose is grip; the map is the board; real time with pause and fast-forward; the race is treaty against war; all four of Plague Inc's grips (watching it spread, the upgrade tree, the headlines, the race); Plague Inc's layout with war-room dressing. Every build so far was turn-based cards; this is a real-time map game with a policy tree and a news ticker. Design and mockup in `docs/race/`; nothing is built until the owner approves the page.
- **D-114 Red Lines: the other side thinks.** The owner's review of D-113's draft (kept in `docs/redlines/REVIEW.md`): "a thin, defensive version of a game that already exists", Rebel Inc minus the depth, tapping bubbles as the main verb, treaty maths that let you win without the Federation, a tree not a draft, content gone by run two, no variance, and the owner's own edge (adversaries who respond to signals, credibility, commitment traps, alliance obligations, misperception) left out. The second draft makes the Federation an agent with hidden dials, declared red lines (some bluffs) and an escalation ladder on screen; makes promises the main verb, deterring only when the player is believed; drafts the cabinet; and puts the fog in the Intel Director's ranges. Three independent critics then read it before the owner (`docs/redlines/CRITICS.md`) and found that no region ever stopped fighting, that a promise never deterred, that the four behaviours were a strategy guide intelligence read out on day ten, that the draft cadence named two different games, and that the jargon was the document's; v5.1 of `docs/redlines/SPEC.md` answers every one. Rule kept from here on: a design is read by critics before the owner sees it, the simulator passes its gates before a tester sits down, and nothing is built until the owner approves the page.
- **D-115 The small game, and a look with art direction.** The owner's review of v5.1 (`docs/redlines/REVIEW-2.md`): the core idea is right and worth owning, but the design "swung from too thin to too dense", every critique was answered with a rule the player cannot see, the real-time clock is dead weight, the phone's conditional annotations hand the player the strategy guide, credibility from the record alone bakes in one side of a live debate (Press, Mercer), the maths does not close, and the look "is a dashboard, not a game". Decisions: cut to the small game (`docs/redlines/SPEC-SMALL.md`): one agent with two hidden dials, promises that are solid where you can hold them and dashed where you cannot, one phone with advisers in character and no annotations, two bars with five movers, a ladder word, eight weeks at one turn a week (recommended; real time stays an option for the owner), the fog lifted in a debrief at the end. The cabinet, the Coalition, warnings, leaders and seeds are out of the first build. Three visual directions are mocked on the same day-18 moment for the owner to pick from (analogue situation room, vector minimalism, Reigns-style cards). Simulated critics are a cheap check, not validation; cold testers are the bar.
- **D-116 The owner's three answers.** Shown the three looks on the same day-18 moment and the small-game cut, the owner chose: the situation room throughout (the paper map under a lamp, grease-pencil promises, red pushpins, the rubber-stamped ladder word, the teleprinter tape, the handset and typed slips for the call); turn-based by week; and a deep strategy game for a niche. The first build is still the slice in `docs/redlines/SPEC-SMALL.md`, in that look, in weekly turns; the depth the niche wants (the cabinet draft, the Coalition, private warnings, leaders, the daily seed, from `SPEC.md` v5.1) is planned as the second and third builds, and the first build's engine leaves room for them (a cabinet hook, a third bloc with a lean, a line type for warnings) without building them.

## Content
- **D-030 Hidden values in prose.** `describeHidden()` gives four bands per value; card text and advisor language carry them. Only Signals Intercept reveals numbers.
- **D-031 Fictional names.** Powers: Republic (Arden), Federation (Kaskad), Coalition (Qorum). Minor states: Vestria, Sorrel Straits, Isle of Caldor, Amberline, Northern Compact, Assembly of Nations. The validator rejects real-world names.
- **D-032 One speaker for both intel directors.** Director Lyle speaks on warnings whichever piece you hold; the piece changes what he brings you, not his face.

## Tech
- **D-040 Preact over React** for the initial-JS budget. **Tailwind v4** via the Vite plugin. **Zod** only at build/validate time; runtime content is compiled JSON in a virtual module.
- **D-041 Content hot reload** through a virtual module with HMR accept, so a run in progress keeps its state while the deck updates. Content changes route through `handleHotUpdate` so Vite never falls back to a full reload mid-run.
- **D-042 Content chunk.** Compiled content is a separate, dynamically imported chunk (~169 KB gzipped at 450 cards and 92 endings) loaded at boot; the size check counts only the initial JS (49 KB gzipped of a 250 KB budget) and reports the content chunk separately.
- **D-043 No webfonts.** System serif and monospace stacks; zero third-party requests, instant offline.

## Product
- **D-050 Daily seat rotation** is independent of unlocks: the Daily is free and every seat appears in rotation, which is also the cheapest way to let players taste locked seats.
- **D-051 itch.io build ships everything unlocked** (`VITE_ALL_UNLOCKED=1`) as the brief asks; unlock *progress* is still tracked, so the service record fills in. A future switch to "paid but with the ladder" is one env var (`VITE_PAYWALL=0 VITE_ALL_UNLOCKED=0`).
- **D-052 Cohort analytics without identifiers.** `days_since_first_run` (an integer computed on the device) rides on `run_start` and `daily_played` so day-7 return can be read as a cohort proportion. Still no cookies, no ids.
- **D-053 Locked seat hints are shown in the seat picker** rather than hidden: the unlock condition is the marketing.
